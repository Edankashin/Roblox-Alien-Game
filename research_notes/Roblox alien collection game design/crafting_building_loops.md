## Key Question 1 — Currency vs crafting vs hybrid: which do top Roblox games use, and what are the design/retention tradeoffs for a young Roblox audience?

### Takeaway
The biggest 2025–2026 Roblox hits (Grow a Garden, Steal a Brainrot, classic/modern tycoons, Pet Simulator 99, Bloxburg, Adopt Me) are overwhelmingly single-soft-currency "earn → buy → earn faster" loops with idle timers, while the crafting-first survival games that succeeded (99 Nights in the Forest, Dead Rails, Build a Boat for Treasure) are actually hybrids: run-scoped material gathering/crafting inside a session, plus a persistent meta-currency between sessions. Pure Minecraft-style crafting progression on Roblox (Islands, Booga Booga) has a harder retention history and tends to be rescued by social/PvP or by a hard reset ("wipe") that costs players.

### Cited Findings

**Audience context (why this matters)**
- In Q2 2025 Roblox reported ~39.7M daily users aged 13 and under versus ~74.4M over 13 (roughly 36% under-13 of ~114M DAU) — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1192573/daily-active-users-global-roblox/); Roblox's own Q2 2025 shareholder letter is the primary source — [Roblox Q2 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509825000261/q225shletterex992.htm) (could not be fetched; figure taken from the Statista summary).
- Secondary stat roundups state that mobile accounts for ~72% of Roblox DAUs and >52% of platform revenue — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/roblox-users); [DemandSage](https://www.demandsage.com/how-many-people-play-roblox/). (Secondary sources; not verified against the filing.)

**Currency-based loops dominate the top of the charts**
- Grow a Garden (The Garden Game, launched March 2025) is a "simulation tycoon" in which players buy seeds, plant on farm plots, and harvest crops for profit; its core loop is explicitly passive: gardens keep growing while players are offline — [GEEIQ](https://geeiq.com/resources/blog/grow-a-garden); [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/gaming-news/steal-a-brainrot-is-robloxs-latest-viral-hit-grow-a-garden-3888650).
- Naavik describes Grow a Garden as feeling "lifted from a 2010s Facebook game — from its gentle first-time user experience to its idle-inspired timers and resource collection," evoking FarmVille, Hay Day and Idle Farming Empire; it recorded 21.3M CCU in June 2025 (vs. Steam's all-time single-game record of 3.2M) and became top-grossing on Roblox after passing 12B visits within months — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/predicting-the-next-big-hits-on-roblox/).
- Grow a Garden had ~34.9B lifetime visits as of the tracker snapshot; the tracker also reports a "retention ratio of 0.0057" and interprets it as players engaging in shorter sessions / periodic visits — [Rowatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/7436755782/grow-a-garden). (The metric's definition is unclear; treat as weak evidence.)
- Steal a Brainrot (SpyderSammy, launched May 2025) is a pure cash loop: each brainrot has a fixed $/second income, "your total income is the sum of the $/s of all Brainrots in your collection," and brainrots are bought from a conveyor/marketplace or stolen from other players' bases — [games.gg tier list](https://games.gg/steal-a-brainrot/guides/steal-a-brainrot-complete-brainrot-tier-list/); [Wikipedia: Steal a Brainrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot); [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/gaming-news/steal-a-brainrot-is-robloxs-latest-viral-hit-grow-a-garden-3888650).
- Steal a Brainrot passed 17B visits and broke 15M CCU (NME, 2025), and per Wikipedia is the only Roblox game to exceed 25M CCU (Grow a Garden was the first past 20M); the two games' creators staged an "admin war" on Aug 23, 2025 that broke both CCU records — [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/gaming-news/steal-a-brainrot-is-robloxs-latest-viral-hit-grow-a-garden-3888650); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot).
- Classic Roblox tycoons: a DevForum developer reports success with "about 200 buttons, with 50 of which unlocked within the first 10–20 minutes," stressing a hook before the grind; the conveyor-factory tycoon loop is "claim a plot, drop money-collectors on conveyor belts, upgrade, reset for rebirth bonuses" with "consistent dopamine hits" — [DevForum: Is this a good tycoon progress rate?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-this-a-good-tycoon-progress-rate/2225354); [DevForum: Tycoon Game Feedback](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/tycoon-game-feedback/2838875).
- Lumber Tycoon 2 is a gather-and-sell currency loop: 21 wood types; logs sell for less than sawmilled planks; the starter temperate forest (Oak, Cherry, Birch, Basic Hatchet) gives "steady but modest income"; "Phase 1 (0–50k)" is grinding starter wood to afford the Fair Sawmill, then better axes unlock larger/more valuable species; Frost Wood tops the value table at ~106 M/u — [Lumber Tycoon 2 Fandom: Wood](https://lumber-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Wood); [lumbertycoon2.xyz progression guide](https://www.lumbertycoon2.xyz/en/guide/lumber-tycoon-2-progression-guide); [lumbertycoon-2.wiki tier list](https://lumbertycoon-2.wiki/woods/tree-tier-list/).
- Pet Simulator 99 (BIG Games) is currency + collection: pets break "breakables" for coins/diamonds; 52 enchants and 13 charms exist, 9 of them multi-tier (I–X) — [bloxguidesgg Enchants](https://bloxguidesgg.com/games/pet-simulator-99/enchants); [Pet Simulator Fandom: Enchants (PS99)](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Enchants_(Pet_Simulator_99)).

**Hybrids: materials inside the run, currency between runs**
- Dead Rails (RCM Games): players "buy coal from the general store and use it to fuel the train" and travel 80 km to Mexico; Bonds are a lobby currency "used to purchase Starting Items, Classes, and Trains" and to revive; collecting every Bond at every point of interest nets ~100–130 Bonds per run — [Roblox Fandom: Dead Rails](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/RCM_Games/Dead_Rails); [Dead Rails Fandom: Bonds](https://deadrails.fandom.com/wiki/Bonds); [TheGamer bonds guide](https://www.thegamer.com/roblox-dead-rails-bonds-farming-guide/).
- 99 Nights in the Forest: "Crafting is your gateway to survival… transform raw materials into tools, structures, and equipment"; progression is "tied directly to your Crafting Bench," with each bench upgrade unlocking a new recipe tier — [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/99-nights-in-the-forest-crafting); [99nightsintheforest.com wiki](https://99nightsintheforest.com/wiki). One guide says the bench has "four distinct levels" — [Gamezebo](https://www.gamezebo.com/walkthroughs/99-nights-in-the-forest-crafting/); another guide's title is "Definitive Crafting Guide (Bench Level 5)" — [Pro Game Guides](https://progameguides.com/roblox/99-nights-in-the-forest-crafting-guide/). (Conflict: 4 vs 5 bench tiers; likely changed in an update — verify.)
- Build a Boat for Treasure: blocks are the "material"; "Gold is the only currency that matters," earned ~8 Gold each time your boat enters a new stage, via chest payouts at the end of a successful run, and from one-time quest bonuses; block tiers run Wood/Stone → Metal/Marble/Glass → Obsidian/Adamantite/Mosaic; the Gold Block (added April 2018) is the strongest material and is awarded one per Treasure reached — [BABFT Fandom: Gold Block](https://build-a-boat-for-treasure.fandom.com/wiki/Gold_Block); [BABFT Fandom: Treasure](https://build-a-boat-for-treasure.fandom.com/wiki/Treasure); [BABFT complete guide](https://build-a-boat-for-treasure-wiki.wiki/en/guides/build-a-boat-for-treasure-complete-guide/).
- Islands (Easy.gg) is the closest Roblox analogue to Minecraft crafting: gather wood → craft Tier 1 workbench → campfire → mine iron and coal → small smelters → steel automation (steel mill costs 100 iron + 50 gold) → industrial smelters → Tier 3+ equipment — [Skyblockipedia Ultimate Guide](https://skyblockipedia.fandom.com/wiki/Ultimate_Guide_to_Islands_-_Roblox); [Roblox Islands Fandom progression post](https://robloxislands.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000096956); [NamuWiki: Islands](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Islands(Roblox)).
- Islands' player count: 50–60k concurrent at its mid-late 2020 peak; after a 2021 wipe "many players left," settling at 20–25k through 2021, then declining to ~8k before discontinuation — [Roblox Islands Fandom "Peak" thread](https://robloxislands.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000142497). (Community post; directional only.)
- Booga Booga (Soybeen) is "the closest thing Roblox has to a traditional open-world survival game," with a deep crafting tree (stone → iron → gold → crystal tools) and tribal PvP; a "Reborn" revival is described as the most popular open-world survival game on Roblox with "over 200,000 active players" — [Roblox Fandom: Booga Booga](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Soybeen/BOOGA_BOOGA); [creation.dev best survival games](https://www.creation.dev/blog/best-roblox-survival-games). (The 200k figure is from a listicle; unverified.)

**Monetization tradeoff tied to loop type**
- DevForum advice: gamepasses that directly inflate income ("especially 2x cash") "can easily drive away players looking for a fun game to sink time into"; a premium currency for exclusive items that is also earnable free (e.g., crates falling from the sky) is preferred — [DevForum: Monetization Ideas for my Tycoon Game](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/monetization-ideas-for-my-tycoon-game/3048249).
- Dead Rails is praised for "co-op survival on a train, layers in strong social mechanics, and monetizes in a way that feels fair" — [MaxPower Gaming](https://www.maxpowergaming.co/post/how-dead-rails-became-hottest-roblox-game).
- Welcome to Bloxburg: modeled lifetime revenue ~$80.3M from 10.1B visits (a formula estimate, not reported revenue), monetized via gamepasses (Multiple Floors, Large Plot), Premium, and recurring house bills — [Profitable.app](https://profitable.app/roblox/games/welcome-to-bloxburg); [Bloxburg Fandom: House](https://welcome-to-bloxburg.fandom.com/wiki/House).

### Inferences
- For an under-16, mobile-first audience, the evidence favors a *single legible number going up* (Sheckles, $/s, Gold) as the spine, with crafting present but shallow and heavily guided (99 Nights' community "priority route", Islands' linear workbench tiers). The best-performing crafting games are the ones where crafting happens *inside a bounded run* and persistent progress is a currency.
- A hybrid that fits the alien-ship brief: creatures generate a soft currency *and* planet-specific materials; ship parts cost both (currency for the generic frame, a few planet-unique materials for the "key" component). This mirrors Dead Rails (in-run cash for shop items + Bonds meta), Astroneer (generic bytes/resources + one planet-unique resource per gateway; see KQ3/KQ7), and Build a Boat (Gold + Gold Blocks only from the treasure).
- Islands' arc is the cautionary tale for pure crafting on Roblox: deep tech trees retained a dedicated core but a wipe removed the sunk-cost that was holding casual players.

### Gaps
- Could not read Roblox's Q2 2025 shareholder letter directly; under-13 and mobile shares are from secondary roundups.
- No primary analyst breakdown (Naavik/Deconstructor of Fun) of Steal a Brainrot's or 99 Nights' loops was fetchable; Deconstructor of Fun coverage of these titles was not found in search results at all.
- Did not find a quantitative comparison of retention (D1/D7) between crafting-first and currency-first Roblox games; only anecdotal player-count histories.
- Release dates for Dead Rails and 99 Nights in the Forest were not captured in any snippet.

## Key Question 2 — How do games make worker/helper creatures feel valuable in a building loop, and what multiplier ranges feel good?

### Takeaway
Helpers feel impactful when they (a) replace a chore the player has already done manually (Restaurant Tycoon 2 chefs train by watching you cook; Palworld pals take over gathering/transport), (b) have *specialized* rather than generic competence (Palworld work suitability per job, Spiritfarer perks per spirit, Grow a Garden one-ability-per-pet), and (c) stack visibly (Grow a Garden duplicates stack; Palworld levels 1→10). Observed multiplier magnitudes are modest per unit (≈1.5x growth for a single good pet; one Palworld pal at level 8 "outproduces" five at level 2) with big swings reserved for rarity/luck (Pet Sim 99's Huge Hunter is +6250% huge-chance for 3,250 Robux).

### Cited Findings

**Palworld (PC/console, Pocketpair) — the clearest "creatures as workers" model**
- Work Suitability "determines which jobs a Pal can perform and how efficiently it completes them"; Palworld 1.0 raised the cap from five levels to Level 10 — [Nodecraft](https://nodecraft.com/support/games/palworld/general/palworld-work-suitability-level-10-explained); [palmods.gg](https://www.palmods.gg/guides/whats-new/work-suitability).
- The 12 job types: Kindling, Watering, Planting, Generating Electricity, Handiwork, Gathering, Lumbering, Mining, Medicine Production, Cooling, Transporting, Farming — [Palworld wiki.gg: Working](https://palworld.wiki.gg/wiki/Working).
- Natural (uncondensed) suitability peaks at level 8 for most jobs, 7 for Transporting, 4 for Farming; Level 10 generally requires combining a naturally talented Pal with the Pal Essence Condenser (+1 suitability per use, 4 uses; at 4 stars all suitabilities +1), Applied Technique books, passive skills, or base-wide Partner Skill bonuses — [guildorder.com](https://guildorder.com/games/palworld/guides/work-suitability-base-labor); [hostedgg.com](https://hostedgg.com/blog/palworld-work-suitability-base-pals-guide); [timesaver.gg](https://timesaver.gg/blog/palworld-best-base-pals).
- Design lesson quoted by guides: "The single most common base mistake is stationing Pals that do five jobs at level 2 each. A Pal that does one job at level 8 outproduces one that spreads itself across five jobs, because production throughput is gated by the level of the Pal actually on the task" — [guildorder.com](https://guildorder.com/games/palworld/guides/work-suitability-base-labor).
- Why it is satisfying: "Watching Pals move materials between production stations makes the settlement feel alive"; "the most satisfying moment… is returning from a long exploration to find your base humming along"; the player's question shifts "from 'Where can I find this?' to 'How can I produce enough of this automatically?'" — [gametruth.com](https://www.gametruth.com/guides/palworld-complete-base-building-and-automation-guide/); [h5playguide](https://h5playguide.com/blog/palworld-automation-makes-bases-powerful-and-efficient-but-excessive-resource-pr); [hiennguyen.blog](https://hiennguyen.blog/time-to-automate-building-the-ultimate-self-sufficient-palworld-base/).
- A hosting blog claims "over 94% of the 380,709 positive reviews highlight the immense satisfaction of achieving a well-oiled base layout" — [SparkedHost](https://blog.sparkedhost.com/palworld/best-base-building-features-in-palworld-1-0). (Methodology unstated; treat as marketing.)
- A community piece frames the "hidden problem of base automation" as excessive resource production once a base is efficient (title only; body not readable) — [h5playguide](https://h5playguide.com/blog/palworld-automation-makes-bases-powerful-and-efficient-but-excessive-resource-pr).

**Spiritfarer (PC/console, Thunder Lotus) — crew as named characters with one perk each**
- Spirit perks are specific and chore-replacing: Summer "Plays music to plants" (the song *Plantasia Fantastica*) to quicken growth in Gardens, Fields and Orchards; Gwen "Shops for fibre," "Shops for thread," and "Uses the loom" when her mood is high; Atul "Repairs buildings on the boat" and "Plays joyful music" — [Spiritfarer Fandom: Summer](https://spiritfarer.fandom.com/wiki/Summer); [Spiritfarer Fandom: Gwen](https://spiritfarer.fandom.com/wiki/Gwen); [TheGamer spirit perks guide](https://www.thegamer.com/spiritfarer-spirit-location-perks-likes-dislikes/).
- Perks are mood-gated: spirits need a daily meal and a bed, and moods must be managed for them to perform their perks — [Red Bull Spiritfarer guide](https://www.redbull.com/gb-en/spiritfarer-guide); [Wikipedia: Spiritfarer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritfarer).
- "Nearly every character comes with a unique 'resource mini-game' that triggers in special locations around the map" — [Play Critically review](https://playcritically.com/2020/12/17/spiritfarer-review/).
- Players gather "not to become rich, but to fulfill the Spirits' requests and see their impact"; the game does not demand max efficiency — the ship stops at night and the next day does not start until Stella sleeps — [The Chozo Project review](https://thechozoproject.com/spiritfarer-review/); [Play Critically](https://playcritically.com/2020/12/17/spiritfarer-review/).
- Spirits also gate construction: blueprints come from shops, Blueprint Chests, or are given by passengers; boat upgrades can require spirit flowers (earned when a spirit departs), so some spirits must be released before others' quests can finish — [Spiritfarer Fandom: Blueprints](https://spiritfarer.fandom.com/wiki/Blueprints); [Spiritfarer Fandom: Summer](https://spiritfarer.fandom.com/wiki/Summer).

**Grow a Garden pets (Roblox) — one passive per pet, stacking, visible timers**
- Pets were added in Update 1.04.0 on May 3, 2025; "having multiple of the same pets can stack their abilities"; 50+ pets across seven rarity tiers (Common → Prismatic) — [Grow a Garden Fandom: Pets](https://growagarden.fandom.com/wiki/Pets); [Gamezebo pets list](https://www.gamezebo.com/walkthroughs/grow-a-garden-pets/).
- Ability classes: "speeding up crop growth, increasing harvest yields, generating free seeds, improving mutation chances, and even interacting with neighboring players' gardens" — [GameRant: All Pets & What They Do](https://gamerant.com/grow-a-garden-all-pets-what-they-do/).
- Concrete examples: Cardinal grows magic crops 1.55x quicker; Chipmunk ("Harvest Stash") gives a chance not to consume a Harvest Tool use; Bunny ("Carrot Chomper") eats carrots and grants bonus Sheckles; Black Bird ("Dark Chirp") grants pets hatched from eggs an age bonus — [DroidGamers pet abilities guide](https://www.droidgamers.com/guides/grow-a-garden-pet-abilities-guide/); [Grow a Garden Fandom: Pets](https://growagarden.fandom.com/wiki/Pets). Community calculators list per-pet cooldowns — [mygagcalculator.com](https://mygagcalculator.com/grow-a-garden-pet-abilities/).

**Pet Simulator 99 (Roblox) — pets as damage/collection multipliers, with paid automation**
- Enchant slots are gated by rank: Rank 1 (1 slot), Rank 3 (2), Rank 8 (4), Rank 11 (5), Rank 20 (6); premium slots at 400 and 800 Robux; up to nine total — [petsimulator99.wiki enchants guide](https://petsimulator99.wiki/enchants/pet-simulator-99-enchants/); [bloxguidesgg](https://bloxguidesgg.com/games/pet-simulator-99/enchants).
- Enchants upgrade tier-by-tier using duplicates plus a small Diamond fee at the Enchant Upgrader (Area 16) — [bloxguidesgg](https://bloxguidesgg.com/games/pet-simulator-99/enchants).
- Auto Tap gamepass (350 Robux) "automatically taps breakables while standing in an area" and lets ultimates charge up to 3x faster; Huge Hunter (3,250 Robux) raises Huge-pet hatch chance by 6250% — [Pet Simulator Fandom: Auto Tap Gamepass](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Auto_Tap_Gamepass_(Pet_Simulator_99)); [Pet Simulator Fandom: Gamepasses (PS99)](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Gamepasses_(Pet_Simulator_99)).
- "Every Rebirth boosts your HUGE, TITANIC, and GARGANTUAN odds" — [BIG Games Update 43 post](https://www.biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-99-update-43).

**Restaurant Tycoon 2 (Roblox) — hired NPC helpers that learn by imitation**
- Default cap of 7 chefs+waiters, 2 operators and 1 driver; the More Workers gamepass raises chefs+waiters to 12 — [Restaurant Tycoon 2 Fandom: Workers](https://restaurant-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Workers).
- Chefs "automatically take orders from an order stand and cook them," but start as trainees who "need to watch the player cook 5 dishes" before working alone; waiters seat customers, take orders and serve; workers level up automatically at a rate set by their happiness, and higher-level chefs cook faster and waiters walk faster — [Restaurant Tycoon 2 Fandom: Workers](https://restaurant-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Workers).

**Steal a Brainrot (Roblox) — the "helper" is the income source itself**
- Income per brainrot runs from $1/s (cheapest Commons, $25) through $14/s ($1.7K Commons), $75/s (top Rare, $9.7K), $18.5K/s (top Mythic Orbi Mochi, $6M) to $1B/s for the top OG-tier (Spyder Elephant; Strawberry Elephant $750M/s); Mutations and Traits "can multiply a Brainrot's earnings several times over" — [games.gg tier list](https://games.gg/steal-a-brainrot/guides/steal-a-brainrot-complete-brainrot-tier-list/); [VGTimes tier list](https://vgtimes.com/guides/166782-steal-a-brainrot-tier-list-best-brainrots-by-income.html).

### Inferences
- "Work suitability" is the most directly portable model for alien creatures: give each species 1–2 jobs (e.g., Mining, Hauling, Welding, Power) at levels 1–10, make one specialist worth more than several generalists, and surface it with a visible "what this creature is doing now" animation (Palworld's satisfaction comes from *watching* pals work).
- Per-helper multipliers in the 1.3x–2x range (Grow a Garden's 1.55x; Spiritfarer's "quickens growth") read as meaningful when the base timer is visible; large multipliers are better reserved for rarity tiers or paid luck (PS99) than for core progression, since the DevForum warns income-doubling passes repel players.
- Restaurant Tycoon 2's "trainee watches you do it 5 times" is a strong onboarding pattern for a kids' game: the player performs the chore manually a few times, then a creature takes it over, which simultaneously teaches the mechanic and makes the creature feel earned.
- Spiritfarer shows helpers can gate *content* (blueprints given by passengers, flowers required for upgrades), not just speed — relevant if a species should be required to unlock a ship system (e.g., only a "Welder" species can finish the hull).

### Gaps
- Could not obtain Palworld's per-level work-speed formula or the exact pal-per-base cap/base levels in 1.0, nor the 1.0 release date.
- Could not obtain Grow a Garden's full pet ability table with cooldown seconds and equip-slot counts (Fandom blocked).
- Pet Simulator 99's base pet-equip slot count and pet-power multiplier ranges were not captured (one snippet mentioned "stack Pet Slots up to 100" from an unreliable source; omitted).
- No source quantified what multiplier range "feels good" to players; the ranges above are observed values, not tested thresholds.

## Key Question 3 — How are multi-stage "build the big thing" goals (ship, rocket, boat, raft) paced and chunked into satisfying milestones?

### Takeaway
Successful games chunk the big build into 4–7 named stages, each gated by one legible "key" (a specific resource, a crew count, a level) plus a generic bulk cost, and they front-load unlocks so the first 10–20 minutes deliver many small milestones before the pace slows (Roblox tycoons, Theme Park Tycoon 2's leveling rework, Astroneer's per-planet gateway, Starbound's four ship tiers, Build a Boat's per-stage gold).

### Cited Findings
- Roblox tycoon pacing: "about 200 buttons, with 50 of which unlocked within the first 10–20 minutes"; "if a game involves a lot of grinding, it's important to introduce the grind slowly — first giving players initial satisfaction of unlocking things… then motivating them to stick around and wait for the next items to unlock" — [DevForum: Is this a good tycoon progress rate?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-this-a-good-tycoon-progress-rate/2225354); [DevForum: Need help figuring out progression scaling](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/need-help-figuring-out-progression-scaling-for-my-tycoon-style-game/4005654).
- Tycoon economy tuning levers named by developers: "dropper earnings per second, dropper price, setup cash multipliers, and setup prices," balanced so progression is "gradual, not too slow or too fast" and players cannot skip to the best content — [DevForum: progression scaling](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/need-help-figuring-out-progression-scaling-for-my-tycoon-style-game/4005654).
- Theme Park Tycoon 2 replaced achievement-gated ride unlocks with a leveling system in version 520 (Nov 4, 2024) that "unlocks most content in the game in a more intuitive way and at a more relaxed pace"; XP comes from guest actions, missions and achievements, and players *select* a reward per level; by version 585 it became a tree where "players can only unlock one stall or ride per level" — [TPT2 Fandom: Leveling](https://tpt2.fandom.com/wiki/Leveling); [TPT2 Fandom: Changelog/2024](https://tpt2.fandom.com/wiki/Changelog/2024); [TPT2 Fandom: List of achievements](https://tpt2.fandom.com/wiki/List_of_achievements).
- Restaurant Tycoon 2 layers milestones: cuisines are unlocked via a World Map; XP is awarded at the end of every in-game day; player/chef levels gate dish tiers (Silver at 11, Golden at 14) — [Restaurant Tycoon 2 Fandom: Player Levels](https://restaurant-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Player_Levels); [Restaurant Tycoon 2 Fandom: Menu](https://restaurant-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Menu).
- Build a Boat for Treasure pays out per stage (~8 Gold per new stage reached) plus a chest at the end and one Gold Block per Treasure, so a single boat run is itself a chunked ladder; block tiers (Wood/Stone → Metal/Marble/Glass → Obsidian/Adamantite/Mosaic) give a visible material ladder — [BABFT complete guide](https://build-a-boat-for-treasure-wiki.wiki/en/guides/build-a-boat-for-treasure-complete-guide/); [BABFT Fandom: Gold Block](https://build-a-boat-for-treasure.fandom.com/wiki/Gold_Block).
- Astroneer (PC/console) chunks the "leave the planet / finish the game" goal per planet: on each body you (1) power the surface Gateway Chambers and (2) descend to the core and insert one planet-specific resource into the Gateway Engine; the recommended order is Sylva (start) → Desolo → Calidor → Vesania → Novus → Glacio → Atrox; chamber power requirements rise 5 → 8 → 6 → 16 → 21 → 26 → 30 U/s; engine keys are Quartz (Sylva), Zinc (Desolo), Explosive Powder (Calidor), Methane (Vesania), Hydrogen (Novus); the finale needs seven Geometric Triptychs at the satellite — [Nerdburglars: Activating Gateway Engines](https://nerdburglars.net/gameguides/activating-gateway-engines-on-each-planet/); [TrueAchievements walkthrough](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Astroneer/walkthrough/3); [TheGamer gateway chamber guide](https://www.thegamer.com/astroneer-gateway-chamber-activation/).
- Starbound (PC) chunks the ship itself: upgrades at the Outpost (Sparrow → Kestrel → Falcon → Eagle) each require more crew members (or fake licenses) plus Upgrade Modules, and each grants more room for crafting stations, containers, furniture and crew — [Starbound Fandom: Spaceship](https://starbound.fandom.com/wiki/Spaceship); [Starbounder: Ship Upgrade](https://starbounder.org/Ship_Upgrade).
- Kerbal Space Program (PC) career mode: contracts earn funds and reputation; science points unlock tech-tree parts; players "start with limited resources" and "unlock new technologies and parts to build more advanced spacecraft and explore further" — [KSP Wiki: Career mode tutorial](https://wiki.kerbalspaceprogram.com/wiki/Tutorial:Career_mode_(Richmountain112)); [ItsGamerTime career mode](https://itsgamertime.com/exploring-career-mode-in-kerbal-space-program-tips-and-tricks/).
- Spiritfarer's boat grows via blueprints (shop, chest, or gifted by passengers) built at the Blueprint Station atop the cabin from gathered resources; categories are Spirit Houses, Production buildings (gardens, kitchens, sawmills) and Decorations, some requiring movement abilities bought with 2 Obols each at shrines (e.g., Glide unlocks the Air Draft blueprint) — [Spiritfarer Fandom: Blueprints](https://spiritfarer.fandom.com/wiki/Blueprints); [Spiritfarer Fandom: Boat Buildings](https://spiritfarer.fandom.com/wiki/Boat_Buildings); [Spiritfarer Fandom: Abilities](https://spiritfarer.fandom.com/wiki/Abilities).
- Forager (PC/console/mobile): "by performing any task in the game, you earn experience points… At each level up, you can choose a perk from an expanding list… [perks] will always give you something new"; mid-game unlocks "either automate previous tasks or speed them up" — [Game Wisdom: Forager's good kind of grinding](https://game-wisdom.com/critical/forager-good-kind-grinding); [Goomba Stomp review](https://goombastomp.com/forager-review/).

### Inferences
- A per-planet ship should follow Astroneer's two-part gate: a *bulk* requirement the creatures can grind toward passively (power/currency/common metal) and a *key* requirement that forces one deliberate expedition per planet (a unique resource or a specific creature species). This gives each planet one memorable "aha" and prevents the ship from being a single long bar.
- Front-load: aim for ~5–10 small ship-part/creature unlocks in the first 15 minutes (DevForum's 50-of-200-buttons heuristic ≈ 25% of unlocks in the first ~10% of play), then let the ship's 4–6 major modules (hull, engine, life support, nav, fuel, crew bay) each take one or two sessions.
- Theme Park Tycoon 2's 2024 shift to "choose one reward per level" is a tested kid-friendly pattern: give the player a *choice* of which ship part to unlock next rather than a fixed order, which creates agency without branching complexity.

- No source gave time-to-complete per Astroneer planet or per Starbound tier.
- Theme Park Tycoon 2's exact XP-per-level curve was not captured.

## Key Question 4 — What do players do between milestones; how do idle/offline mechanics keep retention, and how is a 15–30 minute session paced?

### Takeaway
The dominant Roblox retention pattern in 2025 is "something changed while you were gone" (Grow a Garden offline growth, Steal a Brainrot base income, Bloxburg bills, Adopt Me pet needs) combined with short in-session timers that create a reason to stay a few more minutes (5-minute shop refresh, hourly weather, 3-minute days in 99 Nights). Idle-design literature recommends capping offline gains (commonly 2–24 h) precisely so a return is always rewarded but never "complete."

### Cited Findings

**Idle/offline systems on Roblox**
- Grow a Garden: "plants grow offline, guaranteeing fresh content every login"; the 5-minute seed-shop refresh "creates micro-FOMO moments," rare seeds appear randomly ("each visit into a lottery ticket"), and hourly weather changes mutate plants for bonus profit — [GEEIQ](https://geeiq.com/resources/blog/grow-a-garden); [MaxPower Gaming](https://www.maxpowergaming.co/post/grow-a-garden-keeps-driving-roblox-to-new-heights); [jens.substack: Gamification of User Engagement](https://jens.substack.com/p/gamification-of-user-engagement).
- Steal a Brainrot: every owned brainrot yields a fixed $/s; total income is the sum across the base — [games.gg](https://games.gg/steal-a-brainrot/guides/steal-a-brainrot-complete-brainrot-tier-list/). The "idle" value is also what other players steal, so waiting is risky as well as rewarding — [Wikipedia: Steal a Brainrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot).
- Pet Simulator 99 sells automation of the active chore (Auto Tap, 350 Robux) rather than offline income — [Pet Simulator Fandom: Auto Tap Gamepass](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Auto_Tap_Gamepass_(Pet_Simulator_99)).
- Welcome to Bloxburg uses a *negative* idle timer: a bill arrives every in-game week (2 h 48 min of real time), equal to 0.6% of house value (0.3% with Premium; solar panels up to 20% off); unpaid bills shut off electricity and water — [bloxburgbuilds bill calculator](https://bloxburgbuilds.com/guides/bloxburg-bill-calculator); [Bloxburg Fandom: House Bills](https://welcome-to-bloxburg.fandom.com/wiki/House_Bills); [Playbite](https://www.playbite.com/q/when-do-you-pay-house-bills-in-bloxburg).

**In-session pacing timers**
- 99 Nights in the Forest: each day lasts 3 minutes and each night 1 minute 30 seconds (4:30 per cycle); the Deer predator does not appear by day, so gathering is day-work and the campfire is the safe zone at night — [99 Nights Miraheze: Days](https://99nightsintheforestroblox.miraheze.org/wiki/Days); [99 Nights Fandom: Days](https://99-nights-in-the-forest.fandom.com/wiki/Days); [Playgama](https://playgama.com/blog/game-faqs/how-long-does-one-day-last-in-99-nights-in-the-forest/).
- Dead Rails structures a session as one 80 km train run from the fort to Mexico, with coal purchases as the pacing resource; between runs, players spend Bonds (~100–130 per full run) in the lobby on classes/trains — [Roblox Fandom: Dead Rails](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/RCM_Games/Dead_Rails); [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/roblox-dead-rails-bonds-farming-guide/).
- Restaurant Tycoon 2 awards XP "at the end of every day," making the in-game day the session's natural checkpoint — [Restaurant Tycoon 2 Fandom: Player Levels](https://restaurant-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Player_Levels).
- Spiritfarer (PC): the ship stops moving at night and the next day does not begin until the player sleeps, "giving plenty of time to catch up on unfinished tasks" — [Play Critically](https://playcritically.com/2020/12/17/spiritfarer-review/).
- Stardew Valley (PC/console/mobile): the daily cycle (wake, plan, tend crops/animals, explore, socialize, sleep before exhaustion) "keeps players coming back through the constant promise of progress: crops growing, relationships deepening, and the farm expanding" — [Kokutech Stardew analysis](https://www.kokutech.com/blog/gamedev/design-patterns/world-building/stardew-valley).

**Idle-design theory (mobile/PC literature)**
- Offline earnings: "the game calculates production accumulated during absence, applies a cap (often 2 to 24 hours), and presents the reward as a modal with a rewarded-video multiplier. The cap protects long-term engagement, because unlimited offline gain would remove the reason to reopen the app after a week away" — [Game-Ace: Idle game development](https://game-ace.com/blog/idle-game-development/).
- Pacing: "the best idle games map out a progression curve that slows down just enough to nudge players toward upgrades, new currencies, or time skips, without making the wait feel punishing"; engagement follows a "decaying" pattern from active play to occasional check-ins — [Apptrove](https://apptrove.com/how-to-make-an-idle-game/); [Mind Studios](https://games.themindstudios.com/post/idle-clicker-game-design-and-monetization/).
- Anthony Pecorella's GDC Europe 2016 talk "Quest for Progress: The Math and Design of Idle Games" is the standard reference on exponential cost/linear production and prestige loops — [GDC Vault PDF](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdceurope2016/presentations/Pecorella_Anthony_Quest%20for%20Progress.pdf); Pecorella also argued auto-clickers are "considered necessary by any 'serious' idle game players" — [Wikipedia: Incremental game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incremental_game).

### Inferences
- For the alien game: creatures should keep working offline (mining/assembling) with a visible cap (e.g., storage fills in ~8 h), so a returning player always finds a "collect" moment plus a reason to build more storage — exactly Grow a Garden's offline growth plus idle-literature's cap.
- Inside a session, borrow 99 Nights' hard 4:30 cycle or Restaurant Tycoon 2's end-of-day XP: a planetary "day" of ~3–5 minutes with a day-end report (what creatures built, what arrived) gives a 15–30-minute session 3–6 natural checkpoints.
- Bloxburg demonstrates a soft *maintenance* hook (bills) can drive returns without punishing hard; an alien analogue is creature hunger/morale that slows (not stops) work when unattended.

### Gaps
- No Roblox-specific data on average session length for any named game was found; the 15–30 minute framing could not be validated against measured numbers.
- Could not read Pecorella's deck or the Eric Guan "Idle Game Design Principles" post for recommended cost-multiplier ranges (e.g., 1.07–1.15 per purchase); those numbers are not in any snippet and are omitted.
- Dead Rails' typical run duration in minutes was not captured.

## Key Question 5 — How do base/house-building and base-visiting systems contribute to retention and monetization on Roblox (Bloxburg, Adopt Me, Brookhaven, Grow a Garden)?

### Takeaway
On Roblox, the house/base is the persistent, publicly visible record of progress; it monetizes through *capacity* (plot size, floors, furniture limits, worker caps) rather than through power, and it retains through maintenance loops (bills, pet needs) and social visiting (friends-only locks, neighbor gardens, stealing). Free-to-claim housing (Brookhaven) maximizes reach; priced housing ladders (Adopt Me's 240 → 8,000 Bucks, Bloxburg's house value) give long-run goals.

### Cited Findings
- Bloxburg: "Your house is persistence — a visual record of progress visible to every visitor on your plot… the pipeline is identical: earn money, buy a plot, enter build mode, place structure and decor, maintain bills, expand over time" — [welcometobloxburg.wiki Build overview](https://welcometobloxburg.wiki/build/).
- Bloxburg plot is 30x30 (900 units) by default; the Large Plot gamepass raises it to 50x50 (2,500 units); other gamepasses include Multiple Floors — [Bloxburg Fandom: House](https://welcome-to-bloxburg.fandom.com/wiki/House); [Profitable.app](https://profitable.app/roblox/games/welcome-to-bloxburg).
- Bloxburg bills scale with house value (0.6%/in-game week; halved by Premium), so the more you build the more you must earn — a built-in reason to return and work — [bloxburgbuilds](https://bloxburgbuilds.com/guides/bloxburg-bill-calculator); [Bloxburg Fandom: House Bills](https://welcome-to-bloxburg.fandom.com/wiki/House_Bills).
- Adopt Me (Uplift Games): houses are "a significant part of… gameplay," customizable with wallpapers, floors and furniture; default furniture limit is 4,000, raisable by 500 via Star Rewards (daily-login currency) to a max of 8,000; a "Furniture Storage" update let players store furniture across houses — [Adopt Me Fandom: Houses](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Houses); [Sportskeeda Furniture Storage guide](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/adopt-me-furniture-storage-guide).
- Adopt Me house ladder in Bucks: Tiny Home 240 (new players spawn in a free one), Family Home 489, Estate 972, Castle 1,499, Futuristic House 2,500, Luxury Apartments 8,000; Robux houses include Celebrity Mansion (800 R$) and Millionaire Mansion (1,250 R$); houses are also tradeable — [Bloxodes: All 54 Houses](https://bloxodes.com/wiki/adopt-me/houses); [adoptmehousetrading.com](https://adoptmehousetrading.com/guides/how-to-trade-houses.html).
- Brookhaven RP: most houses are free — claim any plot marked Vacant and customize; players can lock/unlock their house to restrict entry to family/friends — [brookhavenrp.com houses guide](https://www.brookhavenrp.com/guides/houses); [TechWiser: All houses in Brookhaven](https://techwiser.com/roblox-brookhaven-rp-all-houses/).
- Grow a Garden gardens are social: certain pets "interact with neighboring players' gardens," and the game's loop includes unlocking larger plots and rarer plants over time — [GameRant pets](https://gamerant.com/grow-a-garden-all-pets-what-they-do/); [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/predicting-the-next-big-hits-on-roblox/).
- Steal a Brainrot makes the base itself the social object: the goal is "to get as many brainrots as possible… by stealing them from other players and returning them to your base," described as "basically a version of capture the flag" — [Wikipedia: Steal a Brainrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot); [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/gaming-news/steal-a-brainrot-is-robloxs-latest-viral-hit-grow-a-garden-3888650).
- Restaurant Tycoon 2 monetizes capacity the same way: More Workers gamepass lifts the chef/waiter cap from 7 to 12 — [Restaurant Tycoon 2 Fandom: Workers](https://restaurant-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Workers).
- Theme Park Tycoon 2 ties cosmetic unlocks to achievements (scenery remains achievement-gated after v520; "Fully expanded!" for buying all plot expansions unlocks four coaster types) — [TPT2 Fandom: List of achievements](https://tpt2.fandom.com/wiki/List_of_achievements); [Pro Game Guides TPT2 achievements](https://progameguides.com/roblox/how-to-get-all-achievements-in-roblox-theme-park-tycoon-2/).

### Inferences
- The ship-under-construction can serve the Bloxburg "visible record" role if other players can land on/visit it: a half-built hull that friends can see is a social progress display, and the creature roster working on it is the Adopt-Me-style collection display.
- Monetize capacity, not speed: extra creature slots (cf. More Workers 7→12), larger hangar/plot (cf. Large Plot 30→50), furniture/decal limits (cf. 4,000→8,000), and cosmetic hull skins — consistent with the DevForum warning against 2x-income passes.
- Grow a Garden and Steal a Brainrot show two polarities of visiting: cooperative (pets that help neighbors) vs. adversarial (stealing). For an under-16 audience, cooperative visiting (helping a friend's creatures build) is the safer default; Steal a Brainrot proves adversarial visiting is enormously engaging but it depends on a very cheap, disposable economy.

### Gaps
- No primary data on how much of Bloxburg's or Adopt Me's revenue comes from housing vs. other items; Profitable.app's $80.3M is a modeled estimate from visits.
- Meep City housing and Grow a Garden plot-expansion costs were not captured in any snippet.
- Could not find a study quantifying the retention effect of house visiting specifically.

## Key Question 6 — What are the recurring failure modes of crafting-heavy and progression games on Roblox (complexity, onboarding, UI, grind walls, inflation, "nothing to do"), and how have successful ones simplified?

### Takeaway
Documented failures cluster into: hype decay plus a hard wipe (Islands), grind introduced before the hook (DevForum), pay-to-win income passes repelling players (DevForum), hyper-inflated currencies (Steal a Brainrot's $1/s → $1B/s range), and automation that makes the base "complete" (Palworld critique). Successful simplifications are: linearizing the tech tree into numbered bench/workbench tiers (99 Nights, Islands), giving the community an obvious "priority route," replacing hidden achievement gates with a choose-one-per-level system (Theme Park Tycoon 2, 2024), and teaching by imitation (Restaurant Tycoon 2 trainee chefs).

### Cited Findings
- Islands: peak 50–60k players in mid-late 2020 "as the hype started to die down and a wipe happened in 2021, many players left"; ~20–25k through 2021, then slow decline to ~8k before discontinuation — [Roblox Islands Fandom "Peak" thread](https://robloxislands.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000142497).
- Grind walls: "introduce the grind slowly — first giving players initial satisfaction of unlocking things and allowing them to understand how the game works" — [DevForum: Is this a good tycoon progress rate?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-this-a-good-tycoon-progress-rate/2225354); developers also ask whether their tycoon is "too easy," indicating the opposite failure — [DevForum: Is my Tycoon too easy?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-my-tycoon-too-easy/2585163).
- Pay-to-win income: 2x-cash passes "can easily drive away players" — [DevForum: Monetization Ideas](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/monetization-ideas-for-my-tycoon-game/3048249).
- Currency inflation example: Steal a Brainrot's income table spans $1/s to $1,000,000,000/s (nine orders of magnitude) across rarity tiers, with mutations multiplying further — [games.gg](https://games.gg/steal-a-brainrot/guides/steal-a-brainrot-complete-brainrot-tier-list/). (Observation of the number range; no source analyzed its effect on retention.)
- Offline-gain inflation: "unlimited offline gain would remove the reason to reopen the app after a week away" — [Game-Ace](https://game-ace.com/blog/idle-game-development/).
- "Nothing to do" after automation: a Palworld critique titled "Palworld and the Hidden Problem of Base Automation" argues efficient bases over-produce resources — [h5playguide](https://h5playguide.com/blog/palworld-automation-makes-bases-powerful-and-efficient-but-excessive-resource-pr); another piece lists "Six Base-Automation Fixes Palworld Players Want" — [palmods.gg](https://www.palmods.gg/blog/palworld-base-automation-fixes). (Only titles were readable.)
- Procedural repetition (planet games): No Man's Sky "gets repetitive, with the core issue being one biome per planet," versus Outer Wilds' "one small solar system… packed with hand-made content" where "each planet… is unique in both aesthetics and gameplay" — [Steam: Outer Wilds discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/753640/discussions/0/2518023667589043781/); [NeoGAF: Outer Wilds the anti-No Man's Sky](https://www.neogaf.com/threads/lttp-outer-wilds-its-the-anti-no-mans-sky-whide-as-a-puddle-deep-as-an-ocean.1654392/); [Tom's Guide NMS review "Empty Space"](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/no-mans-sky,review-3845.html). (Forum opinion; PC/console titles.)

**Simplifications that worked**
- 99 Nights in the Forest: progression "tied directly to your Crafting Bench" with numbered tiers; the community converged on a fixed order ("navigation, base defense, food and recovery, fuel and weather, then late-game gem crafts") and "craft a map as soon as the recipe is available… gives the team one shared picture of the run" — [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/99-nights-in-the-forest-crafting); [questcodes crafting guide](https://questcodes.com/roblox/99-nights-in-the-forest/crafting); [pixeltwelve beginner guide](https://pixeltwelve.com/articles/99-nights-in-the-forest-beginner-guide).
- Islands: a strictly linear early route (wood → Tier 1 workbench → campfire → iron/coal → smelter → steel mill at 100 iron + 50 gold) — [Skyblockipedia](https://skyblockipedia.fandom.com/wiki/Ultimate_Guide_to_Islands_-_Roblox); [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Islands(Roblox)).
- Theme Park Tycoon 2 (Nov 2024): moved from achievement-gated rides to leveling "in a more intuitive way and at a more relaxed pace," then (v585) to a tree where you pick one unlock per level — [TPT2 Fandom: Leveling](https://tpt2.fandom.com/wiki/Leveling).
- Restaurant Tycoon 2: trainee chefs "need to watch the player cook 5 dishes until they can start cooking by themselves" — onboarding the automation by first making the player do the chore — [Restaurant Tycoon 2 Fandom: Workers](https://restaurant-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Workers).
- Build a Boat for Treasure: one currency ("Gold is the only currency that matters") and a material ladder of ~9 block types — [BABFT complete guide](https://build-a-boat-for-treasure-wiki.wiki/en/guides/build-a-boat-for-treasure-complete-guide/).
- Palworld's community-recommended fix for complexity is specialization: "short paths, specialized workers, and systematic production chains" — [gametruth.com](https://www.gametruth.com/guides/palworld-complete-base-building-and-automation-guide/).
- Forager keeps grind pleasant by guaranteeing novelty: each level-up perk "will always give you something new," and mid-game perks automate or accelerate earlier chores — [Game Wisdom](https://game-wisdom.com/critical/forager-good-kind-grinding).

### Inferences
- Keep the recipe graph a *ladder*, not a *web*: one bench/assembler with numbered tiers (99 Nights, Islands) and at most ~10 material types (Build a Boat) is the complexity ceiling the evidence supports for Roblox's audience.
- Avoid wipes/resets of the creature collection; if a per-planet "reset" is needed (new planet = new base), carry the creatures and ship over (see KQ7) so sunk cost is preserved — Islands' wipe is the counter-example.
- Keep currency within ~3–4 orders of magnitude per planet and let the planet change (new materials) rather than the numbers inflate; Steal a Brainrot's nine-order range works only because it is a disposable, meme-driven economy.
- Design a "what to do when the base is efficient" answer up front (Palworld critique): on this concept, that answer is the launch itself — once creatures out-produce needs, the ship completes and the player leaves.

### Gaps
- No DevForum thread on crafting-UI complexity was retrievable (search budget exhausted before that query ran); the UI-specific failure mode is inferred from Theme Park Tycoon 2's and 99 Nights' simplifications rather than directly documented.
- Deepwoken crafting and SharkBite progression could not be researched (searches not executed).
- No post-mortem for Booga Booga's original decline was found; only its "Reborn" status.

## Key Question 7 — How do planet-hopping games structure the new-planet reset so it feels fresh instead of repetitive?

### Takeaway
Games that make planet-hopping feel fresh change *constraints* rather than just skins: a different survival/power constraint per planet (Astroneer's rising power needs and distinct hazards), a different key resource that only that planet provides (Astroneer gateway resources; Starbound's tier ores), a new environmental protection requirement (Starbound EPPs), and the player carrying a persistent, upgradable vehicle/crew across planets (Starbound ship, Spiritfarer boat). Handcrafted small sets of planets (Outer Wilds, Astroneer's 7 bodies) are consistently praised over procedurally infinite ones (No Man's Sky) for exactly this reason.

### Cited Findings
- Astroneer (System Era; PC/console): seven bodies ordered by difficulty (Sylva, Desolo, Calidor, Vesania, Novus, Glacio, Atrox); each demands more power to activate its chambers (5/8/6/16/21/26/30 U/s) and a *different* resource to activate its core (Quartz, Zinc, Explosive Powder, Methane, Hydrogen on the first five) — [Nerdburglars](https://nerdburglars.net/gameguides/activating-gateway-engines-on-each-planet/); [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Astroneer/walkthrough/3). Community threads debate "Progression and Planet Hopping" and ask for progression reworks, indicating pacing is a live design concern there — [Steam: Progression and Planet Hopping](https://steamcommunity.com/app/361420/discussions/3/1726450077675929479/); [Steam: Game progression rework suggestion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/361420/discussions/3/4241812037678570093/).
- Starbound (Chucklefish; PC): planets are tiered; Tiers 1–2 are starter areas incl. moons and asteroid fields; Tier 3 "mainly focused on the acquisition of titanium ore… without any unusual environmental hazards"; Tier 4 (Jungle, Mutated, Toxic) requires a radiation EPP "or else the deadly radiation will limit the player's health"; "the game abandons fairly linear progression after tier 2 and allows the player to advance at their own pace, and even take risky shortcuts to jump up a few tiers" — [Starbounder: Tier](https://starbounder.org/Tier). The ship persists and grows (Sparrow → Eagle) with crew — [Starbound Fandom: Spaceship](https://starbound.fandom.com/wiki/Spaceship).
- Outer Wilds vs No Man's Sky: "Outer Wilds gives you just one small solar system that is packed with hand-made content of substance, while No Man's Sky tries to give you infinite exploration by procedurally generating the whole universe"; "each planet in Outer Wilds is unique in both aesthetics and gameplay features"; NMS "gets repetitive… one biome per planet" — [Steam: Outer Wilds discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/753640/discussions/0/2518023667589043781/); [NeoGAF thread](https://www.neogaf.com/threads/lttp-outer-wilds-its-the-anti-no-mans-sky-whide-as-a-puddle-deep-as-an-ocean.1654392/); [Steam: No Man's Sky discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/275850/discussions/0/5533260453822439417). (Player opinion, not developer statements.)
- Spiritfarer: the boat is the persistent home; each island visited has a spirit-specific "resource mini-game," so new locations introduce new verbs rather than new numbers — [Play Critically](https://playcritically.com/2020/12/17/spiritfarer-review/).
- Kerbal Space Program: progression to new bodies is gated by tech-tree parts bought with science, funds and reputation from contracts, so "explore further into space" is the reward for each cycle — [KSP Wiki career tutorial](https://wiki.kerbalspaceprogram.com/wiki/Tutorial:Career_mode_(Richmountain112)).
- Roblox space games: Space Sailors (Pulsarnova) offers six destinations (Lunar Gateway, ISS, Moon via an Artemis mission, Mars, Ceres, Venus via the Zephyr Rover) and planet-specific collectibles (Martian, Cererian, lunar samples mined with a pickaxe at bases) — [Roblox Fandom: Space Sailors](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Player:Pulsarnova/SPACE_SAILORS); [Space Sailors on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/5000143962/Space-Sailors). Starscape (ZolarKeth, March 2017) is an open universe of 4,000+ star systems where players harvest resources, build customizable ships, and progress from faction missions/X-structure looting to hauling, mining, drone hunting, system capture and piracy; the tutorial gifts a Stratos ship — [Starscape Fandom](https://starscape-roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Starscape_Wiki); [Starscape Fandom: FAQ](https://starscape-roblox.fandom.com/wiki/FAQ); [Roblox Fandom: Starscape](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Player:ZolarKeth/Starscape).
- Forager (PC/console/mobile) uses land purchase to "fill in the land," expanding the map as the economy grows — [Game Wisdom](https://game-wisdom.com/critical/forager-good-kind-grinding).

### Inferences
- A small, handcrafted planet set (5–7, like Astroneer/Outer Wilds) with one new constraint each (e.g., no sunlight → need a bioluminescent creature for power; low gravity → hull must be anchored; acid rain → creatures need shelter) will read as fresh to kids more reliably than procedural variety.
- Each planet should introduce one *new creature job* and one *planet-only ship material* (Astroneer's per-planet gateway key, Starbound's per-tier ore), so the ship's next module is visibly "made of this planet."
- Carry the ship and creature roster forward (Starbound, Spiritfarer) and let the planet-side base be the disposable part; this preserves sunk cost (Islands lesson) while giving a genuine reset of the building space.
- Starbound's "non-linear after tier 2 with risky shortcuts" suggests letting experienced players skip ahead to a harder planet with under-built ships for faster rewards — a natural replay hook for older players without complicating onboarding.

### Gaps
- Could not read Astroneer's own progression stages (Steam guide blocked) or any System Era developer talk on planet pacing.
- No Roblox space game with a *build-the-ship-to-leave* loop was found; Space Sailors and Starscape are vehicle/combat sandboxes, so there is no direct Roblox precedent for the alien-ship concept in the sources reached.
- Galaxy (Roblox) was not researched (search budget).
- Outer Wilds and No Man's Sky evidence is forum opinion rather than developer or analyst sources.

### Inferences
- The pattern across the reference set: Roblox hits with the broadest reach use one currency and shallow, guided crafting; PC/console references (Palworld, Spiritfarer, Astroneer, Starbound) supply the richer helper and planet-progression models, which need to be simplified (fewer jobs, fewer materials, visible timers) for a mobile-first under-16 audience.
