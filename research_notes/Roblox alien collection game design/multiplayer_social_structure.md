# Multiplayer, Social and Server Structure on Roblox (for a one-planet-per-server alien collection game)

**Research date:** 2026-10-04. **Sourcing note for the report writer:** the research sandbox's egress proxy blocked direct fetches of create.roblox.com, devforum.roblox.com, all Fandom wikis, Wikipedia, Naavik, Medium/Substack, Roblox newsroom/help pages and most games press. Two channels worked: (1) WebSearch result summaries, which quote the blocked pages (facts marked "via search summary" below were read that way, not from the full page), and (2) a sparse git clone of Roblox's official `Roblox/creator-docs` GitHub repo (commit 9f840b1, 2026-10-02), which is the source for every create.roblox.com citation below and is current. The WebSearch budget for the session ran out before the final sweep, so a few named titles (Fisch, Meep City, Fortnite Creative) have no sources and are listed as gaps rather than guessed.

## Key Question 1: Which multiplayer structure (shared server + personal plots, private co-op servers, hub + instanced worlds) correlates with the highest retention and virality on Roblox right now, and why?

### Takeaway
The 2025 record-setters (Grow a Garden, Steal a Brainrot) are both "one small shared public server, each player owns a plot/base in it, everyone can see and touch everyone else's stuff"; the co-op session hits (99 Nights in the Forest, Dead Rails, Dungeon Quest) use a hub/lobby plus a reserved server per squad. Roblox's own design docs say socially-interactive, shared-space games retain better and single-player-ish games struggle, so for a planet-per-server collection game the evidence favors a small shared planet with personal bases as the default, with private/co-op planets as an option rather than the primary mode.

### Cited Findings
- Grow a Garden (released March 26, 2025) is a free-to-play multiplayer idle farming game; players buy seeds and harvest crops for money; seeds rotate in and out of stock and exclusive weekly items require being online, which "help[s] to boost player retention"; each farm "sits right next to others" and players can pay Robux to "swipe crops from your neighbors." It became "the largest game ever by concurrent users," passing 12B visits within months and recording 21.3M CCU in June 2025 (via search summary) — [Naavik, Predicting the Next Big Hits on Roblox](https://naavik.co/digest/predicting-the-next-big-hits-on-roblox/); [Wikipedia: Grow a Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)
- Grow a Garden servers hold 6 garden plots but were capped at 5 players; players on the game's wiki forum attribute the reduction from 6 to 5 to a glitch where simultaneous joins put 7 players in 6 gardens, merging two players' gardens so they could steal/destroy each other's plants for free (via search summary; community explanation, not developer statement) — [Grow a Garden Wiki forum: "why did grow a garden servers went to 6 to 5 max players?"](https://growagarden.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000064812); [allthings.how: Grow a Garden Player Count, Server Limit](https://allthings.how/grow-a-garden-player-count-server-limit-and-record-highs/)
- Steal a Brainrot (created May 2025 by do_small and SpyderSammy under BRAZILIAN SPYDER / Do Big Studios) spawns each player at "one of the eight bases found on the map"; the player earns cash from brainrots kept in the base; brainrots are bought from a conveyor whose price rises each time another player tries to get one, or stolen from another player's unlocked base; ~5.3B visits as of July 24, 2025 (via search summary) — [Roblox Wiki: Steal a Brainrot](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/BRAZILIAN_SPYDER/Steal_a_Brainrot)
- Grow a Garden achieved a Guinness World Record in July 2025 for most concurrently played video game at 21.6M; in September 2025 Steal a Brainrot surpassed it with 25.4M concurrent users (via search summary) — [Mi-3: Roblox garden, brainrot games set new records in 2025](https://www.mi-3.com.au/17-12-2025/roblox-garden-brainrot-games-set-new-records-2025-unprecedented-player-engagement)
- On August 23, 2025, the two games' developers (Jandel for Grow a Garden, SpyderSammy for Steal a Brainrot) hyped a simultaneous "Admin War" of Admin Abuse events across TikTok, X and Discord; both games exceeded 20M CCU the same day and Roblox as a platform hit 47.4M concurrent players, its record (via search summary) — [Tubefilter: Developer beef just helped Roblox set a 47-million-player record](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/08/25/roblox-grow-garden-steal-brainrot-admin-war-record/)
- Grow a Garden won Best New Experience, Best Simulation and People's Choice at the 2025 Roblox Innovation Awards; Steal a Brainrot won Best Creative Direction (via search summary) — [PocketGamer.biz: Roblox reveals 2025 Innovation Awards winners](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/roblox-reveals-2025-innovation-awards-winners/)
- 99 Nights in the Forest (2025, Grandma's Favorite Games) is a co-op survival game where "players are put into teams of up to four other players, or can play the game on their own"; difficulty scales with team size (via search summary) — [TV Tropes: 99 Nights in the Forest](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/NinetyNineNightsInTheForest); [PC Gamer: 99 Nights in the Forest classes](https://www.pcgamer.com/roblox/99-nights-in-the-forest-classes/)
- Dead Rails uses a Lobby where "players can create parties for runs," choosing a gamemode and a party size "from 1 to 10"; a Party Code/ID is generated when a run starts, and on a private server "the Party Code is the only way for your friends to find you" (via search summary) — [Dead Rails Wiki: Lobby](https://deadrails.fandom.com/wiki/Lobby); [Noleep: How to Join Party in Dead Rails](https://noleep.com/en/how-to-join-party-dead-rails/)
- Dungeon Quest's Lobby is where players spawn and "make parties, trade, upgrade their items, sell their items for gold, test their damage, reset their skill points, and claim daily rewards" before entering dungeon runs (via search summary) — [Dungeon Quest Wiki: Lobby](https://dungeonquestroblox.fandom.com/wiki/Lobby)
- Pet Simulator 99 public servers hold 50 players, private servers 35, and the Trading Plaza is a separate server holding up to 50 (via search summary) — [Pro Game Guides: Pet Simulator 99 Private Server Links](https://progameguides.com/roblox/pet-simulator-99-private-server-links-dynamic_date-formatf-y/)
- Roblox's official design guidance: "Many users treat Roblox as a place to hang out with their friends... You can even make invitations a feature of your gameplay through mechanics such as parties and trading. Encouraging social gameplay can also help with retention, as users can act as evergreen sources of content for each other... For these reasons, single-player games often find it harder to build and retain an audience on Roblox." It also warns: "avoid making private servers prohibitively expensive, as this may discourage groups of friends from playing together" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Design for Roblox](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/design-for-roblox)
- Roblox's retention doc lists, for Day-30 retention, "Add social mechanics... Common social systems and elements include trading, parties, guilds, and competitive systems such as PvP (player versus player), tournaments and leaderboards" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Retention](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/retention)
- Roblox's engagement doc: "Remove unnecessary barriers to social interaction: Many successful games on Roblox highlight socializing with friends and other users a core part of their design" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Engagement](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/engagement)
- Roblox's LiveOps doc: "Social: Systems like guilds, groups, trading, and parties promote player interaction and collaboration. By participating in these systems, players provide content for each other, introduce variety into gameplay sessions, and form friends, all of which encourage repeat visits." — [Roblox Creator Docs: LiveOps essentials](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/liveops-essentials)
- Roblox's discovery algorithm now optimizes for retention, having expanded "Recommended For You" from a 7-day view to a 28-day view with separate signals for Day 1, Days 2–7 and Days 8–28 (via search summary) — [Naavik: The State of UGC Games (2026)](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/the-state-of-ugc-games-2026/)
- Roblox (June 2026) describes the discovery signals it rewards: "When players come back to a game over time, bring their friends, and spend money, that's strong evidence that the game is delivering a meaningful experience" (via search summary) — [Roblox Newsroom: Optimizing Discovery (June 2026)](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/06/optimizing-discovery-great-games-reach-millions-players-roblox)
- Roblox's instance-streaming doc explicitly uses the "home base" + "trading hub" pattern as an example: "In many games, players frequently move back and forth between the same areas, for example between their 'home base' and a 'trading hub.' In such cases, you can create a replication focus point in each area" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Instance streaming](https://create.roblox.com/docs/workspace/streaming)

### Inferences
- The two most-played games in Roblox history share a structure: tiny public servers (5 and 8 players), one persistent personal plot/base per player, everyone's collection visible and interactable, and a shared resource that creates contention (seed shop stock; the brainrot conveyor whose price rises when others bid). The small server size is not incidental: it makes every neighbor legible and makes "my stuff next to your stuff" the whole social surface.
- Hub + instanced sessions (99 Nights, Dead Rails, Dungeon Quest) work for round-based co-op where runs end; they are not what the collection/idle hits use for the core loop. Pet Simulator 99 uses a separate place only for the Trading Plaza, keeping the main loop in shared public servers.
- For "each server is one planet, every player has a base," the closest proven analog is Steal a Brainrot's 8-base map and Grow a Garden's 6-plot map. A planet with a fixed number of base slots (6–10) matched to a 5–10 player server is the pattern with the strongest 2025 evidence; private/co-op planets (reserved servers) should be a secondary mode for friend groups, and Roblox's own guidance says not to price them out of reach.
- Roblox's discovery algorithm (28-day retention signals, "bring their friends") structurally rewards the shared-social structure over the solo-instanced one.

### Gaps
- No retention benchmark data (D1/D7/D30) specific to any of these games was found; Roblox does not publish per-game retention and the third-party pages claiming "players with friends retain 3x" (rolearn.dev, bloxg.com) are unsourced marketing content and were not used.
- Fisch's party/server structure could not be researched (search budget exhausted before a Fisch-specific query returned usable content).
- Naavik's full "Predicting the Next Big Hits" and "State of UGC Games (2026)" analyses were blocked; only search-summary excerpts were available, so Naavik's own conclusions about which structure wins were not read in full.

## Key Question 2: How do successful games let players show off collections/bases to others (visiting, leaderboards, display slots), and how much does that drive engagement?

### Takeaway
Showing off is mostly achieved by making the collection physically present in the shared server (brainrot slots on base floors, crops in an adjacent plot, pets following the player) plus explicit visiting systems (Adopt Me house locks, Bloxburg "Visit House"/neighbors, Animal Crossing Dodo Codes). Quantified engagement lift from "show-off" features specifically was not found in any source; the evidence is structural (every top game does it) rather than statistical.

### Cited Findings
- In Steal a Brainrot, the base "is the building in which players hold their brainrots and collect money from them," with "a gate consisting of red laser beams. Only the owner can go through that gate. They can also allow their Friends to enter as well." The first floor has "slots for 10 brainrots"; the second rebirth adds a second floor and the tenth rebirth adds a third; from the second rebirth each rebirth adds one brainrot slot (via search summary) — [Roblox Wiki: Steal a Brainrot](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/BRAZILIAN_SPYDER/Steal_a_Brainrot); [Steal a Brainrot Wiki: Base](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Base)
- Grow a Garden places each player's farm "right next to others" in the server so neighbors' crops are visible and (for Robux) stealable (via search summary) — [Naavik, Predicting the Next Big Hits on Roblox](https://naavik.co/digest/predicting-the-next-big-hits-on-roblox/)
- Adopt Me houses: "Players can lock and unlock their own houses to prevent players that are not in their family or friends list from entering. Doors of unlocked houses will swing open if a player is nearby, while the doors of locked houses will stay put and send a notice that the house is locked" (via search summary) — [Adopt Me! Wiki: Houses](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Houses)
- Welcome to Bloxburg: to visit a house, both players must be in the same server and the owner clicks the visitor and selects "Invite to House"; alternatively "Visit House" can be selected from the friends list; players who choose adjacent plots become neighbors and "can visit each other's houses anytime, even if the other person isn't there" (via search summary) — [Playbite: How to Go to Someone's House in Bloxburg](https://www.playbite.com/how-to-go-to-someones-house-in-bloxburg/)
- Lumber Tycoon 2 persists bases via a Load menu with "six save slots" showing slot number, last-saved time and "Data Size," where "Data Size is how many items and structures are in one's Land... Loading speed also depends on Data Size"; the game autosaves after the first manual save (via search summary) — [Lumber Tycoon 2 Wiki: Load](https://lumber-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Load)
- Pet Simulator 99's Trading Plaza is a separate server (up to 50 players) with player booths where items are listed at prices; guides warn of "extra zero" pricing traps and booths listing items at discounted RAP to lure buyers (via search summary) — [Pro Game Guides: PS99 Private Server Links](https://progameguides.com/roblox/pet-simulator-99-private-server-links-dynamic_date-formatf-y/); [U7Buy: PS99 Trading Plaza Booth Guide](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/ps99-trading-plaza-booth-guide/)
- Animal Crossing: New Horizons (reference, non-Roblox): a Dodo Code is "a randomized 5 character code" used to invite others to one's island; "Each island can hold a maximum of 8 players at a time" (via search summary) — [Animal Crossing Wiki: Dodo Code](https://animalcrossing.fandom.com/wiki/Dodo_Code); [NookFriends: How to visit others](https://www.nookfriends.com/articles/animal-crossing-new-horizons-how-to-visit-others)
- Roblox ships a Profile Card developer module that "is a great way to see more information about others within a game, from badges achieved to the player's favorite games," and a Friends Locator module that "lets players easily find and teleport to their friends inside a place" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Profile Card](https://create.roblox.com/docs/resources/modules/profile-card); [Roblox Creator Docs: Friends Locator](https://create.roblox.com/docs/resources/modules/friends-locator)
- Roblox's design doc: "Users spend a lot of time and money on their avatars and typically want them to be seen by their friends and other users" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Design for Roblox](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/design-for-roblox)
- Roblox's retention doc lists leaderboards among social/competitive systems that help long-term retention — [Roblox Creator Docs: Retention](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/retention)
- Ordered data stores "can only store numbers... You can rank and sort this data numerically" and are the documented primitive for global leaderboards — [Roblox Creator Docs: Choose between cloud services](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores-vs-memory-stores)
- Roblox's Friend Invite/Referral system populates `Player:GetJoinData().ReferredByPlayerId` for all invitation types so developers can reward both inviter and invitee — [Roblox Creator Docs: Friend referral system](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/referral-system)

### Inferences
- The dominant 2025 pattern is "passive show-off": the collection is a physical, always-visible part of the shared map (base slots, plots, following pets), so showing off costs the player nothing and costs the developer no extra UI. An alien "display slot" system on each base (N visible pedestals that grow with progression, as Steal a Brainrot's floors do) directly mirrors the proven pattern.
- Explicit visiting (Adopt Me/Bloxburg/Animal Crossing) is layered on top for cross-server friends: a lock state (owner / friends / everyone), an invite action from the player context menu, and a "visit from friends list" entry point. Bloxburg's "neighbors can visit even when the owner is offline" shows a base can be loaded from saved data without the owner present, which is what cross-server planet visiting requires (see Q5 on DataStore limits for base snapshots).
- Lumber Tycoon 2's "Data Size" number on each save slot is a visible admission of the cost of saving free-form bases; a slot-based base (fixed pedestals) rather than free placement keeps save data small and loads fast.

### Gaps
- No source quantified the engagement lift of visiting or display features (no A/B data, no developer talks found in the accessible set).
- Meep City's estate-visiting system and Fortnite Creative's island-code visiting were not researched (search budget exhausted before those queries could run).
- Grow a Garden's exact friend-visiting flow (whether it has a cross-server "visit garden" or only same-server neighbors / private servers) was not confirmed; one fan site describes a private-room ID but it is not an authoritative source and was not used.

## Key Question 3: How do trading systems help or hurt (economy health, scams, moderation burden)? Should a new small team ship trading at launch?

### Takeaway
Trading is a proven long-term retention/economy pillar in Adopt Me and Pet Simulator 99, but the sources show it carries a large, ongoing scam and support burden that Adopt Me only partially contained by gating trading behind a Trade License + quiz, trade history and in-game reporting, and that is policed by platform rules against off-platform/Robux trades. Evidence favors launching without open trading and adding a gated, logged, value-visible system later once the economy and moderation tooling exist.

### Cited Findings
- Adopt Me introduced a "Trade License," "a new toy that gives you the ability to trade Legendary and Ultra-rare pets, access your 30 day trading history, and report scammers in-game"; obtaining it requires passing a "Trade License Quiz... made of 3 easy trading questions, to make sure the players know what kinds of trades are legitimate, and which will result in them being scammed" (via search summary) — [Adopt Me!: Trade Changes & Scam Prevention update](https://www.playadopt.me/news/trade-changes-and-scam-prevention-update)
- Adopt Me's official account: "Trading items for Robux, real money or 'cross-trades' (items in other games) is against Roblox rules and if you sell or buy Adopt Me items for real money OR Robux, your account and all alt accounts will be banned. 'Trust trades' are scams." — [Adopt Me! on X](https://x.com/PlayAdoptMe/status/1246626367338434561)
- Adopt Me's wiki rules state players cannot offer or request trades with more than 18 items at once, and that cross-trading (for Bucks, Robux, gift cards, or items in other games) is prohibited (via search summary) — [Adopt Me! Wiki: Rules and Guidelines](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Adopt_Me!_Wiki:Rules_and_Guidelines)
- Uplift Games (Adopt Me's studio) maintains dedicated support articles "Inappropriate Trading Conduct" and "Dealing with a Scammed Account," i.e., a standing support workload for trading disputes (titles via search results; pages blocked) — [Uplift Games Help: Inappropriate Trading Conduct](https://upliftgames.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/39522252445972-Inappropriate-Trading-Conduct); [Uplift Games Help: Dealing with a Scammed Account](https://upliftgames.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/39529601838740-Dealing-with-a-Scammed-Account)
- Roblox publishes a help article on player trading scams covering common scam types (page blocked; title via search) — [Roblox Help: How to avoid trading scams](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/203312390-Player-Trading-Scams)
- Pet Simulator 99 scam patterns reported by players and guides: scripts that "swap a high-value pet for a basic one the split-second before you hit 'accept'"; the "extra zero" booth pricing trap; impersonation of creators/Big Games developers; and "RAP manipulation" where items are listed at discounted RAP in public booths (via search summary) — [Pet Simulator Wiki forum: Scams beware](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000333139); [U7Buy: PS99 Trading Plaza Booth Guide](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/ps99-trading-plaza-booth-guide/)
- PS99 pet values are maintained by third-party value lists computed from "trader input, community feedback, in-game trading booth prices, and Recent Average Price (RAP) data"; e.g., the top pet (Huge Empyrean Axolotl) listed at ~915B gems (via search summary) — [Game.guide: Pet Simulator 99 Value List](https://www.game.guide/pet-simulator-99-value-list)
- Roblox policy: Roblox "prohibits selling, trading, or giving away Robux and digital goods except through official channels on the Roblox platform"; engaging in "off-platform, secondary market transactions for virtual content from experiences is prohibited" under the Creator Terms; consequences include account termination (via search summary) — [DevForum: Trading In Game Items For Robux](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/trading-in-game-items-for-robux-is-it-allowed/3572074); [Romarket: Can You Buy & Sell Roblox Games? TOS Explained](https://romarket.app/guides/can-you-buy-and-sell-roblox-games)
- Rolimons operates game-statistics pages (e.g., for Pet Simulator 99) alongside its limited-item value tracking (page blocked; existence via search) — [Rolimons: Pet Simulator 99 game page](https://www.rolimons.com/game/8737899170)
- Roblox's economy guidance: balanced economies require "resource sinks" to "remove resources from circulation," and recommends A/B testing prices with Experiments and tuning with Configs "without restarting your servers" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Balance virtual economies](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/balance-virtual-economies)
- Roblox's onboarding guidance treats trading as a mid/late-game feature surfaced contextually: Squishmallows' marketplace "mainly serves mid and late-game players trading rare or high-level Squishmallows," so new players meet it via contextual tutorials; it also suggests a timed hint "if a player hasn't engaged with a game's trade feature in their first two sessions" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Onboarding techniques](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding-techniques)
- Roblox's retention doc names trading as a Day-30 retention mechanic — [Roblox Creator Docs: Retention](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/retention)
- Dungeon Quest exposes trading in its lobby alongside parties and upgrades (via search summary) — [Dungeon Quest Wiki: Lobby](https://dungeonquestroblox.fandom.com/wiki/Lobby)
- Roblox's DataStore sample explicitly motivates session locking because without it "one server saves out-of-date information. This can lead to data loss and common item duplication loopholes" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Player data and purchasing sample](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/player-data-purchasing)

### Inferences
- The help side of trading (D30 retention, player-generated content, economy depth) is well attested; the hurt side is concrete and recurring: scams need in-game reporting, trade history, a confirmation UI resistant to last-second swaps, item-count caps, and a support process for "I got scammed" tickets. Adopt Me, a large studio, needed a license/quiz gate and a dedicated support desk; Pet Sim 99's open booth market still produces regular scam reports.
- Trading also introduces duplication-exploit risk unless player data uses session locking and atomic UpdateAsync writes (Q5), and it creates off-platform RMT markets that Roblox policy makes the developer partly responsible for policing.
- For a small team: do not ship open trading at launch. Ship the non-trading social loop first (visiting, display, light stealing/borrowing). If/when trading is added: gate it behind a license/quiz and a progression threshold; cap items per trade; require two-step confirmation with a cooldown after any slot changes; log every trade to a DataStore for 30-day history; expose an in-game report button; and publish an in-game value reference so third-party lists don't become the only source of truth.

### Gaps
- No quantified data on scam volume, support ticket counts or trading's measured retention impact for any specific game was available (Adopt Me's announcement page and Uplift's help center were blocked; only search summaries were readable).
- Rolimons' methodology and its coverage of in-experience items (vs. Roblox Limiteds) could not be verified; the site was blocked.
- Whether Steal a Brainrot has or had an official trading system was not confirmed from an authoritative source (one low-quality page mentions trading; not used).

## Key Question 4: What does the party/co-op flow look like in top games (invite, teleport, shared vs individual progress), and what platform party features exist?

### Takeaway
Roblox's platform-level Party (up to 6 friends, Dec 2024) auto-places a party into the same server and the 2025 Party API (`Player.PartyId`, `SocialService:GetPartyAsync`) tells a server where every party member is across servers (PlaceId, JobId, reserved-server code) so developers can teleport them together; top co-op games layer a lobby → party → reserved-server-per-run flow on top (Dead Rails 1–10, 99 Nights up to ~5), with per-run instances disposable and persistent progress (classes, cosmetics, currency) kept per player.

### Cited Findings
- Roblox Party (announced December 2, 2024): "a seamless way for up to six friends to group up and jump into an experience together"; members "can automatically join the same game server, move between experiences as one group, and use Party chat (text for ages 13+, with voice support coming later)"; Party "is limited to existing friends"; a Party API for developers was slated for early 2025 to customize the experience for party members (via search summary) — [Roblox Newsroom: Introducing Party on Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2024/12/join-the-party-on-roblox); [DevForum: Party Is Here!](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/party-is-here-unlock-a-new-level-of-engagement/3282068)
- The Party API was announced on the DevForum ("Party API Is Here! Enable Connected Player Experiences and Drive Deeper Engagement"); page blocked, date not confirmed (2025) — [DevForum: Party API Is Here!](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/party-api-is-here-enable-connected-player-experiences-and-drive-deeper-engagement/3676068)
- `Player.PartyId` is "A read-only string identifying the party the player currently belongs to within the experience. If the player is not in a party, this value is an empty string." — [Roblox Creator Docs: Player.PartyId](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Player#PartyId)
- `SocialService:GetPartyAsync(partyId)` "reflects the current state of the party across all active server instances within the experience" and returns, per member, `UserId`, `PlaceId`, `JobId`, `PrivateServerId` and `ReservedServerAccessCode` ("Useful for teleporting party members to each other using TeleportService:TeleportAsync()"); `SocialService:GetPlayersByPartyId(partyId)` returns connected players in that party on the current server; Studio has a "Party Simulator" testing mode — [Roblox Creator Docs: SocialService](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/SocialService)
- Official teleport guidance: to teleport a whole group, check `Player.Team` or `Player.PartyId` and use `Team:GetPlayers()` or `SocialService:GetPlayersByPartyId()`; `TeleportAsync()` is server-only; set `TeleportOptions.ShouldReserveServer = true` for a fresh reserved server or `ReservedServerAccessCode` to join a specific one; `SetTeleportData()` is "non-secure" and visible to the client, so currency/inventory must go through DataStores/MemoryStores; wrap in `pcall` and retry, especially for reserved servers; `TeleportInitFailed` fires when a teleport silently fails — [Roblox Creator Docs: Teleport between places](https://create.roblox.com/docs/projects/teleport)
- "Access Control for Places" (Creator Dashboard → Audience → Access Settings) has three modes: Fully open ("players can join any place in your game through teleports from any game, including deep links, game invites, joining a friend"; "If you don't choose this option, players who try to join their friends in non-start places will instead join your game's start place"), Limited to same universe, and Secure within universe only (server-initiated teleports only; recommended "for places that exclusively use reserved servers") — [Roblox Creator Docs: Teleport between places](https://create.roblox.com/docs/projects/teleport)
- DevForum guidance: `ReserveServer` "is used to get a server that is private only to the people that are getting teleported into it. Friends will not be able to join." (via search summary) — [DevForum: How to reserve and teleport to private servers](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-reserve-and-teleport-to-private-servers/2037956)
- `TeleportService:GetPlayerPlaceInstanceAsync(userId)` returns the PlaceId and JobId of the server a user is in within the same game so another player can follow them, but the docs note the JobId "returned is not appropriate for use with reserved servers" — [Roblox Creator Docs: TeleportService](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/TeleportService)

### Inferences
- The modern canonical flow for a co-op planet: (1) friends form a platform Party (≤6) or an in-game party in the hub/planet; (2) server reads `Player.PartyId`, gathers members with `GetPlayersByPartyId`, and calls `TeleportAsync` with `ShouldReserveServer = true` to a "private planet" place; (3) late-joining party members are routed via `GetPartyAsync` (which exposes the `ReservedServerAccessCode`) or an in-game party code (Dead Rails pattern), because the friends-list "Join" button cannot enter reserved servers; (4) persistent progress stays per-player in DataStores (shared across all places in the universe), while the run/planet instance itself is disposable.

- Progress model used by the hits: individual (each player's bases/inventory are theirs) with shared instance state only for the duration of the session. Shared ownership of a planet would be novel and unproven on Roblox; the sources show individual progress plus shared space.

## Key Question 5: What Roblox technical limits must a small team plan for (server size, DataStore, replication of creatures, cross-server visiting, place structure)?

### Takeaway
Plan for small servers (5–10 players, ≤100 if voice chat matters; 200 is the general cap and 700 is an unstable beta), one ~4 MB-max DataStore key per player written via UpdateAsync with session locking and a 500 MB + 1 MB/lifetime-user storage budget, client-side-rendered creatures driven by lightweight server state with StreamingEnabled on, and a hub/planet multi-place universe where reserved servers host private planets and `GetPartyAsync`/MessagingService (1 kB messages) coordinate cross-server visits.

### Cited Findings
**Server size**
- As of July 24, 2024 "all users can set the max player count per server to 200," while DevForum beta members may set 700; community guidance says 700-player servers "are very unstable" and even 300–400 "may still be unstable" (via search summary) — [DevForum: Max Player Count Increased (in Beta)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/max-player-count-increased-in-beta/722045); [DevForum: Server Player Limit](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/server-player-limit/2800295)
- Voice Chat "is only available for places that support a maximum of 100 players" and "is enabled by default for verified 13+ users on all new games with a maximum of 100 players"; "Games with voice often see an uplift in engagement, DAU, and spending" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Voice chat](https://create.roblox.com/docs/chat/voice-chat)
- `Players.MaxPlayers` "can only be set through a specific place's settings on the Creator Dashboard"; `PreferredPlayers` is the matchmaker fill target below MaxPlayers — [Roblox Creator Docs: Players](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Players)
- Observed sizes: Grow a Garden 5 players/6 plots; Steal a Brainrot 8 bases; Pet Simulator 99 50 public / 35 private; Dead Rails parties 1–10 (sources in Q1)
- Roblox began dynamically allocating server CPU "based on the real-time number of players," rolling out November 10–12, 2025, raising baseline CPU and adjusting as players join/leave (via search summary) — [DevForum: Weekly Recap November 10–14, 2025](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/weekly-recap-november-10-14-2025/4071175)

**DataStore limits and saving bases**
- Per-key value limit 4,194,304 characters (4 MB); key/datastore/scope names ≤50 chars; metadata value ≤250 chars; experience-level request limits per minute: Read 300 + 40×CCU, Write 300 + 20×CCU, List 300 + 2×CCU, Remove 300 + 40×CCU; per-key throughput 25 MB read / 4 MB write per minute; max 30 queued requests per queue — [Roblox Creator Docs: Data store error codes and limits](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/error-codes-and-limits)
- Storage limit: "Total latest version storage limit = 500 MB + 1 MB × lifetime user count," measured on compressed size of the latest version of each key; "A lifetime user is any user who has joined your game at least once"; do not pre-compress — [Roblox Creator Docs: Data store error codes and limits](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/error-codes-and-limits)
- DevForum announcement: limits became per-experience instead of per-server with more generous ceilings; access-limit rollout April 9–23 and storage limit activating April 9 (2025); "new limits taking effect in early 2026" (via search summary) — [DevForum: DataStores Access and Storage Updates](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/datastores-access-and-storage-updates/3597255)
- NEW: "Extended Services" lets creators buy capacity "beyond Roblox's default limits" for data stores (and memory stores); requires an ID-verified 18+ account in a supported country with a payment method — [Roblox Creator Docs: Extended Services](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/extended-services); [DevForum: Extended Services for Data Stores is now available](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/extended-services-for-data-stores-is-now-available/4504347)
- Best practices: "Use a small, fixed set of data stores"; "Store the persistent data for each player under one key whenever the data fits within the 4 MB object size limit"; buffer in memory and save "periodically, when the player leaves, when the server shuts down, and at critical checkpoints" (sample uses 180 s); stagger requests with jitter; "Prefer UpdateAsync over SetAsync"; shard hot keys read by every server (example: an "Admin Abuse" event's Weather key); only the latest version counts toward storage; deleted stores have a 30-day restore buffer — [Roblox Creator Docs: Best practices for data stores](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/best-practices)
- Session locking: without it "one server saves out-of-date information. This can lead to data loss and common item duplication loopholes"; the reference implementation writes a `LockId` into key metadata inside the same UpdateAsync and refuses access while another server's unexpired lock exists; final saves on `BindToClose` — [Roblox Creator Docs: Player data and purchasing sample](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/player-data-purchasing)
- Data stores "are consistent per game, so any place in a game can access and change the same data, including places on different servers" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Data stores](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores)
- Automated right-to-be-forgotten processing requires static key patterns like `User_{UserId}` — [Roblox Creator Docs: Best practices for data stores](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/best-practices)

**MemoryStore / MessagingService (cross-server coordination)**
- MemoryStore memory quota "64 KB + 1.2 KB × [number of users]" at game level (8-day traceback); request quota "1000 + 120 × [concurrent users]" request units/min; sorted map/queue max 1,000,000 items and 100 MB, values ≤32 KB, keys ≤128 chars, max TTL 45 days; per-partition throttling begins around 30,000 RU/min (sorted maps/queues live on one partition; hash maps shard); hash-map per-key ~5,000 writes / 15,000 reads per minute — [Roblox Creator Docs: Memory stores](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/memory-stores)
- MessagingService: message size 1 kB; messages sent per server 600 + 240×players per minute; received per topic (40 + 80×servers)/min; received for entire game (400 + 200×servers)/min; subscriptions per server 20 + 8×players; 240 subscribe requests/min — [Roblox Creator Docs: MessagingService](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/MessagingService)
- Cloud-service comparison: Data stores = permanent, cross-server (NoSQL); Memory stores = ≤45-day, cross-server (in-memory cache); all cloud services are server-script only — [Roblox Creator Docs: Choose between cloud services](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores-vs-memory-stores)

**Replication of many creatures**
- `Humanoid` "comes with a significant computation cost"; recommendations: "Play NPC animations on the client... In games with a large number of NPCs, consider creating the Animator on the client"; use `AnimationController` instead of Humanoid for static/moving NPCs with a custom movement controller; disable unused humanoid states; pool NPC models; "Only spawn NPCs when users are nearby"; avoid server-driven animations which "need to be simulated on the server and replicated to the client" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Improve performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve)
- Instance streaming is "enabled by default for new places created in Studio"; recommended `StreamingMinRadius` 64 and `StreamingTargetRadius` 1024, `StreamOutBehavior` Opportunistic, `ModelStreamingBehavior` Improved, `StreamingIntegrityMode` PauseOutsideLoadedArea; per-model `ModelStreamingMode` (Atomic/Persistent/PersistentPerPlayer); additional replication foci via `Player:AddReplicationFocus()` (too many foci risk client OOM); client physics "only occurs in streamed areas" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Instance streaming](https://create.roblox.com/docs/workspace/streaming)
- "Don't store everything in ReplicatedStorage. The client loads everything that is in this container." — [Roblox Creator Docs: Design for performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/design)
- The new Server Authority model (server as single source of truth with client prediction/rollback) requires `StreamingEnabled`, `NextGenerationReplication`, fixed simulation and deferred signals — [Roblox Creator Docs: Server authority model](https://create.roblox.com/docs/projects/server-authority)
- Community pet-system practice: handle pet movement on the client by iterating all players' equipped pets locally; represent each pet on the server as a tagged lightweight value/part (CollectionService) or send the equipped-pet list on join; firing RemoteEvents per heartbeat for pet movement "would quickly exceed the maximum number of remote events"; one developer kept hundreds of enemies at 60 FPS by keeping only values (e.g., CFrame) per entity on the server and letting clients create visuals (via search summary) — [DevForum: Syncing Equipped Pets for New Players](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/syncing-equipped-pets-for-new-players-performance-considerations/2857024); [DevForum: Best way to go about client replication?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/best-way-to-go-about-client-replication/4173285); [DevForum: Help with pet movement replicating](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-pet-movement-replicating-to-other-players/1081266)

**Place structure and cross-server visiting**
- A game is "made up of individual places, comparable to scenes in Unity or maps in Unreal Engine"; the start place is where players load first; non-start places are reached by teleport — [Roblox Creator Docs: Projects](https://create.roblox.com/docs/projects); [Roblox Creator Docs: Publish games and places](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/publish-games-and-places)
- Reserved servers: `ReserveServer(placeId)` returns an access code plus `PrivateServerId`; join via `TeleportOptions.ReservedServerAccessCode`; `TeleportOptions.ServerInstanceId` (a JobId) targets a specific public server; `GetPlayerPlaceInstanceAsync` locates a friend's public server but not reserved ones (details in Q4) — [Roblox Creator Docs: TeleportService](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/TeleportService); [Roblox Creator Docs: TeleportOptions](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/TeleportOptions)
- Cross-server chat is enabled by default for all games, groups servers "based on age, language, and occupancy," connects "about 200 users" per global channel, and is disabled in private servers — [Roblox Creator Docs: Cross-server chat](https://create.roblox.com/docs/chat/cross-server-chat)
- Custom Matchmaking became available to all experiences (Creator Hub "Custom Matchmaking" page) letting developers reweight signals or define their own; a Text Chat signal launched December 6, 2025 to match players who can chat together (via search summary) — [DevForum: Custom Matchmaking is now available to all experiences](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/custom-matchmaking-is-now-available-to-all-experiences-on-roblox/4051419); [DevForum: Updates to Matchmaking: Text Chat Signal](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/updates-to-matchmaking-introducing-the-new-text-chat-signal/4116099); matchmaking experiments can be run from Creator Hub Experiments (100% rollout recommended) — [Roblox Creator Docs: Experiments](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/experiments)
- `TextChatService:GetChatGroupsAsync(players)` / `VoiceChatService:GetVoiceGroupsAsync` return chat-group IDs so a server can, "for real-time use cases such as matchmaking and teleporting," keep players who are allowed to chat together (requires enabling "Chat & Voice Groups APIs" in Experience Settings → Communication) — [Roblox Creator Docs: TextChatService](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/TextChatService)

### Inferences
- Base saving: a planet base stored as a compact slot list (slot index → alien id, level, cosmetics) plus inventory fits comfortably in one <100 KB key per player, far below the 4 MB cap; at 1 MB per lifetime user the storage budget is effectively unlimited for slot-based bases, and Extended Services exists as a paid escape hatch. Free-form building (Bloxburg/LT2 style) is where keys balloon (LT2 exposes "Data Size" for that reason).
- Cross-server base visiting = load the target player's saved base key read-only on the visitor's server (DataStores are universe-wide), so an offline friend's planet can be toured; reads cost against 300 + 40×CCU/min and 25 MB/min per key, which is ample for occasional visits but means a popular player's base key should be cached in server memory, not re-read per visitor.
- Live "go to my friend's planet" = `GetPlayerPlaceInstanceAsync` → `TeleportAsync` with `ServerInstanceId` for public planets; for private planets use `GetPartyAsync` (party members only) or an in-game planet code stored in a MemoryStore hash map (code → access code, TTL hours). MessagingService's 1 kB/600+240n budget is fine for notifications ("X is visiting your planet") but not for state sync.
- Creatures: keep one small server-side record per alien (position/target/state as attributes or a tagged part) and render/animate on each client with `AnimationController` + local `Animator`; spawn only near players and let streaming unload distant bases. With 8 bases × 10–30 aliens = 80–240 creatures per server, server-side Humanoids would be the first thing to break.
- Server size: 6–10 players keeps every base visible and voice-eligible (≤100), and matches the proven GaG/SAB sizes. Reserve 1 social slot (the maximum allowed at <5 players; 20% rounded down otherwise) so friends can always join.

### Gaps
- No official numeric guidance exists on "instances per server" or bandwidth per client; Roblox's docs give qualitative advice only.
- The DevForum "DataStores Access and Storage Updates" post could not be read in full, so the exact 2026 limit changes referenced in the summary are unconfirmed beyond what the current docs show (which are the live formulas above).
- Whether `TeleportAsync` has a documented rate limit per server was not found in the docs clone.

## Key Question 6: How did Steal a Brainrot's light-PvP "stealing" change the social meta, and does a lighter version (visiting and borrowing aliens) make sense?

### Takeaway
Stealing turned a passive idle-collector into a live social drama: every neighbor is a threat and a target, base-lock timers create tension windows, and theft produces shareable emotional moments (TikTok "kids crying" clips) that fueled the fastest growth in Roblox history, while Grow a Garden showed a softer variant (pay-to-steal with public shaming) also works. A lighter "visit and borrow" mechanic preserves the social-interaction and content-creation benefits with less griefing/age-safety risk, but there is no Roblox precedent proving borrowing drives virality the way stealing did.

### Cited Findings
- Steal a Brainrot's steal rules: a base is "locked for 30 seconds once the player joins a server and can be locked for 60 seconds once the timer expires, being extended for 10 seconds every time the player rebirths (e.g., 80 seconds for two rebirths, 110 for five rebirths)"; the initial lock stays 30 s; "carrying a brainrot will notify the original owner, slow down the stealer and disable all of the stealer's items, making the stealer vulnerable to attacks from any other user. Attacking a stealer will teleport the brainrot back to its original base"; players defend with "defensive items and locking their base"; rebirthing "will consume all brainrots in the player's base" (via search summary) — [Roblox Wiki: Steal a Brainrot](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/BRAZILIAN_SPYDER/Steal_a_Brainrot); [Steal a Brainrot Wiki: Base](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Base)
- The tutorial itself teaches theft: after buying a first brainrot and earning cash, "the tutorial tells the player to steal brainrots from other players' bases" (via search summary) — [Roblox Wiki: Steal a Brainrot](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/BRAZILIAN_SPYDER/Steal_a_Brainrot)
- A player-maintained strategy wiki documents emergent meta such as the "Trap n' Swap" and "Overtrap" methods (placing traps where a target spawns, timing the base-lock expiry) — evidence of deep player-vs-player strategy layered on an idle game (via search summary) — [Steal a Brainrot Wiki: Methods Of Stealing](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Methods_Of_Stealing)
- Analyst framing: "The stealing mechanic isn't just a gimmick—it's the entire foundation of the Steal a Brainrot economy"; the loop is "buy → generate income → steal or be stolen from"; the game gained "tens of millions of views" on TikTok/YouTube "for stealing and strategy tips along with reactions of children crying after having their Brainrots stolen"; the characters' AI-meme origin gave it "a brand/pop-culture edge" (via search summary) — [Medium: Steal a Brainrot Phenomenon](https://medium.com/@emtertoszef/steal-a-brainrot-phenomenon-6af7b49081e0); [Free Systems: The Algorithm Behind Steal a Brainrot](https://freesystems.substack.com/p/the-algorithm-behind-steal-a-brainrot) (the latter also attributes retention to "intermittent reinforcement," mixing predictable progress with surprise bonuses)
- Steal a Brainrot hit 25.4M CCU in September 2025, the all-time record, ~4 months after launch (via search summary) — [Mi-3 (Dec 17, 2025)](https://www.mi-3.com.au/17-12-2025/roblox-garden-brainrot-games-set-new-records-2025-unprecedented-player-engagement)
- YouTube's 2025 year-in-review: "The biggest 'games' of the year were creator-produced Roblox experiences like Grow a Garden and Steal a Brainrot" — [Updates From YouTube on X](https://x.com/UpdatesFromYT/status/1995880230435901905); Roblox content has surpassed one trillion all-time YouTube views (via search summary) — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/roblox-content-surpasses-1-trillion-all-time-views-on-youtube/)
- Search-summary claim attributed to coverage around Naavik's State of UGC Games (2026): growth of Grow a Garden, Steal a Brainrot and 99 Nights "is led by YouTube and TikTok influencers, where a single video can catapult an experience to millions of concurrent users," and "One in every five game-related TikTok videos globally mentioned Roblox during July and August 2025" (origin of the one-in-five statistic not verified; page blocked) — [Naavik: The State of UGC Games (2026)](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/the-state-of-ugc-games-2026/)
- Grow a Garden's softer theft: walk up to another player's crop and the game prompts you to spend 37 Robux to take it; "The game will notify everyone in the server of your crimes, so expect to get side-eyed by other players"; free private servers are the recommended protection (via search summary) — [Droid Gamers: Grow a Garden Stealing](https://www.droidgamers.com/guides/grow-a-garden-stealing/); [Grow a Garden Wiki: Stealing](https://growagarden2.fandom.com/wiki/Stealing)
- Parent-facing coverage frames Steal a Brainrot as emotionally intense for young children (theft, crying reactions, spending pressure) — [Inclusive Teach: What is "Brainrot" in Roblox? A Parents Guide (Sept 28, 2025)](https://inclusiveteach.com/2025/09/28/what-is-brainrot-in-roblox/); [UT Daily Beacon opinion](https://utdailybeacon.com/206768/opinion/opinion-what-steal-a-brainrot-means-for-the-future-of-youth-entertainment/)
- Roblox's design doc on streamers: "Consider how your game might work in a streaming context... could a streamer make great, fun content with it? Can they easily involve their friends, or other streamers?" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Design for Roblox](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/design-for-roblox)
- Roblox's user-base doc: "Engaging games will generate a lot of organic traffic through word of mouth... - Adopt Me's house model (lock to family/friends) and Bloxburg's invite/neighbor model are the non-PvP visiting precedents (Q2 sources).

### Inferences
- What stealing changed: (a) every other player on the server became content (a threat, a target, a rescuer who can attack the thief), which is exactly Roblox's "players as evergreen content for each other" thesis; (b) timers (30 s join lock, 60 s+ relock, extended by rebirth) create scheduled tension windows that reward staying online and checking in; (c) loss aversion produced the emotionally charged clips that TikTok/YouTube amplified; (d) the thief penalties (slow, no items, owner alerted, anyone can hit you) keep theft risky and reversible, which is why it reads as "light PvP" rather than griefing.
- Grow a Garden's variant shows the dial can be turned down (paid, public, rare) and still function as a social hook, while private servers absorb players who hate it.
- A "visit and borrow" system for an alien collection game preserves most of the upside with lower risk: visitors can enter a friend's planet (Adopt Me-style lock levels: friends / party / anyone), "borrow" an alien for a timed window (owner keeps ownership, earns a cut or a bond bonus), and the owner gets a notification and a visitor log — this keeps the "someone touched my stuff" emotional ping and gives creators a reason to visit each other's planets on camera. To recover the tension that made stealing viral, consider an opt-in "raid" or "poach" mode on public planets with SAB-style guardrails (lock timers, thief debuffs, recoverable aliens), rather than making theft the default for all players.
- Age-safety context matters more in 2026 than in mid-2025: with chat segmented by age group (Q7), low-chat social mechanics (visiting, borrowing, emotes, notifications) do more of the social work than text chat can.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how much of Steal a Brainrot's growth came from the steal mechanic vs. the brainrot meme IP vs. creator marketing; the attribution above is analysts' qualitative judgment.
- No Roblox game with a "borrow/lend" mechanic was found in the accessible sources, so the lighter version is an inference from Adopt Me/Bloxburg visiting plus SAB/GaG stealing, not a proven pattern.
- Steal a Brainrot's own retention curve and whether theft-driven churn (players quitting after being robbed) is material were not available.

## Key Question 7 (platform context): Which 2025–2026 Roblox social-feature and policy changes should the team design around?

### Takeaway
Since late 2024 Roblox has shipped Party (≤6) and the Party API, renamed Groups to Communities, made Custom Matchmaking GA, enabled cross-server chat by default, and — most consequentially — required facial-age-estimation checks for all chat with age-segmented chat groups (global from January 2026), followed by "Trusted Connections/Trusted Friends" for cross-age friends and a rename of Connections back to Friends; Roblox Connect calling APIs sunset July 15, 2026. Design social features that work without chat across age bands and use the new chat-group APIs when grouping players.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- A planet shared by an under-9 and a 16-year-old may be a server where those two cannot text or voice chat at all; social design must therefore carry through non-chat channels (visiting, borrowing, emotes, gifts, notifications, visible bases). When forming co-op planets or filling servers, call `GetChatGroupsAsync` so squads can actually talk, or rely on Roblox's chat-aware matchmaking signal.
- Party-based planet joins are friends-only by platform design, which is safe for young audiences; treat the Party as the unit for private planets and "Trusted Friends" as the natural scope for base-entry permissions.
- Expect continued churn in naming (Connections ↔ Friends) and chat rules through 2026; avoid hardcoding assumptions about who can chat.
