## Key question 1: What does a step-by-step "model a cute Roblox pet in Blender" tutorial actually do, in order, and which steps make it read as one fluid creature rather than assembled shapes?

### Takeaway
Every Roblox pet tutorial found starts from one primitive (a cube) and reaches the final form by loop cuts, bevels or subdivision and by extruding limbs and tails *out of* the body, not by placing separate primitives; the "simulator pet" style (Pet Simulator lineage) deliberately keeps a few separate beveled-cube parts (paws, ears) but holds them to one rule (same bevel, same smooth shading, one palette), while the "cute organic" style (Adopt Me lineage) uses one subdivided cage with spheres only for eyes. Fluidity comes from (a) continuous geometry (extrude, not place), (b) bevel or Subdivision Surface applied before export, (c) Shade Smooth over the whole body, and (d) a single material or palette texture so joins have no colour seam.

### Cited Findings

**The canonical written tutorial (simulator style, Pet Simulator reference).**
- "Creating Simulator-Styled Pets in Blender" (DevForum Community Tutorials, thread 517174; the search index gives its age as about 2,379 days, so roughly spring 2020; uses an older Blender). Order of steps as the snippets give them: getting started (remove the default lamp and camera), shaping the body, beveling, paws, ears, tail, then exporting and final touches. Body: start from a cube; "Loop cut your model and roll your mouse twice"; shape with loop cuts and proportional editing, which "you can skip for a plain rounded-square look." Bevel: "Select all the edges along the sides of the cube but don't select the cuts from the loop cut. Once you have them all selected, press Ctrl + B to bevel. I beveled mine .2 (Type .2) and I did 6 segments." Paws: a scaled, beveled cube duplicated with Shift+D. Tail: extrusions then a bevel, then faces set to smooth. The reference game named is Pet Simulator 2. A commenter reported the curved tail "caused weird shading on the geometry, and more subdivisions fixed it"; another suggested the Bevel modifier as the more intuitive route. (snippet) — [DevForum 517174](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/creating-simulator-styled-pets-in-blender/517174); [search summary of the bevel steps](https://blenderartists.org/t/how-would-i-bevel-a-loop-cut/1255971)
- "How to make Pets in Blender" (DevForum Community Tutorials, thread 1468850, 2021) is a hotkey-driven walkthrough: extrude, loop cut, UV unwrap. (snippet) — [DevForum 1468850](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-pets-in-blender/1468850)
- "How are modern simulator pets made?" (DevForum Building Support, thread 2882620): the asker wanted low-poly pets like Lucky Block Simulator's and was "stuck on texturing and faces"; the answer given: model ("sculpt") in Blender or Maya, paint textures in an image editor (Photoshop), optionally finish in Substance 3D Painter "for extra lighting and detail"; pull reference pets from the Creator Store Toolbox and export them as `.obj` to study; export the final as `.fbx` for animation; in Blender's Material panel set Base Color to an Image Texture. (snippet) — [DevForum 2882620](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-are-modern-simulator-pets-made/2882620)
- Faces in the simulator style are decals or image textures, not geometry: a DevForum "Block pet modelers" thread (2019-era) describes using face decals "to get the best possible look", and a pet pack on itch reports that "some pets don't include the face texture PNG, while others do" on export. (snippet) — [DevForum 405839](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/block-pet-modelers/405839); [tmats pet pack](https://tmats.itch.io/tmats-pet-pack)

**The organic "cute" method (one cage, subdivision, spheres for eyes).**
- Creative Bloq, "Here's how to model a cute cat (or anything else) in Blender in under 4 minutes" (Maciek Lazowski; about 16 months old at search time, so mid-2025): body from a cube with a Subdivision Surface modifier; eyes and nose are spheres with a Mirror modifier on the Y axis, then smoothed; the article frames this as "box modelling basics", turning a cube into a cat. (snippet) — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/heres-how-to-model-a-cute-cat-or-anything-else-in-blender-in-just-a-few-easy-steps)
- Jay Grewall's course (listed on BlenderNation): start from the default cube, use Inset and Extrude to add arms, hands, legs and feet, then apply Subdivision Surface "to create geometry and a smooth appearance"; the creature is a baby alien. (snippet) — [BlenderNation listing](https://bazaar.blendernation.com/?p=16979)
- Grant Abbitt (Gabbitt Media / GameDev.tv "Low Poly Characters"): a simple base mesh "that defines the silhouette", selective added detail, then decimation "to keep a consistent low-poly look"; the course covers texture grids, basic rigs and Mixamo animation. (snippet) — [GameDev.tv course](https://gamedev.tv/courses/blender-low-poly-characters); [workflow listing](https://bazaar.blendernation.com/listing/detailed-low-poly-characters-my-workflow-essential-tips-blender/)

Roblox's own Blender page covers "subdividing edges and faces to create smooth curves." (snippet) — [School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/your-first-blender-character); [ARwall 2025](https://arwall.co/blogs/arwall-blogs/how-to-model-in-blender); [Roblox docs: Blender](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/blender)

(snippet) — [ArtStation a03lrz](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/a03lrz); [nhance-school](https://nhance-school.com/courses/blender-roblox-character-art)

**Colour without seams: palette textures and vertex colours.**
- The one-material palette method (Imphenzia's PixPal, jayanam): "one material for all models to avoid draw calls", a texture of colour squares, and the UV islands of the unwrapped model moved onto the squares. Imphenzia's PixPal palette texture is CC0 (v1.3 adds Blender 5 and current engine projects). A forum user found 1 px per colour too low and 32x32 px per colour fine; the UV-Colorize add-on automates the shift and warns the collapsed UVs break light baking. (snippet) — [PixPal thread](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/imphenzia-pixpal-palette-texture-pbr-node-material-example-updated-to-version-1-2/56066); [jayanam on BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2017/10/25/low-poly-blender/); [UV-Colorize](https://github.com/RouBroeck/UV-Colorize)
- Roblox staff tutorial "Vertex Colored Meshes using Blender and Studio" (DevForum 3050119): vertex colour is RGB per vertex; colour "can only be added where a vertex exists, so a low-poly object like a cube gives you little detail compared with a sphere"; painting, filling and "using geometry to refine or feather the color transitions"; replies add that vertex-painted meshes must be exported as `.fbx` for the colours to come through, that vertex paint can act as shading while the MeshPart's colour stays changeable, and that it "probably only works on MeshParts". (snippet) — [DevForum 3050119](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/3050119)
- Vertex colours and SurfaceAppearance do not mix: an October 2025 feature request states vertex colouring "only works on Roblox materials and has no effect on surface appearance rendering"; Roblox's reply elsewhere says Overlay AlphaMode is "mainly meant for clothing over body colors" and in Overlay mode vertex colours "take over the tinting of the masked pixels". (snippet) — [Feature request 3981469](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/vertex-coloring-support-for-surfaceappearance/3981469); [Bug 985602](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/surfaceappearence-colormap-does-not-respect-baked-vertex-colors/985602)
- Outlines: there is no toon shader in Roblox; a single `Highlight` on a grouped model gives a black outline through walls; the inverted-hull (enlarged black duplicate) method is reported "very glitchy", with parts vanishing close to the camera and "see-through areas on the special mesh". (snippet) — [DevForum 1851704](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/adding-a-toon-shader/1851704); [DevForum 3972375](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/a-outline-effect-without-the-need-of-blender/3972375)

**Why a creature reads as cute (the proportion rules the tutorials assume).**
- Lorenz's 1943 baby schema: "big heads, huge eyes set low on the face, round cheeks, tiny noses, soft limbs"; design guides put the eyes "much lower, sometimes at the bottom third of the head", about one to one-and-a-half eye-widths apart, and warn that detail works against cuteness ("Hello Kitty doesn't even have a mouth"). A SUNY honours thesis on stuffed-animal eye size found the eye-size effect "not statistically significant", so exact eye-size numbers are heuristics. (snippet) — [Psychology Today, Feb 2026](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/lies-and-deception/202602/why-we-think-babies-are-so-cute); [Tuts+ elements of cute character design](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/the-elements-of-cute-character-design--vector-3533); [SUNY thesis](https://soar.suny.edu/entities/publication/68ab90cc-169b-4363-82d0-aa12c3de0c55)
- Uplift's rule for Adopt Me pets: "boil each pet down to its most distinguishing feature and then enhance and exaggerate it" (camel hump, giraffe neck); "cup feet and big eyes" are the traits their style guides preserve. (snippet, repo for the first half) — [Brands Untapped](https://www.brandsuntapped.com/uplift-games-vicky-huxley-and-sim-furniss-on-adopt-mes-journey-into-consumer-products/); [Mojo Nation](https://www.mojo-nation.com/uplift-games-vicky-huxley-and-sim-furniss-on-adopt-mes-journey-into-consumer-products/)

### Inferences
- The two Roblox pet styles are built differently and should not be mixed in one roster. Simulator style (PS99 lineage): a beveled cube body, duplicated beveled-cube paws and ears, a decal face, one flat colour per part. Organic style (Adopt Me lineage): one subdivided cage with limbs extruded from the body, sphere eyes, a hand-painted or palette texture. Our roster's grammar (round body, big eyes, short limbs, one bold colour) is the organic style, so the tutorial steps that matter are: one cage, extrude limbs, Subdivision applied, Shade Smooth, one material.
- The assembled look is tolerated in the simulator style because every part obeys one rule (same 0.2/6-segment bevel, same smooth shading, same palette) and the parts are few and chunky; it is not tolerated when parts are many, thin and intersecting, which is our case (repo).
- The tutorials never model the face: eyes are spheres or decals added after the body is done, which matches the repo's "place the face after the melt" step.
- A palette texture (32 px squares, one material) is the cheapest seam-free colouring that also survives Roblox's SurfaceAppearance rules; vertex colours work on plain MeshParts only and need FBX. This is the fallback the repo note already names, now with the numbers.

## Key question 2: How do the top studios describe their creature pipeline (interviews, job posts, RDC talks, ArtStation, DevForum)?

### Takeaway
The only studio that documents anything is Uplift Games (Adopt Me): in-house senior 3D artists design and model pets, job posts ask for C4D/Blender/Maya plus Substance, PBR, "both high and low poly art styles", a technical animator builds skeletal assets, and the art director's design rule is "one exaggerated distinguishing feature". BIG Games, Splitting Point and Rumble publish nothing about pet production; Fisch's models were made by its solo developer; Steal a Brainrot's characters come from AI-generated meme images, which is now a public copyright question. No RDC talk on pet or creature art was found.

## Key question 3: Concrete numbers: triangles per pet, texture size, number of materials, variants per base mesh (plus how rarity shows in the model, and animation)

### Takeaway
No studio publishes its budgets. Community and vendor figures converge on a few hundred to about 2,000 triangles per simulator pet (2 to 5k in one vendor claim), one 512 or 1024 px texture (Roblox's own table: 256 px for 5-stud objects, 512 for 10, 1024 for 20), one material per pet or one shared palette, and three or four recolour variants of the same mesh per rarity system (Normal, Golden, Rainbow, Shiny in PS99; Normal, Neon, Mega Neon in Adopt Me; Shiny, Golden, Rainbow plus weight-based size in Grow a Garden; one of about ten mutations at a time in Steal a Brainrot).

### Cited Findings

**Triangles.**
- Roblox limit: "Individual meshes can not exceed 20,000 triangles" (repo, read from creator-docs). A 2026 DevForum feature request, "Increase the max triangle limit", shows the cap is still an active complaint. (snippet) — [DevForum 4650221](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/increase-the-max-triangle-limit/4650221)
- DevForum guidance: keep character models "below about 2K triangles, with the overall limit roughly 10K"; small props 50 to 500; for a low-poly look one developer keeps "well below 1000 across all parts combined"; total scene count matters more than any one mesh, and MeshParts are instanced so repeated copies are cheap. Older threads recall the per-MeshPart limit rising from 5k to 10k before the 20k figure. (snippet) — [DevForum 197600](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/what-is-a-good-triangle-count-for-a-mesh/197600); [DevForum 868821](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-many-polygonstriangles-should-i-have-on-my-models/868821); [DevForum 1529723](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/reasonable-amount-of-triangles-for-models/1529723)
- A low-poly dog and pig thread: the dog "had many triangles because it was built before they learned to make spheres with fewer faces", i.e. multi-part pets add up fast. (snippet) — [DevForum 884002](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-low-poly-dog-and-pig/884002)
- Vendor claims: Tripo3D "2–5k tris per pet" for PS99-style models; Meshy's Roblox page markets remeshing "to Roblox's 2K–10K triangle budgets"; Nilo reports raw AI output "often exceeds 50,000 triangles" against the 20,000 cap. (snippet) — [Tripo3D](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/pet-simulator-99-3d-model); [Meshy Roblox page](https://www.meshy.ai/use-cases/free-game-assets/roblox-developers); [Nilo](https://nilo.io/articles/ai-generated-roblox-assets-tutorial)
- Roblox's own generator default: Assistant mesh generation (March 2026) defaults to a 10,000 maximum, and the announcement suggests "100s to 1K" for small or simple objects (see KQ4).

**Textures.**
- Roblox texture specifications: 4096 maximum; the engine streams low-quality versions first; sizing guidance "256×256 for roughly 5-stud objects, 512×512 for about 10-stud objects, and 1024×1024 for about 20-stud objects". (snippet, official page) — [Roblox docs: texture specifications](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/art/modeling/texture-specifications)
- In practice uploads wider than 1024 on a side are downscaled ("a 1536×512 sheet was reduced to 1024×341"); on 2 to 3 GB phones mesh textures can stay blurry; developers split models into separate meshes to keep texture quality. (snippet) — [DevForum 30343](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/uploading-4k-images/30343/55); [DevForum 4087719](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/mesh-texture-is-blurry-although-the-same-image-is-loaded-in-full-resolution-in-gui/4087719)
- The repo already records 1024 px for anything on a character and the SurfaceAppearance map set (repo).

**Materials and draw calls.**
- One material per model, or one palette material shared by all models "to avoid draw calls", is the low-poly convention (jayanam, Imphenzia; see KQ1). A DevForum cost thread: textures and vertex colours are "roughly equal in cost", and "if all of your models use the same color palette, you can do this with just one texture". SurfaceAppearance's `Color` lets one colour map be reused with different tints (repo). (snippet) — [DevForum 1770744](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-vertex-painting-better-than-textures/1770744)
- Recolouring a textured pet without a new texture: material variants "can be recolored by changing the part's color and they have all the same maps that a SurfaceAppearance does". (snippet) — [DevForum 854063](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/surfaceappearancecolor/854063)

**Variants per base mesh and how rarity is shown in the model.**
- Pet Simulator 99: Golden pets "look exactly like the pets you exchanged but will be coated in gold" (10 of the same pet at the Gold machine); Rainbow pets (10 goldens at the Rainbow machine, 100 base copies in total) "glow with rainbow colors"; Shiny is a hatch variant (Shiny Relics raise its odds); Huge pets are "the second strongest type ... right behind Titanic" and can themselves be Shiny, Gold or Rainbow; since update 61 (May 2025) eggs can transform into Golden, Rainbow or Shiny. So one base mesh carries four surface variants and two size classes. (snippet) — [progameguides: golden](https://progameguides.com/roblox/how-to-make-golden-pets-in-pet-simulator-99-roblox/); [Twinfinite: rainbow](https://twinfinite.net/guides/rainbow-pets-pet-simulator-99/); [BIG Games update 61](https://www.biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-99-update-61); [progameguides: shiny relics](https://progameguides.com/roblox/what-do-shiny-relics-do-in-pet-simulator-99-roblox/)

(snippet, per-pet wiki pages) — [Adopt Me wiki: Lynx](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Lynx); [Aye Aye](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Aye_Aye); [Mini Pig](https://breezewiki.discard.no/adoptme/wiki/Mini_Pig); [Shark Puppy](https://breezewiki.discard.no/adoptme/wiki/Shark_Puppy); [Swordfish](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Swordfish)
- Grow a Garden: pet mutations Shiny (about 31 to 32 percent, a flat XP bonus), Golden (about 6 percent), Rainbow (about 3 percent, the best passive boost); in Grow a Garden 2 a pet spawning during the Rainbow Moon can turn Rainbow at 2 percent, and size tiers Normal, Big and Mega scale the boost (x2.25, x3.5, x6 for the Rainbow Golden Dragonfly). Pet size is driven by weight: a fan calculator's hatch-weight classes run Small (under 1 kg), Normal, Large, Semi Huge, Huge (5 to 6.9 kg), Semi Titanic, Titanic, Godly, Colossal (10 kg+), and the class "sets starting stats, growth rate, maximum size". Fan-maintained numbers; sources disagree on exact percentages. (snippet) — [esports.gg](https://esports.gg/guides/roblox/grow-a-garden-a-complete-pet-mutations-guide/); [GINX tier list](https://www.ginx.tv/en/grow-a-garden-pet-mutations-tier-list); [GAG2 wiki: Rainbow Dragonfly](https://growagardencalculator.com/grow-a-garden-2/wiki/pets/rainbowdragonfly); [pet weight calculator](https://www.growagardencalculator.ca/pet-weight-calculator)

**Animation patterns (idle bob, follow, hatch).**
- Idle bob is a sine offset on the CFrame; the amplitude "is the vertical distance between each trough and crest", so the pet must be raised by half the amplitude or it dips into the floor; a drift that snaps back at its limit reads as "rises slowly but drops fast" and should step down gradually. Following is done in a RunService loop; threads ask how to make the pet "idle on the floor properly, instead of still floating" and keep a consistent side of the player; the alternative is to parent a rigged pet to the character and play animations; a community follow module is recommended for simulator-style pets. (snippet) — [DevForum 1164331](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-can-i-fix-my-bobbing-script/1164331); [DevForum 1099749](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-can-i-fix-this-script/1099749); [DevForum 583373](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/optimising-pet-follow-script/583373)
- Egg hatch: pin the egg in front of the camera each frame from the camera's render CFrame (or use a ViewportFrame, which cannot show ParticleEmitters); shake with a sine function whose angle grows over time, applied through `CFrame.Angles`; a tilt-and-spin reveal, repeated per egg for 3x hatches; a £2 itch "Egg Hatching System" `.rbxm` has tap-to-hatch, a smooth shake and a slide-away reveal. (snippet) — [DevForum 2542547](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/egg-hatching-animation/2542547); [DevForum 266417](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/increasing-a-tween-value/266417); [DevForum 2876100](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-egg-open-animation/2876100); [itch Egg Hatching System](https://aidenshop.itch.io/egghatchsystem)
- The reveal beats the references use (multi-tap crack, shards, silhouette in a ring, a card with "N% chance") are in the repo (ZPLSaNotv).
- Adopt Me's technical animator role builds "skeletal assets" and verifies "animation quality in-engine" (KQ2), so its pets are rigged and keyframed, not tweened. (snippet)

### Inferences
- A workable budget for a cute collection pet on phones, from the convergence above: 1,000 to 2,500 triangles per pet including face parts, one 512 px texture or a shared palette, one body material plus at most an eye and a glow slot, and every rarity variant as a material or colour swap of the same mesh (gold, rainbow cycle, emissive regions) or a uniform scale (Huge, Big/Mega), never a new mesh. This matches the repo's 1,800 to 2,500 targets and its tier table.
- Adopt Me's per-species Neon regions imply the body is split into parts or material slots at those regions (tail, ears, spots), so a "glow zone" must be planned into the mesh, not added later; our melt pipeline's "kept parts" for glow are the same idea.
- Size as a rarity signal (PS99 Huge/Titanic, Grow a Garden weight classes) is universal and cheap; it needs only a scale and a stronger idle bob.

### Gaps
- No studio-published triangle count, texture size or material count for any of the named games; the 2 to 5k figure is a vendor's, and the DevForum numbers are community opinion.
- Shiny visuals in PS99 and Grow a Garden (sparkle particles? a sheen?) were not described by any source found.

## Key question 4: Which AI tools, if any, do successful Roblox devs actually use for creature models in 2026, and what do they say about the results?

### Takeaway
By October 2026 Roblox itself ships three generators inside Studio (Assistant mesh generation from text with textures, a Texture Generator, and segmentation/restyle of imported meshes) and has open-sourced Cube 3D and CubePart; third-party Meshy, Tripo and 3D AI Studio are what the TikTok creators and small devs actually name; the one top game whose creatures are AI-derived is Steal a Brainrot, and its AI origin is now a copyright liability. No top studio says it uses AI for pet meshes; Roblox's own materials pitch the tools for props, blockouts and parts, and users report dense, uneven output that still needs cleanup.

### Cited Findings

**Roblox's own tools (first party).**
- Cube 3D (read, GitHub `Roblox/cube` README): announced March 2025 as "a shape tokenizer and a text-to-shape generation model"; open weights; output is an `.obj` (shape only, no texture) from `python -m cube3d.generate --prompt ...`; v0.5 (July 2025) adds "higher fidelity 3D compositions and bounding box conditioning", was "trained on an additional ~2.8 million synthetic 3D assets", and the README admits "the model sometimes struggles when the bounding box is too extreme for a given prompt, for example the cat, where it can produce disconnected components or generates it along a diagonal"; macOS runs through MPS but `--fast-inference` "will not work on MacOS"; Blender 4.3+ is used only to render turntable GIFs. — [Roblox/cube README](https://github.com/Roblox/cube/blob/main/README.md)
- CubePart (read, May 2026, `Roblox/cube/cubepart`): "an open-vocabulary, part-controllable 3D generator": given a mesh (or text prompt) and "a user-defined parts schema (an open-ended list of part names)", it "synthesizes a set of meshes, one per schema element, that assemble into a coherent object", and the README claims the assets "can be directly integrated into game engines and driven by animation, physics, and behavior scripts without manual post-processing"; weights are an 8.6 GB multi-part DiT plus a 1.3 GB shape VAE (so not runnable on the 8 GB Mac). — [CubePart README](https://github.com/Roblox/cube/blob/main/cubepart/README.md); [Roblox newsroom, May 2026](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/05/cubepart-roblox-open-vocabulary-part-controllable-3d-generator)
- Studio Assistant mesh generation (DevForum announcement, 19 March 2026): generate "a textured 3D mesh from a text prompt in seconds" through `GenerateModelAsync`; "the max triangle count is optional and defaults to 10000. Lower values give more faceted and low-poly generations"; the post suggests "100s to 1K" for small or simple objects; the same release adds `generate_mesh` and `generate_material` tools to Studio's MCP server for external clients (named: BloxBot, Claude Code, Cursor). (snippet) — [DevForum 4527258](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/assistant-updates-mesh-generation-new-mcp-server-tools-screenshot-tool-and-more/4527258); [BloxBot guide](https://www.bloxbot.ai/guide/roblox-studio-mesh-generation-mcp-tools)
- Texture and segmentation update (DevForum, September 2026; BloxBot dates it 23 September): revamped texture generation that can "restyle one or more meshes", segmentation extended to imported meshes, image previews for generated meshes and procedural models; a Hint Image "skips preview generation and creates the final model directly". (snippet) — [DevForum 4890084](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introducing-new-texture-generation-tools-segment-any-mesh-and-image-previews/4890084); [BloxBot](https://bloxbot.ai/guide/roblox-ai-texture-generation-mesh-segmentation-2026)
- Texture Generator (read, creator-docs, beta): select a MeshPart or a Model of MeshParts, set a "generation angle" that "highlights the significant features or surfaces", optionally upload an Art Style image with a Strength value "to influence the overall style, color palette, and mood", prompt with weighting syntax (`pirate++`, `futuristic--`, `(robotic)1.5`); the doc warns that for a Model "the meshes should be arranged in a sensible way such that a unified body is formed" and that the tool suits "custom 3D assets where the desired texture is contextual to the asset itself" (Material Generator for tiling surfaces). — [creator-docs texture-generator.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/studio/texture-generator.md)
- Solid modelling on MeshParts (Studio beta, July 2026): Union, Intersect and Subtract now work on watertight MeshParts, but "textures from the tools are discarded and only the geometry and vertex colors are preserved". (snippet) — [DevForum 4515374](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/studio-beta-solid-modeling-on-meshes-new-fragment-and-sweep-apis/4515374)
- Secondary descriptions disagree on whether Cube-in-Studio output is textured: Meshy's pages call it "convenient for quick blockout shapes, but it outputs untextured geometry"; a Revix tutorial says it returns MeshParts with geometry and textures when beta settings are on. The March 2026 announcement settles it for the Assistant path: textured. (snippet) — [Meshy Roblox page](https://www.meshy.ai/use-cases/free-game-assets/roblox-developers); [Revix](https://revix.tech/blog/how-to-generate-3d-models-for-roblox-studio-with-ai)

### Inferences
- The 2026 first-party route for a two-person team is the Studio Assistant (textured mesh, triangle cap settable to about 1k) plus Texture Generator for restyling, reachable from Claude Code through the Studio MCP `generate_mesh` tool we already have installed. ## Key question 5: What makes the "AI-made" look ("a bunch of shapes mashed together") and what fixes it?

### Takeaway
Two different failures get the same label. Generator output fails as "melted": under-described features smoothed away, dense uneven triangles, bad normals, one baked texture on throwaway UVs. Code-built or part-built creatures fail as "mashed": separate primitives that intersect and keep their own silhouettes, with no shared rule and no continuous skin. The fixes are different: specific prompts, multi-view input and retopology for the first; one continuous mesh (extrude, bevel or subdivide, smooth, one material), one exaggerated hero feature, baby-schema proportions, and a finishing coat (glow regions, ring, particles, animation) for the second.

### Cited Findings

**Generator artefacts, as described by the tools and by users.**
- Meshy's own text-to-3D guide: "Melted/blobby details — under-described features get smoothed"; fixes: "Regenerate with a simpler prompt, use multi-view inputs for better structure, or apply retopology before you continue." (snippet) — [Meshy text-to-3D](https://www.meshy.ai/features/text-to-3d)
- A Blender cleanup guide (2026): AI meshes "typically arrive as dense triangulated geometry with irregular edge flow, overlapping faces, or no UV layout at all"; the cleanup order is non-manifold geometry, ngons, normals and UVs, then retopologise and export. A DEV Community post adds "inconsistent or inverted normals, smoothing artifacts (sometimes the normals, sometimes custom split normal data the generator wrote), and throwaway UVs that exist only to carry the one baked texture". (snippet) — [visiomake](https://visiomake.com/en/blog/fix-ai-generated-3d-model-topology-blender); [dev.to](https://dev.to/mileswill/how-to-fix-a-broken-glb-from-an-ai-3d-generator-8bn)
- Nilo: raw output "often exceeds 50,000 triangles" and "frequently contain[s] poor edge flow and non-manifold edges". vsquad: AI output "lacks wear, dirt, roughness, normal depth, or surface history" and suits stylised props better than characters. (snippet) — [Nilo tutorial](https://nilo.io/articles/ai-generated-roblox-assets-tutorial); [Nilo retopology](https://nilo.io/articles/roblox-ai-retopology-tool); [vsquad](https://vsquad.art/blog/articles/best-ai-3d-generators/)
- Roblox's Cube v0.5 README documents the opposite artefact, disconnected components, when the bounding box fights the prompt (read; KQ4).
- The reference creators' own verdicts on their Claude Design creatures: "there are still some imperfections", "the water animations are kind of janky", and the GLBs arrive as "gray silhouettes" with no colour or animation (repo).

**Why part-built creatures read as mashed, and what the tutorials do instead.**
- The repo's diagnosis: every Body step is a separate primitive that intersects its neighbours, so there is a crease at every join; smooth shading and bevels cannot remove an intersection; only a union of the volumes can (repo).
- Every tutorial in KQ1 grows limbs, ears and tails out of one cube by extrusion or keeps a few chunky beveled parts under one rule; the DevForum simulator tutorial's "weird shading" on the curved tail was fixed by more subdivisions, and the "cute cat" method adds only the eyes and nose as separate spheres (snippet; KQ1 sources).
- Roblox's Texture Generator doc states the same requirement from the texturing side: a Model's meshes "should be arranged in a sensible way such that a unified body is formed" or the texture will not read as one object (read; KQ4).
- Uplift's style rule (one exaggerated distinguishing feature; cup feet and big eyes kept) and the baby schema (big head, large eyes low on the face, tiny nose, simplicity) are the design-side fixes; a UI artist on PS99 names readability "at small sizes" as the test (snippet; KQ1 and KQ2 sources).
- The finishing coat that rescues the references' creatures is emissive accents, a floor ring, orbiting particles and animation; Adopt Me's Neon regions and the mutation colour systems of PS99, Grow a Garden and Steal a Brainrot are the shipped versions of that coat (repo; KQ3 sources).

### Inferences
- For generated meshes the fix is a pipeline, not a prompt: decimate or QuadriFlow to budget, fix normals, re-unwrap to a palette or 512 px bake, re-place the face, and only then compare side by side with the hand-built roster (repo's AI route).
- For our part-built species the fix is structural: one connected skin (metaball melt, Skin modifier or a subdivided cage), chunky limbs at least a quarter of the body width, the face placed on the finished skin, one material, and glow planned as a region. The three render checks in the repo (64 px silhouette, clay seam check, 128 px face) are the measurable version of "reads as one creature".
- "AI-made" as a viewer's judgement is triggered by three cues visible at phone size: visible intersections, uneven surface detail (smooth here, dense there) and a lighting-baked texture that fights the scene light. Flat or palette colour with Roblox's own lighting removes the third cue outright.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison (same creature made by hand, by generator, by code) exists in any source found; the artefact lists are vendor and practitioner descriptions.
- No source quantifies how much of a creature's cute reading comes from the mesh versus the finishing coat; the evidence is the references' before/after and the per-game variant systems.
