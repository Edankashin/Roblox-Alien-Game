## What exactly makes the top games' UI look "professional" at a glance, and which of those traits are cheap to copy in code?

### Takeaway
The look that reads as "pro" on Roblox in 2026 is a small, consistent system: image-based chunky plates (often a stud or wood texture), thick dark outlines on everything including icons, one rounded display font with a stroke, saturated two-tone colour by function, standard rarity colours, and a fixed vocabulary of conventions (red square X top-right, grey for disabled, lock for locked, gold stripe for premium). Everything except the painted plates and rendered icons is cheap to copy in code with UICorner, UIStroke, UIGradient and the new UIShadow.

### Cited Findings
- Pet Simulator 99's UI is described on the DevForum as mostly custom graphics, with most buttons built as ImageButtons carrying custom art for each button or menu (snippet) — [DevForum: Unique or good looking ui](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/unique-or-good-looking-ui/2914121)
- A student design write-up on pet-simulator UIs notes a preference for icons over text labels (saves space, fits more options), elements placed along the screen edges "where they act as a border", and "quite bold" UI designs across the genre (snippet) — [Charisma Liu, UI notes](https://charismaliu.notion.site/UI-21a5cd9428d54afabdeeaf250f0ffe3b)
- The current genre look is sold as a commodity: "Stud-style" packs ship Shop, Rebirth, Index, Equip Best, Rewards, Upgrades, Settings, inventory and progression bars; a "Huge Clean Stud GUI Pack" lists Index, Rebirth, Fuse and RNG Spin frames; a "Cartoony UI Pack Pro+" is a scripted Frames-only system with hover and click effects (snippets) — [Modern Cartoon Simulator UI](https://builtbybit.com/resources/modern-cartoon-simulator-ui.109159/); [Huge Clean Stud GUI Pack](https://builtbybit.com/resources/huge-clean-stud-gui-pack-20-frames.110766/); [Cartoony UI Pack Pro+](https://builtbybit.com/resources/cartoony-ui-pack-pro-ui-system.107727/)
- The repo's own frame-by-frame read of Steal an Egg (then the number one game) records the stud-dialect shop: a bright stud header (shop green `#51DF51`, index blue `#68C9FE` to `#89ACFE`, studs at about 10% contrast, two or three diagonal lighter stripes), "— FEATURED —" / "— PASSES —" headings in yellow `#FFEF4A` with a black stroke at about 7% of panel height, a full-width featured egg banner at about 2.6:1 with a darkened illustrated background and per-pet odds, a Pet Index with rarity-coloured cells and a green "CLAIM!" button, a left icon stack (Rebirth, Index, Shop), cash bottom left and a pet bar along the bottom; the two named dialects are "stud" and "wood" (parchment and planks) — [media/tiktok/NOTES.md, ZPLRwG4Co and ZPLRwXx6J](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)
- A solo dev who ships with Claude (lihfolk) gives the single most repeated icon rule: "make sure all your icons are black-stroked, thick, or it just doesn't look good", pointing at thin-stroked arrows next to thick ones; and "large tiles for bundles and big purchases, small tiles for singles" — [media/tiktok/NOTES.md, ZPL8uaNaS](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)
- Roblox's own UI/UX guidelines name the conventions players already know: an X on the close button, grey on unusable buttons, a lock icon over locked features; and give consistency examples verbatim: "Close buttons are always square, red, and contain a white X, appearing only in the top right corner of a dialog" and "Whenever a player can't afford an item in the shop, the price displays in red" — [Roblox Creator Docs: UI and UX design](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/ui-ux-design)
- The same page says buttons must sit in a container (a colour around the text or icon) to read as interactive, and "adding highlights or shadows can enhance their tactile appeal by suggesting 3D depth"; headers larger and bolder than body; it cites Jailbreak's gold stripe under season-pass-exclusive icons and Dragon Adventures giving the highest-value coin bundle more size and more padding — [Roblox Creator Docs: UI and UX design](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/ui-ux-design)
- Roblox's UI curriculum: limit the colour theme to the key associations, never rely on colour alone (pair with icons, shapes or animation), keep icons simple enough to survive a phone screen, "display text on top of a contrasting color or with a stroke", and "use stylized text sparingly" (titles, alerts); it points designers at the free Game UI Database for genre conventions — [Roblox Creator Docs: Choose an art style](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/user-interface-design/choose-an-art-style)
- The in-engine primitives that produce the look: `UIStroke` thickness in pixels or, with `StrokeSizingMode = ScaledSize`, relative to the parent (and on text, relative to font size); `ApplyStrokeMode` Contextual (glyph outline) versus Border, with two strokes allowed on one text object; `LineJoinMode` Round/Bevel/Miter; `BorderStrokePosition` Center/Inner/Outer and `BorderOffset` (Improved UIStrokes beta); `UICorner` in Scale rounds by the shortest edge so 0.5 is a pill; `UIGradient` now Linear, Radial or Conical with Offset and Rotation — [Roblox Creator Docs: UI appearance modifiers](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/appearance-modifiers); [UIStroke reference](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/UIStroke)
- A `UIShadow` object now exists: Shape or Text mode, `BlurRadius`, `Color`, `Transparency`, `Offset`, `Spread`, `Inset = true` for an inner shadow, `ShowBehindParent`, a `UIGradient` child for a multicolour shadow, and stacking by `ZIndex`; parts are flagged "Upgraded UIShadows" beta in the docs — [Roblox Creator Docs: UI appearance modifiers, Shadow](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/appearance-modifiers)
- Fredoka One is a built-in engine font (`Enum.Font.FredokaOne`, value 26), so the rounded display face the genre uses needs no upload — [Roblox Creator Docs: Font enum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/Font)
- Rarity colour convention in a current top game: Steal a Brainrot's steal messages use green = Common, blue = Rare, purple = Epic, yellow = Legendary, red = Mythic, rainbow = Brainrot God, black and white = Secret, black and yellow = OG (snippet) — [Steal a Brainrot wiki: Stealing](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Stealing)

- Steal an Egg places its Shop and Index buttons by spawn, and its Index pays a claimable reward for every first-time pet (snippets) — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/roblox/steal-an-egg-codes-and-tips/); [FandomWire tips](https://fandomwire.com/top-10-best-roblox-steal-an-egg-tips-tricks/)
- A pre-release competitor teaser (the dinosaur ranch game) shows the current HUD vocabulary: floating "✦ DIG" verb markers, a 4-slot round hotbar bottom centre, a dark event pill ("THUNDERSTORM 3:39 left · 1.5x LUCK"), nameplates with name, stage, weight and mutation tag, a compass strip with "Your Ranch 42m", and a codex with a 3D rotatable model, IDLE/MOVE toggle, rarity tag and a black "?" silhouette for uncaught entries — [media/tiktok/NOTES.md, ZPL8MtkYu](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)
- DevForum reviewers describe the current simulator look as simple, clean layouts that newer players can read easily (snippet) — [DevForum: UI designer feedback](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/ui-designer-feedback/2540159)

### Inferences
- Cheap to copy in code (all engine primitives, no art): the outline-everything rule (UIStroke on frames and text), two-tone gradient faces (UIGradient, lighter at top), rounded corners (UICorner in Scale so it survives resizing), inner and drop shadows (UIShadow, including Inset for the "lip"), one display font with a dark glyph stroke, the red square X, grey-for-disabled, red-for-unaffordable, and rarity colours as a data table. The repo's Builder already does gloss, inner shadow and shine as code overlays; UIShadow could replace the inner-shadow overlay once it is out of beta.
- Not cheap in code: the painted 9-slice plates (stud or wood texture) and the rendered glossy icons. These are what PS99 and Steal an Egg actually buy from artists, and what the paid "Stud UI" packs sell. The gap between "clean code UI" and "top-game UI" is almost entirely these two asset classes, which is why the playbook's batch-4 icon pack and planned 9-slice plates are the right next step.
- Steal a Brainrot's palette shows the rarity convention is loose at the bottom (green can be Common) but firm from Rare upward (blue, purple, yellow/gold, red, rainbow, black). The playbook's grey/green/blue/magenta/amber/rainbow/black ordering sits inside that convention.

### Gaps
- No primary source describes Pet Simulator 99's or Adopt Me's UI from the studio side (no BIG Games or Uplift UI-artist interview surfaced); the PS99 "image-based ImageButtons" claim rests on one DevForum snippet.
- Fisch's interface: no usable source at all (every result was SEO spam); the fandom check was blocked by the search budget.
- Grow a Garden and Steal a Brainrot HUD layouts (what sits where) were not found; only colour conventions and shop timers are sourced.

## What do UI tutorials and docs with real numbers say about button size, font size, padding, outline thickness, and corner radius on phones (and the layout rules: safe areas, thumb zones, Scale versus Offset, iPhone SE fit)?

### Takeaway
Official numbers are few: a 9 x 9 mm touch target (Roblox cites the W3C mobile note), a `MinTextSize` floor of 9, and UICorner radii of 0.05 to 0.2 in Scale in Roblox's own sample; third-party guides add 44 x 44 px. The layout rules are firmer than the numbers: Scale for everything that must survive devices, UIAspectRatioConstraint to keep shapes, keep the bottom corners for the thumbstick and jump button, keep frequent buttons within thumb reach (40% from the top is reachable on a phone and not on a tablet), and let `ScreenInsets = CoreUISafeInsets` keep interactive UI clear of the notch and the top bar.

### Cited Findings
- Scale is a percentage of the container, Offset is pixels, and the two add; Studio converts a bare `0.5` to Scale and a bare `20` to Offset — [Roblox Creator Docs: Position and size](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/position-and-size)
- "On mobile devices, the default controls occupy a portion of the bottom-left and bottom-right corners of the screen... avoid placing important info or virtual buttons in these zones." Most players use two thumbs (thumbstick and jump), so frequently used buttons must stay in easy-reach zones; "A button placed 40% below the screen's top edge is reachable on a phone but almost unreachable on a tablet." — [Roblox Creator Docs: Position and size, Cross-platform factors](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/position-and-size)
- The docs' own sample sizes a custom mobile button off the engine's jump button (80% of its size, offset 20 px left of it) once `TouchGui` exists, and the laser-tag course scales its blast button to half the jump button and offsets it to the upper right — [Roblox Creator Docs: Position and size](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/position-and-size); [Implement designs in Studio](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/user-interface-design/implement-designs-in-studio)
- Touch target: the UI curriculum says UIAspectRatioConstraint helps meet the WCAG mobile recommendation of "a touch zone for interactive UI elements that's at least 9x9 mm on mobile devices", and warns that elements too small are hard to select "especially if they're in close proximity on mobile devices", so "review the size of your interactable UI elements on various screen sizes" — [Roblox Creator Docs: Choose an art style](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/user-interface-design/choose-an-art-style); [W3C Mobile A11y target size](https://w3c.github.io/Mobile-A11y-TF-Note/#targetSize)
- Third-party guides give 44 x 44 px as the minimum tappable size, suggest a `UISizeConstraint` with a 44 x 44 minimum and a larger invisible hit area behind small icon buttons, and advise "when in doubt, make buttons bigger"; one claims 70%+ of players are on mobile, which is not in Roblox's docs (snippets) — [creation.dev: Roblox UI design best practices](https://www.creation.dev/blog/roblox-ui-design-best-practices); [rolearn.dev mobile-first](https://rolearn.dev/insights/mobile-first-design-roblox-engagement); Apple 44 pt and Android 48 dp per [W3C mobile a11y wiki](https://www.w3.org/WAI/GL/mobile-a11y-tf/wiki/M2)
- Text floor: "It's recommended that no values lower than 9 be used for MinTextSize, otherwise text may not be readable to most users"; UITextSizeConstraint is meant to pair with TextScaled — [Roblox Creator Docs: UITextSizeConstraint](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/UITextSizeConstraint)
- Corner radii in Roblox's sample game: `CornerRadius` of `0.075, 0`, `0.1, 0`, `0.2, 0` on labels and buttons, `0.05, 0` on prefab buttons and portraits, `0.15, 0` on a bar and its container; every text element uses TextScaled; UIAspectRatioConstraint values of 7 (objective bar), 13.78 (team counters), 0.895 (crosshair), 5 (blaster selector); counters get a UIStroke in a dark team colour (`8, 78, 52` green, `158, 18, 94` pink) — [Roblox Creator Docs: Implement designs in Studio](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/user-interface-design/implement-designs-in-studio)
- UIStroke thickness is in pixels by default (docs illustrate 4 and 12); do not tween the Thickness of a stroke on text, it "renders and stores many glyph sizes each frame", causing performance problems or flicker — [Roblox Creator Docs: UI appearance modifiers](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/appearance-modifiers)
- Safe areas: `ScreenGui.ScreenInsets` defaults to `CoreUISafeInsets` (clear of the top-bar buttons and cutouts; "recommended if the ScreenGui contains interactive UI elements"); `DeviceSafeInsets` avoids notches only; `TopbarSafeInsets` flexes horizontally with the top bar; `None` is for non-interactive backgrounds — [Roblox Creator Docs: On-screen containers](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/on-screen-containers)
- Notched-screen support shipped with Studio emulation of iPhone X, XR and 14 Pro (no SE in that list) (snippet) — [DevForum: Notched Screen Support](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/notched-screen-support-full-release/2074324)
- Community consensus on Scale versus Offset: "I make my UIs 100% in Scale unless I have a specific need for offset"; Scale alone distorts squares, so add a UIAspectRatioConstraint; UIScale works for overall size but "positioning was awkward"; UIAspectRatioConstraints can waste space at some resolutions; TextSize is computed in Offset, which is why text differs across devices (snippets) — [DevForum: How to properly set GUI sizes](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-properly-set-gui-sizes/657654); [Mobile UI Scaling Help](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/mobile-ui-scaling-help/733681); [How do you make UI stay the same size](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-do-you-make-ui-stay-the-same-size-while-scaling-up-down-automatically-with-different-screen-sizes/3924753); [UI on Phone, Tablet, and PC](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/ui-on-phone-tablet-and-pc/477962); [Scale vs. Offset](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/scale-vs-offset/188291)
- The owner's reference creator (cuhfilme) states the same rule as the one thing to teach Claude: "Use Scale, never Offset, for Position and Size", demonstrated by flipping the emulator between iPad and iPhone 7 with the UI staying put — [media/tiktok/NOTES.md, ZPLRwXx6J](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)
- Player-side text scaling: the Text Size setting maps to `GuiService.PreferredTextSize` (Medium default; Large, Larger, Largest); TextScaled text ignores it; a UITextSizeConstraint caps it; AutomaticSize elements grow with it; TextWrapped text wraps more; `TextService:GetTextSize` honours it — [Roblox Creator Docs: Accessibility](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/accessibility)
- Developers report that after the setting shipped "multiple Roblox games now have broken UI when the client's setting is set to anything above 'Medium'", and that the only ways to opt a text object out are TextScaled or a UITextSizeConstraint, which conflicts with RichText font-size markup (snippets) — [DevForum: Preferred Text Scaling needs to be reverted](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/preferred-text-scaling-needs-to-be-reverted-abrupt-release-with-no-concerns-addressed-are-disruptive/3911199); [DevForum: new Preferred Text Size setting and RichText](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/new-preferred-text-size-setting-makes-it-impossible-to-have-text-objects-using-richtext-without-textscaled-enabled-that-arent-affected-by-the-setting/3914959); launch covered August 2025 by [Can I Play That](https://caniplaythat.com/2025/08/27/roblox-introduces-global-text-size-accessibility-setting/)
- Numbers tutorials actually give for motion on buttons: grow to 1.08 on hover, squish to 0.9 on press, with a click sound (TikTok creator "_smooth", snippet); UIFX module default Elastic ease, 0.9 size multiplier, 0.3 s (snippet) — [TikTok discover: Roblox UI design](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/roblox-ui-design); [DevForum: UIFX](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/uifx-animation-module/3295824)
- The repo's own measured phone numbers (the only iPhone-SE-class figures found anywhere): at 667x375 a panel is 395 to 526 px wide, a 14 px Fredoka line needs 17 px of height, a small shop tile is 136.9 x 130.4 px, a card chip under 0.38 of a 115 px card clips a five-letter word, "Launch Gold" is 84.0 px at 14 px, and the tap floor used for the shop price button is 26 px — [docs/vault/05-ui-design/UI-Playbook.md](../../docs/vault/05-ui-design/UI-Playbook.md)

### Inferences
- There is no official font-size table for Roblox UI; the 9 px floor is a legibility minimum, not a phone recommendation. The playbook's 14 px mobile minimum and 18/24/36/48 ladder is stricter than anything published and is defensible, but it is a house rule, not a platform rule.
- The playbook's 26 px tap floor at 667x375 is below both the 44 px third-party figure and the 9 mm Roblox-cited figure (on an iPhone SE at 326 ppi, 9 mm is roughly 115 physical px, or about 57 logical px at 2x). Any button under about 44 logical px needs a larger invisible hit area, which the creation.dev pattern (an oversized transparent button behind the small face) solves without changing the look.
- Labels that run with TextScaled off at the 14 px floor (the playbook's shop descriptions, about-lines, What's-on card) are exactly the objects the Preferred Text Size setting enlarges. Every measured fit in the playbook assumes Medium; at Largest those strings will wrap or clip unless each label carries a UITextSizeConstraint (which freezes it) or the layout is allowed to grow.

### Gaps
- No tutorial or doc gives padding or outline-thickness numbers for phones; the only figures are the docs' 4 and 12 px illustrations and the playbook's own 2 to 3 px text stroke and 3 px button stroke.
- Whether Studio ships an iPhone SE preset today could not be confirmed (docs name iPhone X and Galaxy A51 in a tutorial and now group phones as Flagship/Mid-range/Entry-level); custom devices can be defined by width, height and pixel density, so an SE profile can always be added.

## How are reveal, hatch and reward moments designed (timing, sound, particles, text) in the top pet games?

### Takeaway
No studio-side source documents PS99's or Adopt Me's hatch; the reconstructable pattern (from the owner's reference videos, DevForum build threads and the systems sold to imitators) is: egg shown large, a shake whose amplitude grows with time (often tap-driven, "Again!", "One more!"), a crack flash or shard burst, a dark silhouette in a ring, then the pet with its rarity name in the rarity colour and a "N% chance" line, closed by "Tap to continue"; paid passes sell faster, multiple and automatic hatches, and the paid-random-items policy forces per-item odds to be shown before purchase and to update while luck boosts are active.

### Cited Findings
- The owner's reference clip of a Claude-built hatch (vos_studiostt, before/after): a MYTHICAL EGG on a glowing pedestal, "Tap to crack!" then "One more!" with progress dots, white flash, black silhouette in a crack burst, card "Ocean Horse, MYTHICAL, 20% chance", "Tap to continue"; the upgraded version grows the egg with each tap, shatters it into polygon shards, shows a black hydra silhouette in a ring, then "Void Hydra, SECRET, 3% chance" under magenta light beams; the notes flag three beats the repo's reveal lacks: multi-tap crack with dots and growth per tap, shards instead of a flash, and a printed chance line — [media/tiktok/NOTES.md, ZPLSaNotv](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)
- The dinosaur-ranch teaser stages its hatch in an incubator: a brick egg under a lamp dome with close-ups as it cracks; nameplates afterwards carry name, stage, weight and mutation — [media/tiktok/NOTES.md, ZPL8MtkYu](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)
- Shake that grows: tweens cannot change amplitude mid-flight, so builders drive the egg's CFrame each frame with a sine term whose amplitude scales with elapsed time (`tick()` against a shake duration) (snippet) — [DevForum: Egg Hatching Animation?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/egg-hatching-animation/2542547)
- Keeping the egg centred needs a RenderStepped loop or a ViewportFrame, and ViewportFrames do not render ParticleEmitters, so particles have to be 2D or outside the viewport (snippet) — [DevForum: Help with egg open animation](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-with-egg-open-animation/2876100)

- Adopt Me's hatch is a timed nursery flow (egg in a nest, a hatching bar, a short cracking animation, roughly 30 to 60 s, a sound at the reveal) and the pet is "determined by the egg's internal probability table, not by how quickly or carefully you complete tasks" (snippets from low-quality guides) — [games.gg Adopt Me rare egg guide](https://games.gg/adopt-me/guides/adopt-me-rare-egg-hatching-guide/)

### Inferences
- The genre's reveal grammar is anticipation (shake that escalates, or taps that each do something), rupture (flash or shards), identity (silhouette in a ring before the model), and valuation (rarity name in its colour, a percentage, confetti scaled to tier). The repo's reveal has rupture, identity and valuation; the escalating anticipation beat and the printed chance are the missing parts, and both are policy-friendly.
- Because time per hatch is what the passes sell (skip, triple, auto), the base animation should be long enough to be worth skipping but never block the next action: a skip-on-tap after the first beat is the implicit industry default.
- Particles inside a ViewportFrame do not work, so a reveal that renders the creature in a viewport needs its burst as 2D images or BillboardGuis, which is consistent with the playbook's "radial burst behind, confetti for Epic and above".

### Gaps
- No source gives PS99's or Adopt Me's actual hatch timings, sound cues or particle counts; the only timing figures are a community 0.2 to 0.5 s UI tween range and Adopt Me's 30 to 60 s nursery wait from unreliable guides.
- The DevForum search on hatch sound design was blocked by the search budget.

## How do the best devs test UI on devices, and what breaks most often?

### Takeaway
The documented method is Studio's Device Simulator (now a toolbar in beta with Flagship, Mid-range and Entry-level phone groups, physical-size scaling and custom devices), plus `ScreenInsets` for the notch and top bar, plus a pass with the player's Text Size setting raised. What breaks most, by report: text that overflows or clips (TextScaled, RichText, Preferred Text Size), layouts built in Offset, full-screen frames under the top bar, feedback loops between AutomaticSize or Scale cells and ScrollingFrames, and taps that fall through inactive frames.

### Cited Findings
- Device Simulator (beta, "New Device Simulator") previews screen size, pixel density, on-screen keyboard and platform from a toolbar usable mid-playtest; phones are grouped Flagship, Mid-range and Entry level "so you can walk a layout down the performance ladder"; Rotate, a mock Android back button, two-finger gestures via Alt + drag; display scaling as Physical size (calibrated against a library card), Actual resolution or Fit to window; custom devices take name, platform, width and height (1 to 7680 x 1 to 4320), pixel density (72 to 10,000) and keyboard height per orientation — [Roblox Creator Docs: Device Simulator](https://create.roblox.com/docs/studio/device-simulator)
- Older tutorials name the presets "iPhone X or Samsung Galaxy A51" and enable the emulator from the Test menu — [Roblox Creator Docs: Create HUD meters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/use-case-tutorials/ui/create-hud-meters)
- The UI curriculum's method is to block in UI as basic shapes and review the composition "alongside Roblox's core UI on multiple devices", reserving space for the thumbstick (bottom left) and jump button (bottom right) even on PC layouts — [Roblox Creator Docs: Wireframe your layouts](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/user-interface-design/wireframe-your-layouts)

## What are the common mistakes that make a Roblox UI look amateur?

### Takeaway
Reviewers and guides converge on decoration without a system: several fonts, more than two or three colours, gradients on everything, default grey buttons and Source Sans, flat text with no stroke or shadow, inconsistent corner radii and spacing, icons jammed against edges, mixed icon stroke weights, blurry low-res images, Offset layouts, clutter on the HUD, hover-only interactions on a touch platform, and silent taps.

### Cited Findings
- "Many developers spend weeks perfecting their game mechanics, then throw together a default-looking GUI in an afternoon"; the fix is consistency and restraint: the same fonts, colours, corner radii and spacing throughout, no HUD clutter (snippet) — [creation.dev: Roblox UI design best practices](https://www.creation.dev/blog/roblox-ui-design-best-practices)
- Reviewer feedback across DevForum threads: padding needs work, icons too near the top edge and too far from the side, a close button that should sit near the menu button without touching it; a "bland" UI whose font and art style clash so the text looks flat; pixelated, blurry UI at small sizes; non-uniform button containers; "remove the spawn button gradient"; "limit your usage of different fonts on the same UI"; add "a slight outline or shadow to text so it doesn't look flat"; avoid more than three colours; vary corner radii rather than one radius for everything; two frames with the same name and elements positioned with the default UI tool rather than the Properties tab (snippets) — [Looking for feedback on the UI](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/looking-for-feedback-on-the-ui-for-the-experience-i-work-on/3173843); [Feedback on my GUIs?](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-guis/401969); [UI feedback for a newbie](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/ui-feedback-for-a-newbie/2865723); [Game UI Feedback](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/game-ui-feedback/2978380); [How to Improve this UI](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-improve-this-ui/2190704); [Feedback on my UIs](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-uis/1865705); [Any tips for UIs](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/any-tips-for-uis/401944)
- The long-running DevForum design guide: one or two font families maximum; a stroke on buttons "gives them more definition"; custom font files cannot be uploaded, so design in the built-in set (snippets) — [DevForum: The ultimate UI design guide](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/the-ultimate-ui-design-guide/1236916)
- Roblox's own warnings: bright and moving elements overwhelm when overused ("Moderation is key"); small screens "can easily get overwhelmed with excessive buttons, screens, and text"; show only what matters in context (Super Striker League swaps Sprint/Tackle for Deke/Pass with the ball); do not rely on colour alone; icons lose detail on phones — [Roblox Creator Docs: UI and UX design](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/ui-ux-design); [Choose an art style](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/user-interface-design/choose-an-art-style)
- "Desktop players can hover over elements for tooltips, but mobile players cannot", so hover-only affordances need a tap or long-press path (snippet) — [rolearn.dev mobile-first](https://rolearn.dev/insights/mobile-first-design-roblox-engagement)
- Mixed icon stroke weights read as broken ("make sure all your icons are black-stroked, thick") — [media/tiktok/NOTES.md, ZPL8uaNaS](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)
- Untrained Claude output is itself the canonical amateur case: "Claude sucks at making UI for Roblox games unless you know how to do it correctly", producing something "really not that intuitive" until shown screenshots of the top game and corrected — [media/tiktok/NOTES.md, ZPLRwXx6J](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)
- The playbook's avoid list matches the reviewers': default grey buttons, Source Sans, Inter or Montserrat, glass or blur panels, outline-free card grids, emoji icons, gradient text, silence on tap — [docs/vault/05-ui-design/UI-Playbook.md](../../docs/vault/05-ui-design/UI-Playbook.md)

### Inferences
- Most "amateur" tells are the absence of a token system: the moment colours, radii, strokes and spacing come from one module (the repo's Theme), half the list is impossible to produce by accident. The remaining half (blurry images, thin-next-to-thick icons, hover-only tooltips, HUD clutter) are asset and information-design mistakes that a theme cannot catch and need a review checklist.

### Gaps
- Every mistake list is individual opinion (reviewers, one blog, one TikTok creator); no survey or official "common mistakes" page exists.

## What is the production method (Figma or Photoshop slices versus UI built in code), what do UI designers charge, how do studios commission UI, and which tools, plugins, frameworks and platform features are in use?

### Takeaway
Two production paths coexist. Designers paint in Figma or Photoshop, export flattened PNGs and 9-slice plates, and rebuild in Studio by hand or with one of a new crop of Figma-to-Roblox plugins (roimport, UILint v2, Framewisp, FigBloxUI) or a Figma-like editor inside Studio (Sketch); image uploads are moderated with a placeholder until approved. Programmers build in code with the engine's modifiers, optionally through react-lua, Fusion or Vide, and since January 2026 can ship CSS-like StyleSheets with tokens, themes, state selectors and transitions. ### Inferences

The playbook's "rendered glossy icons" and "bevelled 9-slice plates" items are exactly the shopping list.
- StyleSheets are the platform's version of the repo's Theme module. They would mainly buy state selectors (hover, press, selected) and transitions declared once; migrating is optional while the feature is in client beta and only one StyleSheet can bind to a tree.

### Gaps
- No 2025-26 survey of UI rates exists; every figure is one listing, several undated, and the search summaries did not always say which listing gave which number.
- How PS99, Adopt Me or Grow a Garden commission or staff UI internally is unsourced.

## How does the repo's UI Playbook score against what was found?

### Takeaway
The playbook already encodes the genre's visual language and the platform's conventions with more precision than any public tutorial (hex palette, Scale-only, outlined Fredoka One, the red-X rule, rarity colours paired with shapes, layer orders, measured phone fits, reduced motion). What it lacks is the platform's accessibility and input layer: a stated `ScreenInsets` policy, a touch-target floor near 44 px or 9 mm, a plan for Preferred Text Size on its TextScaled-off labels, an explicit thumb-zone rule for the HUD, hover-free affordances, the "no stroke tweens on text" rule, and the three missing reveal beats.

### Cited Findings
- Gets right, with the matching source: Scale for all Position and Size ([Roblox docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/position-and-size), [cuhfilme](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)); Fredoka One as a built-in font ([Font enum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/Font)); "any text over the world gets a UIStroke" matches "display text on top of a contrasting color or with a stroke" ([curriculum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/user-interface-design/choose-an-art-style)); the red square white-X close button top right in the same place on every panel is Roblox's own consistency example word for word ([UI and UX design](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/ui-ux-design)); grey for a button that cannot act, with a toast reason, matches the grey convention plus the feedback rule; rarity tiers paired with a shape icon satisfies "don't rely on colour alone"; large tiles for bundles ([lihfolk](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md), [Dragon Adventures example](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/ui-ux-design)); the gold "Tap for the odds" line is a worded affordance, which the policy requires over a bare (i) ([paid random items](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items)); press 0.92 and hover 1.05 sit beside the community's 0.9 and 1.08; Back ease on the reveal and Reduced Motion handling match the docs' easing and accessibility pages; the event-pill anatomy and compass strip match the current competitor — [docs/vault/05-ui-design/UI-Playbook.md](../../docs/vault/05-ui-design/UI-Playbook.md)
- Lacks, with the source that supplies it: no stated `ScreenInsets` value per ScreenGui (default `CoreUISafeInsets` is recommended for anything interactive; a `None` ScreenGui behind a notch can be hidden) — [On-screen containers](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/on-screen-containers); a 26 px tap floor against 44 x 44 px and 9 x 9 mm — [creation.dev](https://www.creation.dev/blog/roblox-ui-design-best-practices), [curriculum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/user-interface-design/choose-an-art-style); no rule for `GuiService.PreferredTextSize` although many labels run with TextScaled off at 14 px (those are exactly the labels the setting enlarges) — [Accessibility](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/accessibility); no thumb-zone or reserved-corner statement for the HUD (the Build tray and the bottom-anchored banners sit in the zone the docs reserve for the thumbstick and jump button) — [Position and size](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/position-and-size); no "hover-only is forbidden" rule for tooltips — [rolearn.dev](https://rolearn.dev/insights/mobile-first-design-roblox-engagement); no warning against tweening UIStroke thickness on text — [appearance modifiers](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/appearance-modifiers); the reveal lacks the multi-tap crack, shards and "N% chance" line that the owner's own reference shows — [ZPLSaNotv](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md); the "What to avoid" list omits mixed icon stroke weights and blurry images, the two most-cited asset mistakes — [lihfolk](../../media/tiktok/NOTES.md), [DevForum feedback](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/ui-feedback-for-a-newbie/2865723); the gloss, inner shadow and shine are code overlays where `UIShadow` (Inset, BlurRadius, gradient child) now exists in beta — [appearance modifiers](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/appearance-modifiers); the Theme module has no stated relationship to StyleSheet tokens and themes, now publishable — [UI styling](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/styling), [client beta post](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/client-beta-you-can-now-publish-styles-in-your-experience/3901480)
- A note on one playbook claim: it says "no web fonts"; the DevForum guide's claim is that font files cannot be uploaded, and Fredoka One needs none, so the rule is consistent but the reason is better stated as "built-in fonts only" (snippet) — [DevForum: The ultimate UI design guide](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/the-ultimate-ui-design-guide/1236916)

### Inferences
- The cheapest high-value additions are three lines in the playbook and one Builder change: (1) every interactive ScreenGui uses `CoreUISafeInsets`; (2) any face under 44 logical px gets an invisible hit button at 44 px or more; (3) a label with TextScaled off carries a UITextSizeConstraint, or its band is sized for Largest; and a device-pass checklist that adds Text Size Largest and Reduce Motion to the iPhone SE and iPad runs.
- The reveal upgrade (tap-to-crack with growth, shards, chance line) is the one place where the reference material is ahead of the playbook on the game's single most important emotional beat.

### Gaps
- Whether the repo's HUD actually overlaps the thumbstick and jump zones on a phone was not verified here (would need a Studio screenshot at 667x375 with TouchGui visible).
