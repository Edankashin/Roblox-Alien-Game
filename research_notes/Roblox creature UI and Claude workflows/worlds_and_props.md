## Key question 1: How are the top games' maps structured (hub and zones, one hero landmark per zone, readable paths, bounded islands)?

### Takeaway
Every top collection game separates a social hub from themed zones and bounds the world hard (an island, a ring of bases, an archipelago on open water), and the references the team already studied agree on the same rule: one hero landmark per zone, a pale path that leads to it, and a world that reads as a single object from the establishing shot. Roblox's own environmental-art curriculum teaches the same structure from the greybox up (lanes, pockets with at most three entrances, placeholder colours for orientation, an unplayable ring drawn before the playable area).

### Inferences
- The structure the sources converge on: a hub with the shop, storage, index and leaderboard clustered at spawn (PS99 Spawn, Adoption Island centre, the Brainrot conveyor plaza), themed zones gated by progression (PS99 areas, Fisch's boat at level 10, Mushroom Tycoon's six areas), and a hard edge (island water, a tile rim, a barrier ring). The alien game's "one island per world with a camp in the middle" is the same pattern.
- "Bounded island" is both an art and a performance choice: the curriculum draws the unplayable ring before anything else, and the cinematic reference films the island as an object; a world that cannot be framed from a pull-out shot is not finished.
- Seasonal re-dressing of one fixed island (Adopt Me's snow, Ice Palace, Halloween signposts) is how a live game refreshes the map without a new layout; signposts to unbuilt landmarks are a cheap way to promise content.

## Key question 2: What does a "build an Adopt Me style map" or "Pet Sim style map" tutorial actually do, in order, and which steps make a map read as finished rather than placed?

### Takeaway
The only fully readable, current, step-by-step source is Roblox's own environmental-art curriculum; the YouTube "low poly map" and "simulator map" series follow the same arc (block out, scatter mountains, trees, roads, rocks, then light it), and the steps that separate "finished" from "placed" are the ones that come last: a treated edge, a painted path, the lighting and atmosphere pass, ambient particle layers, local lights on landmarks, and deleting the greybox and baseplate.

### Cited Findings

- Chapter 4's build order: floors as block and wedge parts with a distinct Color, Material or MaterialVariant per zone; modular walls, corners and door frames tinted per team; props as cover; roof "in layers from the top down"; then "delete the greybox geometry and the baseplate to remove unseen polygons"; then terrain (unplayable ring, playable area, painted primary path alternating Mud, Leafy Grass, Ground, Sand, Salt, Slate and Snow, a water pool separated by a Slate crescent), rocks on the border, foliage, containment walls; then Lighting, Atmosphere, Clouds, Bloom, DepthOfField, foreground cloud and dust ParticleEmitters, SurfaceLights along the perimeter and SpotLights in doorways — [Construct your world](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/construct-your-world)

## Key question 3: What is the build method and prop pipeline (Blender props versus Studio parts, modular kits, Terrain versus mesh ground, prop counts, triangle budgets, instancing, atlases)?

### Takeaway
Roblox's own guidance is a Blender (or Maya) modular kit on a 5-stud grid with consistent pivots, one square trim sheet (up to 1024) shared by the kit and the props, SurfaceAppearance packages, props that carry "only enough geometry to read from their silhouette", and terrain for the ground with custom-material overrides; the engine's hard limit is 20,000 triangles per mesh, and identical meshes with identical SurfaceAppearance and materials are instanced into one draw call, which is why uploading a prop once and cloning it matters more than its triangle count.

### Cited Findings
- "Individual meshes can not exceed 20,000 triangles"; "a vertex can not be influenced by more than 4 bones or joints"; only one animation track exports with a mesh — [Mesh specifications](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/specifications)
- Modular kit rules: pieces snap at "5-stud increments"; pivots "at the forward-most lower corner"; "each asset is at least 5 studs tall and wide"; dimensions divisible by the smallest footprint (largest sample piece 15 by 5); split large pieces into child meshes (UpperTrim, LowerTrim, Wall) "so each mesh can have its own material, collision, and rendering settings"; clipping "usually means one asset's pivot is inconsistent" — [Develop polished assets](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/develop-polished-assets)
- Trim sheets "tile on one axis", the sample has six rows and must be square; tileable textures and trim sheets "can be up to 1024x1024" and "the closer you get to this maximum size, the higher the performance cost"; props need "only enough geometry to read from their silhouette"; the sample prop kit is panels, crates, fire extinguishers, security cameras, rocks and plants, reusing the kit's trim sheet "to keep style consistent"; tools named: Substance Designer, Blender, ZBrush, Maya — [Develop polished assets](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/develop-polished-assets)
- Asset library: import .fbx or .obj, one Model with a MeshPart per component; SurfaceAppearance with Albedo, Metalness, Normal and Roughness, converted to a package and reused on every asset; tileable textures become MaterialVariants with "Set as Override" for terrain and "Organic" tiling for ground; per-asset flags: Anchored (everything but doors), CanCollide off for foliage, CanTouch only where Touched is needed, CanQuery off unless raycasts must hit it, CastShadow off "where missing shadows won't be noticed", DoubleSided only on foliage, CollisionFidelity Box or Hull, RenderFidelity Automatic (or Performance for complex foliage) — [Assemble an asset library](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/assemble-an-asset-library)
- Instancing: "Meshes with the same MeshContent share one draw call when their SurfaceAppearance (or TextureContent) and materials match. Re-importing a whole map can create separate asset IDs, which blocks instancing"; triangle count "matters less than draw calls"; textures: "1024x1024 pixel texture consumes four times the graphics memory of a 512x512 texture", an image "usually needs at most 512x512 pixels" and minor images "under 256x256"; small UI images go in a sprite sheet via ImageRectOffset/ImageRectSize — [Improve performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve)
- "Built-in materials use far less memory than custom textures, so reserve texture budget for central assets"; "reuse meshes and textures through resizing, rotating, and overlapping to reduce draw calls"; convert assets to packages to avoid duplicate IDs — [Design for performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/design)
- Terrain is 4 by 4 by 4 stud voxels with one material each; 23 default materials; MaterialColors per material; water has WaterColor, WaterReflectance (0 to 1), WaterTransparency (0 to 1), WaterWaveSize (0 to 1), WaterWaveSpeed (0 to 100); Decoration grass only on the Grass material, GrassLength 0.1 to 1; Generate offers nine biomes; heightmaps at 1 pixel = 4 studs up to 4096 by 4096; brush base size 1 to 64 studs — [Terrain](https://create.roblox.com/docs/parts/terrain)
- Custom materials cannot be applied to terrain directly, only as an override of a base material; "terrain materials are global per place"; TerrainDetail customises top, side and bottom faces; "square textures work best"; glass refraction "isn't supported on mobile devices" — [Materials](https://create.roblox.com/docs/parts/materials)
- Terrain versus meshes on DevForum: "100K triangles really isn't enough to be concerned over for the grass baseplate"; a poster cites Roblox's claim that "90% of devices (including mobile) can render 1 million triangles at 60FPS"; terrain voxels are "stored using run-length-encoding in memory, so large sections that are completely solid or completely air do not cost very much"; for plain shapes "you may as well use a part" (snippet) — [DevForum, Optimize Smooth Terrain Better](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/optimize-smooth-terrain-better/3781706)
- Triangle (mesh) terrain plugins exist for "stylized low-poly environments to performance-optimized landscapes" (snippet, third-party listing) — [creation.dev, Triangle Terrain Editor](https://www.creation.dev/learn/triangle-terrain-editor-plugin-roblox-studio); a DevForum guide converts triangle terrain to smooth terrain with Part to Terrain (snippet) — [DevForum, Guide making efficient terrain](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/guide-making-efficient-terrain/2291922)
- Blender terrain can hit Roblox's triangle limits; "plenty of tutorials on low-poly terrain" exist without plugins (snippet) — [DevForum, How to design a map in blender](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-design-a-map-in-blender/1014812)
- "Roblox doesn't have good support for low res textures, as they get automatically smoothed out"; keep resolution high and pixelate in external software (snippet) — [DevForum, I need help on Lowpoly](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/i-need-help-on-lowpoly/424470)
- A palette-atlas workflow (collapse each face's UVs to a point on a small colour atlas, "set interpolation to closest for crisp edges") is the standard Blender low-poly method; a commercial gradient atlas ships as one 1024 by 2304 file (snippet; the Vertex Arcade tutorial URL was not captured) — [tenebra23, Texture Gradient Palette](https://tenebra23.itch.io/texture-gradient-palette)

## Key question 4: What lighting and post-processing settings do the top games use, and what do the docs now say about lighting technology?

### Takeaway
No top game's lighting values are published anywhere reachable; what is documented is that Roblox has deprecated `Lighting.Technology` (Future, ShadowMap, Voxel, and the never-described "Unified") in favour of `LightingStyle` (Realistic or Soft) plus `PrioritizeLightingQuality`, that low graphics quality forces the Soft path on the client regardless, and that Roblox's own sample world ships with Realistic, a dim teal ambient, ShadowSoftness 0.15, Atmosphere density 0.285 with haze 2 and glare 0.3, Bloom 1.5 at size 56 and a faint far depth of field, which is the closest thing to a published recipe.

### Cited Findings
- `Lighting.Technology` "has been superseded by" `LightingStyle` (artistic intent) and `PrioritizeLightingQuality` (quality versus view distance); the enum values are Legacy (0, deprecated, not selectable), Voxel (1, "a 4x4x4 voxel grid"), Compatibility (2, deprecated; approximate it with Voxel plus a ColorGradingEffect Retro tonemapper), ShadowMap (3, "more realistic, sharper shadows from sunlight"), Future (4, "the most advanced option"), Unified (5, "deprecated and cannot be selected in Studio", no description) — [Technology enum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/Technology)
- `LightingStyle`: Realistic (0) is "the most advanced and realistic lighting and shadows Roblox can deliver"; Soft (1) is "a flat, retro-Roblox look with softer lights and shadows"; "Soft with [PrioritizeLightingQuality] enabled uses shadow maps instead of voxel lighting"; `PrioritizeLightingQuality` true keeps "advanced shadows and high-quality shaders at closer distances" as quality drops, false keeps view distance — [LightingStyle enum](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/LightingStyle); [Lighting class](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Lighting)
- The docs are mid-transition: the Lighting guide says ShadowSoftness "only applies when LightingStyle is Realistic", while the class reference still says it only works "when Technology is ShadowMap or Future and the device supports shadow maps" — [Lighting guide](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/lighting); [Lighting class](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Lighting)
- A tutorial dated 7 October 2026: "Older tutorials may ask you to select Future or Unified under Technology. The Technology reference says that workflow has been superseded by LightingStyle and PrioritizeLightingQuality" (snippet) — [obby.fun, realistic lighting](https://www.obby.fun/blog/roblox-studio-realistic-lighting)
- Lowering a player's graphics quality "switches Realistic lighting to Soft"; a community reply: "there is currently no way of preserving lighting quality unless you bake lighting as texture overlays since the engine is hard-coded to apply the user's graphics quality settings" (snippet) — [DevForum, Realistic Lighting on Lower Quality Settings](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/realistic-lighting-on-lower-quality-settings/4027710)
- Future is Bright Phase 3 (the "Future" technology) shipped "on desktop graphics APIs (D3D11, Vulkan, Metal)"; devices without per-pixel support "fall back to ShadowMap, or to Voxel if ShadowMap isn't supported"; "per-pixel lighting distance depends on the current quality level" (snippet) — [DevForum, Future is Bright Phase 3 Released](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/future-is-bright-phase-3-released/878634)
- Phase 1 (Voxel) "was enabled on all platforms"; Phase 2 (ShadowMap) was "enabled on desktop platforms first, with mobile falling back to Legacy"; Phase 1 also moved the pipeline to "HDR rendering with a tonemapper" and removed the stylized "black part bloom" trick (snippet) — [DevForum, An update on Future Is Bright](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/an-update-on-future-is-bright); [DevForum, Future Is Bright Phase 1 Released](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/future-is-bright-phase-1-released)
- A developer "moved from Future lighting to ShadowMaps due to performance issues" (snippet) — [DevForum, Future is Bright - Shadowmap Lighting](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/future-is-bright-shadowmap-lighting/883655)
- Shadows "are degraded automatically and disabled at quality levels below 4"; options: CastShadow off on small or distant parts, Light.Shadows off, limit light range and angle, fewer lights, lights off room by room — [Improve performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve)
- Global defaults and ranges: Ambient defaults to black and only affects sky-occluded areas while its channels stay at or below OutdoorAmbient (default 127,127,127); GlobalShadows off makes Ambient apply everywhere; ShadowSoftness default 0.2; EnvironmentDiffuseScale and EnvironmentSpecularScale default 0 ("if you increase it, you should usually decrease Ambient and OutdoorAmbient"); ExposureCompensation default 0, range -5 to 5, "each step doubles (positive) or halves (negative) the exposure"; FogStart/FogEnd are hidden once an Atmosphere exists; voxel shadows are 4 studs so smaller objects "won't cast realistic shadows" — [Lighting class](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Lighting)
- Brightness examples shown at 0.5, 1.5 and 3.75; ClockTime 0 to 24 and TimeOfDay are two views of one value and never follow real time; GeographicLatitude moves the sun without changing the clock — [Lighting guide](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/lighting)
- Atmosphere: Density (examples 0 and 0.35) "obstructs the view of objects and terrain"; Offset (0 and 1) controls horizon silhouette versus blending distant objects into the sky, and a low Offset "can make the skybox show through objects and terrain"; Haze (1 and 2.8); Color works "best with a high Haze value"; Glare "requires Haze above 0"; Decay "requires Haze and Glare above 0" and fades from Color toward the Decay colour away from the sun (example 255,90,80) — [Atmosphere](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/atmosphere)
- Post-processing: BloomEffect ("when this effect has a high value, parts with light colors glow"), BlurEffect, ColorCorrectionEffect (Brightness, Contrast, Saturation, TintColor), DepthOfFieldEffect, SunRaysEffect (rays follow ClockTime), ColorGradingEffect with TonemapperPreset Default ("post-2019 look, vivid colors and high contrast") or Retro ("pre-2019 look"; for the full retro look "set all lights to a brightness of at most 1.0"); effects on Lighting apply to everyone, on Camera to one player; effects may not show in Studio unless Editor Quality Level is at its highest — [Post-processing effects](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/post-processing-effects)
- Roblox's sample world values: LightingStyle Realistic ("lets global and local lights work together, and automatically switches between interior and exterior lighting models"); Ambient and OutdoorAmbient both 26,34,36; ShadowSoftness 0.15; GeographicLatitude -18; TimeOfDay 15:16:23; Atmosphere Density 0.285, Offset 0.65, Decay 254,254,254, Glare 0.3, Haze 2; Bloom Intensity 1.5, Size 56; DepthOfField FarIntensity 0.05; Clouds Cover 0.625, Density 0.5, Color 143,143,143 — [Construct your world](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/construct-your-world)
- The sample's local lights: perimeter Neon strips (89 by 1 by 1 studs, colour 199,166,147) carrying a SurfaceLight (colour 211,190,150, Face Bottom, Range 21) "give orientation cues"; spawn-zone Neon slabs in team colours (88,218,171 and 255,170,255); hallway SpotLights (Angle 135, Range 20) on a 0.25-stud Neon block — [Construct your world](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/construct-your-world)
- The DevForum "Guide to Lighting": Bloom threshold is "how bright an object needs to be before applying the bloom. Might affect parts with the material set to Neon"; SunRays Intensity is "the opacity of each ray", Spread "how wide the rays spread", skip it at night; Atmosphere Density "the higher this value, the more of a foggy vibe"; "the atmosphere effect will not appear if you have the default skybox" (snippet) — [DevForum, Guide to Lighting](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/guide-to-lighting/2764787)

### Inferences

## Key question 6: How do they make a map feel alive (NPCs, idle animations, ambient motion, particle layers), and what does the engine allow?

### Takeaway
The documented tools for ambient life are terrain grass and clouds driven by GlobalWind, wind-affected particles, and low-rate large-sprite emitters (Roblox's sample uses a Rate 0.25 cloud layer and a Rate 50,000 dust layer across the island); emitters are capped at 100 particles a second on mobile with a reported 3,600-particle render ceiling, and Highlight outlines are capped at 255 per client.

### Cited Findings
- ParticleEmitter limits: "a single emitter can create up to 400 particles per second, or 100 per second on mobile"; Lifetime is capped at 20 seconds; "keep the rate as low as possible, and use size and other properties to get the look you want"; larger particles cost fill-rate; flipbooks are 2x2, 4x4, 8x8 or custom at up to 30 fps and "clients on low-memory devices, often older mobile phones, may turn them off automatically"; test at both the lowest and highest Editor Quality Level — [Particle emitters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters)
- An undocumented render cap reported on DevForum: 16,000 particles on computers, 3,600 on mobile and Xbox (snippet) — [DevForum, Particle not appearing](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particle-not-appearing/573833)
- Roblox's sample ambient layers: foreground clouds on a transparent anchored block with LightEmission 0.3, LightInfluence 0.2, Orientation FacingCameraWorldUp, Size 100, Squash -0.25, Lifetime 30, Rate 0.25, Acceleration (0,-0.8,0), Drag 0.1, LockedToPart, plus a second layer at Rate 0.1; island-wide dust with colour 192,241,255, ZOffset -5, Lifetime 1 to 10, Rate 50000, RotSpeed -60, Speed 1 to 5, Acceleration (1,-1,1), "keep speed slow for subtle micro-motion" — [Construct your world](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/construct-your-world)
- Decoration grass sways with GlobalWind; with Reduce Motion on, "grass animation speed decreases, but its direction doesn't change"; clouds "drift slowly across the sky" with wind; Fire and Smoke follow wind by default, other particles only with WindAffectsDrag and Drag above 0 — [Terrain](https://create.roblox.com/docs/parts/terrain); [Global wind](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/global-wind)
- Highlight: "255 simultaneous Highlight instances on the client at a time", extras "are ignored without any error", a disabled Highlight still takes a slot; DepthMode AlwaysOnTop or Occluded; FillTransparency and OutlineTransparency 0 to 1 — [Highlight class](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Highlight); an older DevForum thread recalled a 30 to 35 cap and a plan to raise it to 255, now reflected in the docs (snippet) — [DevForum, highlight limits](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-it-possible-to-increase-the-limits-of-highlight-nodes/3976811)

## Key question 7: What phone performance limits do builders keep to (parts, triangles, particles, draw calls) and how do they test them?

### Takeaway
Roblox's docs give one illustrative scene budget, "below 1,000 draw calls and 1,000,000 triangles" on a baseline device (an older revision said 600 and 650,000; a mid-2024 DevForum budget says 500 and 500,000 for 60 FPS on most phones), tell builders to pick one baseline phone and keep the Developer Console open, and warn that the Studio emulator is not accurate for memory; the per-system caps that bite on phones are 100 particles a second per emitter, shadows off below quality level 4, no glass refraction, and memory before part count.

### Cited Findings
- "At 60 FPS, the total budget for each frame is 16.67 milliseconds"; example baseline: "stay below 1,000 draw calls and 1,000,000 triangles" (presented as the example's figures); "lower-end, especially mobile, devices have severe memory limits and can crash from out-of-memory errors"; "choose one baseline device, test on it throughout development"; "Studio's device emulator is not accurate for memory, because Studio runs both server and client"; use Render and Summary stats (Shift+F2) and the Developer Console (F9); streaming "improves join times, reduces memory footprint, and increases frame rate"; EnableSLIMAvatars and LevelOfDetail SLIM for crowds; transparency only 0 or 1 where possible — [Design for performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/design)
- An earlier revision of the same page gave "600 draw calls and 650,000 triangles" (snippet of the older text) — [Design for performance (older copy)](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/performance-optimization/design.md)
- A mid-2024 DevForum budget: about 500,000 triangles and 500 draw calls in scene "aimed at 60 FPS on most phones and tablets" (community author) (snippet) — [DevForum, Real world building and scripting optimization](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/3127146)
- A vendor blog claims under 100 draw calls per frame on mid-range mobile; unverified against Roblox's docs (snippet) — [Nilo](https://nilo.io/articles/optimize-3d-models-roblox-limit)
- Part-count folklore (2017 to 2020 threads): keep a shooter under about 4,000 parts, a slower round game around 7,000; "5,000 blocks within a 1,000-stud radius can lag lower-end devices, and 10,000 in that radius lags all devices"; 15 to 20k+ "could cause problems for mobile"; a counter-report runs "about 90k parts and meshes" with a custom loading system and argues memory, scripts, unanchored parts, textures and unions matter more (snippet) — [DevForum, Reasonable brick counts](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/reasonable-brick-counts/290266); [DevForum, 13000 parts](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/13000-parts-is-it-too-much/344990); [DevForum, How to build games for mobile](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-build-games-for-mobile/280366)
- Rendering levers with numbers: shadows "disabled at quality levels below 4"; textures at most 512 for most images and under 256 for minor ones; Model.LevelOfDetail SLIM and MeshPart.RenderFidelity Automatic or Performance for distant meshes; CollisionFidelity Box cheapest, "generally safe to set any small anchored part's collision fidelity to Box"; CanCollide, CanTouch, CanQuery off where unused; StreamingMinRadius and StreamingTargetRadius lowered for aggressive streaming; MicroProfiler scopes (Prepare, Perform, computeLightingPerform, LightGridCPU, ShadowMapSystem, physicsStepped, stepHumanoid; investigate updateInvalidatedFastClusters "over 4 ms"); preload only loading-screen, menu and spawn-area assets — [Improve performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve)
- Particles: 100 a second per emitter on mobile, flipbooks may be disabled on low-memory phones — [Particle emitters](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/particle-emitters); glass refraction "isn't supported on mobile devices" — [Materials](https://create.roblox.com/docs/parts/materials)
- Terrain: a poster cites Roblox's claim that "90% of devices (including mobile) can render 1 million triangles at 60FPS" (snippet) — [DevForum, Optimize Smooth Terrain Better](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/optimize-smooth-terrain-better/3781706)
- Curriculum optimisation order: CastShadow off on edge foliage; dedupe asset IDs via packages; combine meshes (8 draw calls to 1) and lower polygons; delete layered transparencies; MicroProfiler and Scene Analysis — [Optimize your experience](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/optimize-your-experience)
- Testing tools: the Device Simulator previews "screen dimensions, pixel density, on-screen keyboard, and touch input" (no accuracy claim for frame rate or memory; the page names VR presets, not phone ones); Controller Emulator; "Server & Clients" multi-client ("usually 1-2 clients is sufficient, although you can simulate up to eight"); Network Simulator for latency, jitter and packet loss; StudioDeviceSimulatorService for scripted device cycling — [Testing modes](https://create.roblox.com/docs/studio/testing-modes)
- Lowering a player's quality switches Realistic to Soft lighting client-side (snippet) — [DevForum, Realistic Lighting on Lower Quality Settings](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/realistic-lighting-on-lower-quality-settings/4027710)
- The repo's budgets: "Future lighting with at most two shadow-casting lights per world, particles under 200 on screen, meshes under 1,500 triangles per alien and 600 per prop. Check on an iPhone SE in the emulator before calling anything done" — [docs/vault/06-art-pipelines/Look-Plan.md](../../docs/vault/06-art-pipelines/Look-Plan.md)

### Inferences
- A consistent phone budget from the three sources: about 500 draw calls and 500,000 triangles in view at 60 FPS, with the docs' 1,000 and 1,000,000 as the ceiling for a baseline device. For the alien game (30 trees at 300 to 900 triangles, 32 species under 1,500, a terrain island), triangles are not the constraint; draw calls and memory are, so instancing (one upload per prop) and texture restraint matter most.
- Because the emulator is not a memory test and Soft lighting is forced on low quality, the repo's "iPhone SE in the emulator" check needs a second leg: a real low-end phone with the Developer Console open and graphics quality at level 3, where shadows are already off.

### Gaps

- The docs' Device Simulator page names no phone presets, so whether an "iPhone SE" preset still exists could not be confirmed from the docs (the repo's TESTING workflow assumes it).
