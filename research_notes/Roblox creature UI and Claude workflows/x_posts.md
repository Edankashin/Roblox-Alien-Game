# What Roblox developers say on X about building creatures, worlds, UI and effects (as of 2026-10-15)

Scope: the X half of the owner's 2026-10-15 ask ("YouTube videos and TikToks and X posts about tutorials on how successful Roblox game developers designed their models and everything else in the game including the UI"). The other five notes in this folder cover YouTube, TikTok, the DevForum and the docs; none cites a post on X. This note closes that gap as far as search snippets allow, and only adds what the other notes do not already hold.

Method and evidence tags. 42 web searches (39 standard, 3 extended) and one failed fetch; 66 distinct posts are recorded below. What the sandbox could and could not do:
- x.com does not resolve from the fetch tool (`ENOTFOUND`), and the `site:x.com` operator returns no X result at all. The search tool's domain filter (`allowed_domains` set to x.com and twitter.com) does return X posts, so every entry below comes from a result title that carries the author's own words, cut to the first 200 characters or so by the index.
- **(snippet)**: the author's words as the result title shows them, nothing opened. A confidence of "snippet-only" applies to every entry. Quotes are trimmed at an ellipsis and have emoji removed; the words inside are unchanged. Where I add a reading taken from the search tool's own summary of the result (a reply, a thumbnail, a thread), I write "per summary", and treat it as weaker.
- **Dates** come from the status id: an X id encodes its posting time, and the decode matched every date a snippet printed (Exile 23 February 2024, Erythia September 2019, EGOTISMS November 2023, Bethink February 2020). Treat a date as accurate to the day.
- **Role tags**: *ships* (staff or contractor on a live Roblox game), *freelance* (sells art, UI or VFX), *tool* (builds a plugin), *official* (a Roblox account), *commentary* (fan, reporter, investor, aggregator). The ask was for people who ship, so commentary is kept only where it exposes a claim worth checking.
- No model names appear in this note: where a post names the Claude model it used, the quote shows `[model]`.

What the X pass adds to the other notes: (a) two UIStroke facts the Playbook lacks; (b) a collision setting the prop importer lacks; (c) the Grow a Garden pet "mutation" looks as contractor-made VFX over one mesh; (d) price and ratio data from commercial pet packs; (e) a primary-source check on the viral "Claude built a whole Roblox game" claim; (f) one lead on the Adopt Me creative director's shared cartoon lighting preset whose values sit in an image the snippet cannot show. The studios themselves (BIG Games, Uplift, the Grow a Garden team) post updates, hiring calls and fan-facing art on X, almost never process; the process lives in the individuals below.

## Creature and pet art: what X shows about how pets are made, staffed and varied

### Takeaway
No post on X shows a studio's step-by-step creature pipeline. What it shows instead is (1) the labour model (Adopt Me hires 3D artists for pets at $50k to $85k a year, a Roblox pet-and-map modeller is advertised at $3k+ a month for weekly output, Pet Simulator X began as one person's work), (2) the product shape of a pet (a commercial pack is 80 pets with rainbow variants, 16 eggs and 240 icons on recolourable textures, so rarity is a recolour and the icon count is three per pet), (3) three cheap shape rules from an asset-maker for keeping low-poly cute (round edges, tapered legs, squircle shapes), and (4) the state of Claude-and-Blender claims, where the one primary source found says "scripted", not "modelled". The AI-character question around Steal a Brainrot is still a copyright story, not a pipeline story.

### Inferences
- The kit ratios (5 to 6 pets per egg, 3 icons per pet, a rainbow variant by recolour) say the market already treats variants as texture work. The repo's rarity-by-tint and per-species effects fit that, and a species needs one mesh, one palette and a short list of regions that can carry colour or glow.
- Kenney's three rules are a better ship check for "cute, not mashed" than polygon counts, and they are cheap to compute on a body: edge bevel present, limb width tapering from root to tip, plan outline closer to a rounded square than a box.
- The Claude-on-X evidence is thin where it matters. Every capable-sounding claim is a relay, a template or a result without an image in the snippet; the one primary source says scripting. This agrees with the existing report's view that Claude scripts well and the creature build is better scripted than generated.
- "Mutation" looks being simple VFX over one mesh (Grow a Garden) supports building each species' glow regions into the mesh slots now.

## Worlds and props: what X shows about lighting, vertex colour, materials and prop settings

### Takeaway
The X material on worlds is thin and old, but it holds three usable things: a prop-import setting the repo's `Props.luau` does not set (collision fidelity Hull or Box on decor meshes), a lighting lead (the Adopt Me creative director's shared "generic cartoon lighting" preset, whose values are in an image), and a reminder that the Grow a Garden look is largely smooth plastic: a single flat material with the colour doing the work. Vertex colour on meshes has been the Adopt Me environment lead's tool since 2017.

### Cited Findings
- **@KVetteJ** (2022-04-28, developer): "Any mesh that a player does not need precise physics on should have its collision fidelity set to "Hull". Any mesh that will never be interacted with should be set to non-collide and the collision fidelity to "Box". This helps with memory and helps performance." Teaches: a concrete import rule, two settings per decor mesh. `src/shared/Props.luau` sets `Anchored`, `CanCollide`, `CanQuery` and `CanTouch` but never `CollisionFidelity`. ### Inferences
- Collision fidelity is a one-line change per prop in the importer and a small memory saving across hundreds of scattered props; the post's two rules (decor non-collide plus Box, walk-on props Hull) map directly onto the existing `collide` flag.
- The lighting lead is worth one Mac action: open Bethink's post in a logged-in browser and copy the numbers from the image, then compare them with `src/shared/data` lighting values after the Technology to LightingStyle change.
- "Smooth plastic everywhere" lines up with the repo's single glossy-material body surface; nothing here argues for more material variety on props.

### Gaps
- No post found describes map layout, zone structure, prop counts or modular kits; those searches returned commission ads and showcase posts with no method.
- No mobile performance budget appears on X from a studio. The only numbers are old engine limits (a 2018 raise to 10k triangles per mesh, a 2023 note on 10k for dynamic meshes) and a Unity-side LOD budget that is not Roblox.
- Crykee's Halloween-environment breakdown is named in a search summary but its URL was not returned.

## UI: what X shows from the people who build and sell it

### Takeaway
The X UI material confirms the structure of the existing UI notes (Figma plugins, StyleSheets, freelance prices) and adds three things: two UIStroke facts that change the Builder (join mode on text strokes, and a stroke thickness that cannot use Scale), the pay and cadence of UI as a job (Fisch hires UI designers for weekly frames, Adopt Me gives UX/UI to a 2D artist who also paints promo art), and the shape of icon packs (25 icons in four tones). The cartoony Steal a Brainrot UI was a freelancer's work in a style "heavily inspired by PLS Donate".

### Cited Findings

**Stroke and text.**
- **@EGOTISMS_RBLX** (EGOTISMS, UI designer on Blox Fruits and earlier Dragon Adventures, 2023-11-27, ships): "UI life hack #2 Want to avoid these UIStroke artifacts where the outline doesn't render fully in the glyph counter in Roblox Studio? Change the LineJoinMode to either Bevel or Miter!" Per summary a reply adds it only works with fonts that have curved or rounded corners. Teaches: a text outline can fill the holes of letters badly (the counters of o, e, a); the fix is the stroke's join mode. The repo sets no `LineJoinMode` anywhere, and Fredoka is a rounded face. ## VFX and animation: what X shows about particles, flipbooks, pet effects and rig hand-offs

### Takeaway
The top pet games buy effects one at a time from contract VFX artists (a Pet Simulator 99 "Black Hole Ultimate", Grow a Garden's pet mutations), and the techniques people teach on X are the particle flipbook (one sprite sheet, one emitter), mesh VFX with a flipbook, and visual-editor timelines that call `:Emit()` at animation keyframes. The animation side is a hand-off problem (FBX scale and axis, rig export) that several plugin authors have solved in public, and the satisfying micro-loop to copy is the coin pickup.
