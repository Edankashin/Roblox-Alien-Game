"Blocky" pets belong to the simulator lineage: the Pet Simulator series (and Pets GO), its many clones, Bee Swarm Simulator, Grow a Garden (described as blocky and pixelated) and Steal a Brainrot (described by Wikipedia as "voxel characters"). ## 2. What are the sub-styles of "blocky", which games use which, and which is most common in the top games today?

### Takeaway
Five sub-styles can be told apart. The bevelled-cube "simulator pet" with a texture face (Pet Simulator lineage) is the most documented and the most copied, and is the genre default. The voxel or cube-cell look (Steal a Brainrot per Wikipedia, the owner's references ZPLSHwXqp and ZPL8MtkYu) is the one rising in 2025 and 2026. The "most common in the top games today" ranking is my inference; no source ranks the sub-styles.

### Cited Findings

- **Most common, by count of games:** sub-style A. It is the template the 2020 tutorial teaches, the subject of a 2023 takedown wave that hit several games at once (one with about 300 designs), and the subject of the asset packs and hiring posts.

## 3. What is the grammar of the look (primitives, bevels, ratios, limbs, appendages, faces, surfaces, palette, outlines, shading)?

### Takeaway
The grammar is a small kit held to strict rules: a few axis-aligned boxes with one bevel width shared by every block, a head as big as or bigger than the body, stubby block limbs (four or none), appendages as wedges, slabs or block chains, and a face painted as a texture on the front of the head. Colour is flat, with two to four hues plus a dark ink. There is no outline shader on the creature; the "outline" is painted ink or a Highlight.

### Cited Findings
- **Primitives and bevel:** body from a cube; bevel the side edges at width 0.2 with 6 segments (Ctrl+B); paws are scaled, bevelled cubes duplicated; ears built separately and rotated; tails are extrusions, bevelled, then shaded smooth; "a plain rounded-square look" is an accepted end state; a reply says around 3 segments is enough; another reports a curved tail "caused weird shading ... and more subdivisions fixed it" **(snippet)** — [DevForum 517174](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/creating-simulator-styled-pets-in-blender/517174)
- **Faces as textures:** "for your typical blocky pets they are super easy to make in blender ... all you'd need is a decal" **(snippet)** — [DevForum 864535](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/pet-suggestion/864535); a pet pack is "split into colored parts" with the face as a decal **(snippet)** — [DevForum 426398](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/100-good-quality-simulator-pets/426398); "Block pet modelers" thread: face decals "to get the best possible look" **(snippet)** — [DevForum 405839](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/block-pet-modelers/405839)
- **Face construction:** a free method in Photopea: a "512 ... by 512" canvas, ellipse shapes for the eyes with a black stroke **(snippet)** — [DevForum 1529987](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-a-simulator-pet-face-for-free/1529987); a Photoshop method: layered ovals and circles, a small white highlight circle "near the top of the pupil", then mirrored "to create a shiny effect to the eye" **(snippet)** — [DevForum 824714](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-pet-faces-photoshop-tutorial/824714); match the canvas to the face's shape: "At 512px wide, you'll get 341px tall for a face that is 3:2 in studs" **(snippet)** — [DevForum 727146](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/pet-face-pixel-size/727146); a pet set's default faces "include eyes, nose, cheeks" **(snippet)** — [DevForum 2716671](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/pet-faces-where-to-make-these/2716671)
- **Pixel faces on cube heads (owner's reference):** "two big eyes as ringed pixel discs on a coarse grid, small pink cheek squares and a tiny mouth, changed per character (worried, fierce, cheerful)" — repo `media/tiktok/NOTES.md` (ZPLSQAq5q)
- **Head-to-body:** ZPLSQAq5q cube head "about 40% of the body height"; ZPLSHwXqp's cat "a cube head about as wide as its body" — repo `media/tiktok/NOTES.md`
- **Limbs and appendages (owner's references):** block limbs, "faceted low-poly 'ball' hands and feet (small icospheres) and cone spikes", "thin gold bands as trim", an emblem "built as stepped pixel geometry (the lightning bolt is a staircase)" (ZPLSQAq5q); horns and tail as stair-stepped cube runs, a tail "made of dark navy cubes each carrying an orange stripe", feathers as "overlapping ... feather cards" (ZPLSHwXqp) — repo `media/tiktok/NOTES.md`
- **Palette:** ZPLSQAq5q: "flat saturated colours, three or four per model"; ZPLSHwXqp: "three hues and a near-black anchor each", the accent "across the wheel from the body" (hydra near-black navy #061147 with violet #6C08D2; dragon #0E4BA3 with cyan #45AFD0; phoenix #8B1A26 red with gold and navy ink #12101E; approximate, filmed off a monitor) — repo `media/tiktok/NOTES.md`. Adopt Me avoids dark tones **(snippet)** — [Mojo Nation](https://www.mojo-nation.com/uplift-games-vicky-huxley-and-sim-furniss-on-adopt-mes-journey-into-consumer-products/)
- **Outline and shading:** "No outline shader on any creature. The dark ink is built into the pattern" (ZPLSHwXqp) — repo `media/tiktok/NOTES.md`; Roblox has no toon shader; a `Highlight` on a grouped model gives an outline; the inverted-hull method is "very glitchy" **(snippet)** — [DevForum 1851704](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/adding-a-toon-shader/1851704); [DevForum 3972375](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/a-outline-effect-without-the-need-of-blender/3972375)
- **Surfaces:** flat colours and hand-painted textures (vendor claim for PS99) **(snippet)** — [Tripo3D](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/pet-simulator-99-3d-model); vertex colours are a Roblox-endorsed way to add detail to simple meshes, but they do not work under SurfaceAppearance **(snippet)** — repo `creature_models.md` lines 37 to 38
- **Texture budget:** Roblox guidance: 256×256 for about 5-stud objects, 512 for about 10, 1024 for about 20; mesh textures are bilinear-filtered, so pixel art blurs unless upscaled **(snippet)** — [Roblox docs: texture specifications](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/art/modeling/texture-specifications); [DevForum 2303754](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/mesh-texture-pixel-art/2303754)
- **Voxel cell, seen in frames:** the ZPL8MtkYu incubator egg is built of stacked rounded brick cells, each with an inset groove line around its face (olive cells with a band of pink cells); the dinosaurs are navy and grey block assemblies with orange stripe blocks; the ground is a tiled square-brick grid **(seen in frames)** — `media/tiktok/out/ZPL8MtkYu/sheet_02.jpg` (16 to 31 s)

### Inferences
A working spec for an original creature in sub-style A or E, synthesised from the findings above; every number is a starting value to tune, not a measured fact from a shipped game.
- **Blocks:** 3 to 8 boxes in all: head (or head-body), body, 0 or 4 feet, 1 to 3 appendage blocks. Keep all boxes axis-aligned; tilt only appendages, by 10 to 25 degrees.
- **Bevel:** the 2020 tutorial's 0.2 on Blender's 2-unit default cube is about 10% of the edge. Use one absolute bevel width on every block (about 8 to 12% of the head's shortest edge) with 2 to 3 segments. If small parts are bevelled in proportion to their own size instead, they come out round and the set looks inconsistent.
- **Proportions:** head height 40 to 60% of total height. Head width at least body width (cute) or about 40% of height on a biped (the ZPLSQAq5q titan). Legs 10 to 25% of total height, as short boxes set inside the body's footprint.
- **Appendages:** ears as wedges or thin slabs; horns as cones or stepped cube runs; wings as one thin slab or 2 to 3 overlapping "cards"; tails as a chain of 1 to 4 cubes, each smaller than the last, or one bevelled slab.
- **Face:** a texture on the front face of the head (512 px square, or matched to the face's ratio). Eyes take 20 to 30% of face width each and sit in the lower half; dark irises with one or two white highlight dots; an optional tiny mouth and pink cheek squares. On a pixel face, draw on a coarse grid (16 to 32 cells across the face) and upscale with nearest-neighbour before upload, because Roblox filters mesh textures.
- **Colour:** one body hue, one secondary (belly, ears, stripe), one accent, plus a near-black ink for eyes and seams. Rarity changes the material (gold, rainbow, neon regions) or the scale, never the mesh.
- **Shading:** flat or a soft vertical gradient; no outline shader; the dark ink and the bevel highlight separate the blocks.
- **Budget:** a few hundred to about 2,000 triangles (consistent with the convergence in repo `creature_models.md` line 122).

### Gaps
- No source gives measured head-to-body ratios, bevel-to-block ratios or eye sizes for actual PS99, Bee Swarm or Grow a Garden models; the spec above is synthesis.
- The exact face-texture resolution BIG Games or Onett use is not public.

## 4. How is "blocky" different from "simplistic", and what makes a blocky pet look finished and premium rather than cheap?

### Takeaway
"Blocky" is a constraint applied with discipline: a consistent unit and bevel, a strong silhouette, a face with highlights, and detail that grows with rarity. "Simplistic" is the same boxes without the rules: raw unbevelled parts, mismatched scales, a flat face and the same complexity at every tier. Premium blocky pets add secondary blocks, trim, a pixel or painted texture layer and effects as rarity rises, while the base shape stays clean.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Finished:** the same bevel width on every block; every block inside one grid of proportions (heights in halves or thirds of the head); a silhouette that reads as a black shape at 64 px; a face with highlights and an expression; one hero feature at 1.3 to 1.5 times the size realism would give it; 2 to 4 flat colours plus dark ink; and texture or geometry detail that sits *on* the big blocks (seams, inset grooves, trim bands, stripes, stepped emblems) without changing the outline.
- **Cheap:** unbevelled or unevenly bevelled boxes; parts that float or intersect visibly; a stock or flat dot face; too many hues; noisy or stretched textures; blurred pixel faces; every tier the same complexity; a generic animal with no hero feature.
- **Detail ladder by tier:** Common is 3 to 5 blocks and a face. Rare adds one secondary block (a crest, a backpack, a collar). Epic adds trim and a pattern (stripes, inset grooves, a stepped emblem). Legendary adds accessories plus an emissive line or cell. Mythic and Secret add scale, glow lines, a halo or ring, and articulated parts (beating wings, a segmented tail).
- **Originality:** the 2023 DMCA shows that BIG Games treats face, texture and model resemblance in the cube style as actionable. An original game should differ in the face design (eye shape, highlight placement, mouth), in silhouette (non-animal alien anatomy) and in surface motif, not only in colour.

### Gaps
- No interview with a BIG Games, Onett or Grow a Garden artist states quality rules; the rules above are drawn from hiring posts, a competitor's complaint and the owner's references.
