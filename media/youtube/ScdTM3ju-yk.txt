Today, I’ll be explaining what Future Is
Bright Phase 3.0 has done to continue revolutionizing realistic lighting in ROBLOX.
On the day of recording this video on August
1st, 2020, Future Is Bright Phase 3 is currently
in Beta testing, which means that any new
features that have been added will not show
up if you try to play the game outside of
Studio until it is fully released around October
to December of 2020.
I will only be showing the new features of
this phase, so if you are interested in what
Phase 2.5 added, you can go to the card at
the top right of the screen or find it through
the tutorial playlist in the description below.
To enable the new features of this update,
make sure you’ve turned on the Explorer
and Properties tabs through the “View”
tab at the top left of the screen.
From here, click on “Lighting”, go down
into the properties, and change the “Technology”
setting to say “Future”.
Make sure that you are using over 5 bars of
in-game graphics quality, or have the “Edit
Quality Level” through the “Rendering”
tab in ROBLOX Studio’s settings set to at
least level 10 or higher in order to see its
effects.
The largest improvements have been seen with
“PointLights”, “SpotLights”, and “SurfaceLights”.
I’ll be showing a before and after with
the old and new settings for each of these sections.
PointLights emit light from a single point
in every direction, so if we go into this
room here and change the PointLight’s properties
over here -- we click on it, go into the Properties
setting, and I change the “Range” property
to something higher, it’s going to reach
every single part of the room.
Since we’re using the Future setting, I’m
going to go into the Lighting and swap it
back, and this is what it looked like before
with “ShadowMap”, and this is what it
looks like with Future.
And if we go on the outside of this room,
you’ll notice that there is some slight
light leaking improvements, but that’s not
the same in every single case.
SpotLights emit light in a single direction
in an almost cone-like shape from the center point,
so if we go over here to this SpotLight
and we move it closer to something or change
its angle property, the point of light will
either become wider or narrower.
The shadow this now creates on other things
within the environment is now much more noticeable
and refined, so if I compare it over here
to ShadowMap, and this is Future -- ShadowMap
-- this is Future -- it is much more refined,
as shown through the shadows of some of my
YouTube Members trying to change out a lightbulb.
SurfaceLights emit light in a single direction
directly from the surface of the object it
is attached to -- you can choose any of the
surfaces (sides).
This happens in somewhat of a boxy shape,
as shown by these different lights here in
descending height.
SurfaceLights have seen similar changes to
the other lights if we compare the ShadowMap
versus the Future light setting, however,
these updates currently do not support shadows
from SurfaceLights, as shown from these differences
ahead.
While a SpotLight over here with “Shadows”
enabled will not shine through the part below
it, SurfaceLights don’t do that currently
during Future Is Bright Phase 3.
This might be something that gets supported
in the future, but that would be outside of
the Future Is Bright series.
Since there were major changes from this update,
there are things that you need to consider
if you want to use this in your games.
To start, only players that use 5 graphics
bars or higher in-game will be able to see
the Future Lighting.
Anyone that has below that amount will default
to ShadowMap or “Voxel” settings, which
could lead to certain cases like the posts
shown on the screen from other Developers.
However, it has been noted by one of the ROBLOX
Staff members that this could be improved
in the future, but it would not be part of
the Future Is Bright updates.
Another thing to remember is that since this
lighting update makes some things look more
realistic, it could make your games laggier
and limit overall performance, which might
make it difficult for some players who might
have lower-end PCs or on a mobile device,
to enjoy your game on a graphics setting that
allows them to see the new changes.
One user has found a drastic decrease in performance
in their game, going from around 200 all the
way down to 100 FPS on their Menu Screen.
There will hopefully still be performance
improvements made to these new settings from
ROBLOX, but it will likely remain laggier
than other lighting settings.
Also, it was mentioned in the original post
by ROBLOX Staff that shadowed point lights
will cause worse performance than shadowed
spot lights, which is probably because point
lights emit light in all directions, so keep
that in mind when you’re selecting what
lights to use.
Remember that you will likely need to make
a lot of adjustments in your game to make
sure that you are able to see during different
times of day, primarily inside of buildings
that were reliant on few to no light sources.
There are also some bugs when swapping between
Future and ShadowMap, so keep that in mind
and make sure you’re checking between both
(settings) so that you know what certain users
will see on lower graphics settings.
