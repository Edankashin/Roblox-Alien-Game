# Stack blocks, scale height, stage the reveal

In the top Roblox pet games, "blocky" is a strict way of building, not a cheap look. A creature is a handful of axis-aligned boxes that share one bevel (or one voxel cell size), with a head as big as the body, stubby block limbs, and a face painted as a texture on the front of the head. That describes the Pet Simulator lineage, whose owner sent takedown notices in 2023 over copied "cube style" pets; the "voxel characters" of Steal a Brainrot; and the cube-cell and pixel-face creatures in Ethan's own reference clips. The smooth fused-skin aliens Ethan rejected come from the opposite tradition, Adopt Me's organic one. They read as "simplistic" for the same reason cheap blocky pets do: they lacked the rules that make a simple shape look deliberate, namely one unit, one bevel, a face with a highlight, and detail that grows with rank. The top games make their rarest pets worth finding mainly through **scale and ceremony**. Pet Simulator 99 climbs in 2x to 3x jumps from normal pets to Huge, Titanic and Gargantuan, about **1 : 2.7 : 8 : 24** on community mesh scales. Its biggest classes are ridable, it puts glow on chosen body parts with a colour cycle on top, and it plays its rarest hatches to the whole server or to every server. The most valuable pets across these games are majestic, iconic or absurd at least as often as they are menacing. The alien game should grow detail tier by tier, from about **5 to 10 blocks on a Common to 100 to 180 on a Cosmic or Secret**. It should step height from about **2 studs to 10 to 20 studs** instead of today's 1.5x, move effects outward from the body to the ground to the sky, and end the ladder with a moment everyone sees. Blocky is also the style where Claude's tools work best, because a box creature is a table of numbers. An Obsidian note holds the parts table, Claude Design draws it as an exact orthographic sheet for Ethan to approve, and headless Blender builds from the same table and is checked against the sheet. One caveat applies to everything said about other games: no image of a Pet Simulator 99, Bee Swarm, Grow a Garden or Steal a Brainrot model could be opened, and the DevForum and fan wikis could be read only as search snippets. Claims about how those games build their pets are therefore agreement across community sources plus inference, while the Roblox engine facts come from the Creator Docs source, read in full.

## "Blocky" is a construction rule, and Ethan's references sit at its voxel end

Five sub-styles of "blocky" can be told apart in the genre, and they differ on two dials: **how big the building unit is** (one box per body part, or many small equal cubes) and **where the detail lives** (in the geometry, or in a pixel texture on a few boxes). The bevelled-cube "simulator pet" is the most documented and most copied. The canonical DevForum tutorial, built on Pet Simulator 2 pets, starts from one cube, bevels its side edges ("I beveled mine .2 ... and I did 6 segments"), makes paws from scaled, bevelled cubes duplicated four times, builds the ears separately, and accepts "a plain rounded-square look" as a finished state ([DevForum 517174](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/creating-simulator-styled-pets-in-blender/517174)). The face is a decal: "for your typical blocky pets ... all you'd need is a decal" ([DevForum 864535](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/pet-suggestion/864535)). The style has a whole market behind it, from a "Cube Pets - 30 Animated Blocky Pets" pack to hiring posts paying **15K to 25K Robux per set** of "blocky-style pets" ([clearlydev](https://clearlydev.com/product/cube-pets-30-animated-blocky-pets); [DevForum 762736](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/hiring-experienced-3d-modelers-to-create-blocky-style-pets-15k-25k-per-set/762736)).

Ethan's references all sit at the **voxel and pixel end**, not at the smooth bevelled end. ZPL8MtkYu's dinosaur ranch tiles a brick grid over "every surface, including the ground ... so even simple geometry looks deliberate". ZPLSHwXqp's Void Hydra is made of equal cubes, each **about 1/30 to 1/40 of the creature's height**, and dissolves cell by cell. ZPLSQAq5q's titans are stacks of bevelled cubes with a cube head **about 40% of body height**, block limbs, cone spikes, gold trim bands, a lightning-bolt emblem built as a staircase, and a pixel-art face with ringed pixel-disc eyes and pink cheek squares ([NOTES.md](../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)). The biggest new hits lean the same way. Steal a Brainrot, "the only Roblox game to have surpassed 25 million concurrent users", is described as voxel ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)), and Grow a Garden's pets are sold as "pixelated, blocky" figures ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/market/grow_a_garden_roblox_png)). That suggests the mass market is drifting from "bevelled cube plus decal face" toward chunkier voxel models with pixel-style textures, but the visual evidence is thin, and no source ranks the sub-styles by how often they appear in the top 100.

The rejected redesigns were the organic method applied to the whole roster: one fused skin per species, smoothed and decimated. Ethan's verdict on them, "too simplistic and not high enough quality", together with his call for "a blocky type of look like in a lot of popular roblox games", is now Decision B43, which also states that rank shows by "more detail, more height and more VFX per tier", not by a fiercer face ([Decisions.md](../docs/vault/07-alien-game/Decisions.md)). The fix is not to add polygons to the smooth skins. It is to switch to a style whose rules can be enforced.

Blocky also carries an **originality risk** the smooth style did not. In September 2023 BIG Games sent takedown notices to several games with "a blocky pet system like Pet Simulator X's", saying their "pet models, images, and textures" showed "resemblances beyond coincidence". One developer was told to replace "nearly 300 pet designs" and complained that "the cube style barely allows for differences beyond the faces" ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/roblox/pet-simulator-x-creator-under-fire-for-hypocritical-dmca-on-roblox-games-2284798/)). Steal a Brainrot's AI-derived characters now face a public copyright question of their own ([Yahoo/AT&T](https://currently.att.yahoo.com/att/copyright-ai-slop-inside-roblox-153853304.html)). Since the face carries most of a cube pet's identity, the alien roster has to differ in three places, not only in colour. Its face needs its own eye shape, highlight placement and mouth. Its silhouette should follow alien anatomy that no animal pet has. Its surface needs its own motif, such as a per-world cell pattern or element glyphs.

## Premium blocky is one unit, one bevel and a face with a highlight

Nothing separates "blocky" from "simplistic" except discipline, and the line can be written down. A finished blocky pet keeps **the same bevel width on every block**, sizes every block on one grid of proportions, reads as a black shape at thumbnail size, has a face with highlights and an expression, and exaggerates one hero feature. Its detail sits *on* the big blocks (seams, inset grooves, trim bands, stripes, stepped emblems) without changing the outline. A cheap one has unbevelled or unevenly bevelled boxes, parts that float or visibly intersect, a flat dot face, too many hues, blurry pixel textures and the same complexity at every tier. Those lists are drawn from the tutorial grammar, the owner's references and reviewer complaints, since no top studio publishes its rules. DevForum reviewers flag uneven eyes most often, and they split on smooth shading: "you shouldn't shade smooth as it ruins the shading", against "making it smooth like that is a part of the simulator pet style" ([DevForum 1267428](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-simulator-pet/1267428); [DevForum 2748969](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-pet-faces/2748969)). The one studio that states a creature rule, Uplift, says to boil each pet "down to its most distinguishing feature and then enhance and exaggerate it" ([Mojo Nation](https://www.mojo-nation.com/uplift-games-vicky-huxley-and-sim-furniss-on-adopt-mes-journey-into-consumer-products/)). That rule matters even more for boxes, where a horn, an ear shape or a tail chain has to do the work anatomy would do.

The working grammar below combines the tutorial, the references and measurements made in Blender 5.0.1 for this research. Every value is a starting value to tune in Studio, not a figure measured from a shipped game.

| Element | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Blocks | A Common is 3 to 8 boxes: head (or head and body in one), body, 0 or 4 feet, 1 to 3 appendage blocks; boxes stay axis-aligned, and only appendages tilt, by 10 to 25 degrees | Tutorial grammar ([DevForum 517174](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/creating-simulator-styled-pets-in-blender/517174)) |
| Bevel | One absolute width for the whole creature, **6 to 10% of the smallest side** of the main masses, 1 or 2 segments; small detail blocks stay plain | The tutorial's 0.2 on Blender's 2-unit cube is 10%; Blockbench's Beveled Cuboid defaults to a 12.5% chamfer ([add_mesh.ts](https://github.com/JannisX11/blockbench/blob/master/js/modeling/mesh/add_mesh.ts)) |
| Proportion | Head 40 to 60% of total height and at least as wide as the body; legs 10 to 25% of height, set inside the body's footprint | ZPLSQAq5q's cube head at about 40% ([NOTES.md](../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)) |
| Appendages | Ears as wedges or slabs; horns as cones or stepped cube runs; wings as one slab or 2 to 3 overlapping cards; tails as 1 to 4 shrinking cubes | ZPLSHwXqp's stepped horns, cube-chain tails and feather cards ([NOTES.md](../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)) |
| Face | A texture on the front of the head; eyes 20 to 30% of face width each, in the lower half, dark with one or two white highlight pixels; a small mouth and cheek squares; drawn on a 16 to 32 cell grid | Face tutorials ([DevForum 824714](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-pet-faces-photoshop-tutorial/824714); [DevForum 727146](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/pet-face-pixel-size/727146)); ZPLSQAq5q's pixel face |
| Colour | Three or four flat colours per model (body, secondary, an accent across the colour wheel from the body) plus a near-black ink | ZPLSHwXqp: "three hues and a near-black anchor each" ([NOTES.md](../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)) |
| Outline and shading | No outline shader; the dark ink and the bevel's highlight separate the blocks; a `Highlight` only where an outline is needed | Roblox has no toon shader, and the inverted-hull trick is "very glitchy" ([DevForum 1851704](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/adding-a-toon-shader/1851704)) |

The bevel rule is also the budget rule, because bevel segments multiply triangles fast. Measured in Blender 5.0.1 for this research, a plain cube is **12 triangles**, a 1-segment chamfer 44, 2 segments 108, 3 segments 188, and the tutorial's 6 segments **588 per cube**. A nine-box pet comes to 396, 972 or 1,692 triangles at 1, 2 or 3 segments. Six-segment bevels on every cube would fit only four cubes into the project's 2,500-triangle cap, while 180 plain cubes come to 2,160. The workable rule is to bevel only the two to four big masses (body, head) at 1 or 2 segments, keep every detail block plain, and fake small bevels and cell grooves with a painted edge highlight in the texture, which costs nothing. Roblox's own hard limit is **20,000 triangles per mesh** ([specifications.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/modeling/specifications.md)). For a roster of blocks, the real cost is draw calls (one per MeshPart, per unique mesh-and-texture pair, per emitter), not triangles. Identical meshes share a draw call only when their mesh and SurfaceAppearance match, and "decals, textures, and particles don't batch well" ([improve.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/performance-optimization/improve.md)).

The pixel face, the element that most marks a cube pet as premium, has a new engine route. A plain `MeshPart.TextureID` is always bilinear-filtered, so pixel art blurs, and developers have fought this with nearest-neighbour upscales that leave it "still a bit blurry" and eat memory ([DevForum 2303754](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/mesh-texture-pixel-art/2303754)). On **5 May 2026** the Creator Docs added `SurfaceAppearance.ResampleMode`, which "when set to `Pixelated` ... preserve[s] the sharp edges of pixels using nearest-neighbor filtering" ([SurfaceAppearance.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/SurfaceAppearance.yaml)). A 2025 feature request still described textures on models as "always blurred" ([DevForum 4028314](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/pixelated-resamplemode-for-surfaceappearances-pixel-art-meshes/4028314)), and no live test on phones was found, so this is the first thing the Mac session should check. The same class makes rarity recolours free: with `AlphaMode = TintMask`, `SurfaceAppearance.Color` tints only where the map's alpha is opaque ([surface-appearance.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/modeling/surface-appearance.md)). The alpha can mark the body as tintable and leave the face untouched, so a Gold or Crystal overlay needs no new upload.

The market already prices detail as a ladder. A 2021 modeller charged "1.5$ per simple pet, 5$ per not very detailed pet, 8$ per very detailed pet" ([DevForum 1140550](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/pet-modeler-for-hire/1140550)). One Roblox studio's hiring brief stated the rule Ethan asked for almost word for word, "as rarity increases, so should the complexity of the pet", for a set split "2 common, 2 rare, 2 epic, 3 legendary, and 1 mythic" ([DevForum 762736](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/hiring-experienced-3d-modelers-to-create-blocky-style-pets-15k-25k-per-set/762736); [DevForum 1402714](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/open-hiring-3d-modelers-for-pets-2100-usd-600k-robux-pay/1402714)).

The surprise in the reference games is how little of the rarity signal is new modelling. Their rarest pets are mostly the same or similar meshes made **much bigger**, given **glow on chosen parts**, and introduced with **a ceremony that other players see**. The table gathers the levers that are documented. Each line comes from community sources, and the conflicts are noted.

Two patterns follow, and both matter for the alien game. First, **size is the loudest signal and it moves in jumps**. Each PS99 step is 2x to 3x, Gargantuans were still being released in July 2026 ([holdtoreset](https://holdtoreset.com/pet-simulator-99-updates-details-list)), and one Titanic dropped at "a mere 0.05% chance" ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/pet-simulator-99-how-get-titanic-spongebob-squarepants)). Once a pet is too big to trot beside the player, it becomes a mount. The project's ladder is far flatter: a tier scale of 1.0 to 1.5, so that a Huge, fully grown Cosmic stands at only 2.35 times a Common ([Roster-Redesign.md](../docs/vault/01-game-design/Roster-Redesign.md)). A default avatar is "approximately 5 studs tall" ([DevForum 2205072](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/fetch-player-height/2205072)), and on that scale the current top tier never towers over anyone.

Second, **rarity tier and variant finish are separate axes**. Rarity picks the species, whose design carries the spectacle (dragons, angels, a hero silhouette). The variant (Gold, Rainbow, Shiny, Neon) is a material treatment on the same mesh. None of the reference games was found to give every tier an automatic aura. Their particles mostly arrive as traits or mutations, and their glow as colour on chosen parts, which is also the cheapest effect on phones. So the project's per-tier effect ladder already goes further than the references. That is fine, because Ethan asked for VFX, but the size and presence levers are where the alien game lags.

The evidence also backs Ethan's correction that rare should mean "worth finding", not "fiercer". Adopt Me's most valuable pets in 2026 are the Shadow Dragon, Bat Dragon, Giraffe and Frost Dragon ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/adopt-me-trading-values-in-2026-pet-economics-rising-legendaries-scam-prevention)). PS99's priciest marketplace listings in mid-2026 were a Gargantuan Nyan Cat at $845 and a Titanic Nightmare Cat at $300 ([Eldorado](https://www.eldorado.gg/blog/roblox-en/pet-simulator-99-values/)). Steal a Brainrot's top OG tier includes Meowl, Strawberry Elephant and Skibidi Toilet next to the Headless Horseman ([Lootester](https://lootester.com/steal-a-brainrot-tier-list/)). By name, roughly a third of these read as menacing and the rest as majestic, cute-iconic or absurd. That count is an impression from names, not a systematic tally, but it is enough to show a cute face on a huge, glowing, rare body still reads as a prize.

## A seven-tier alien ladder adds blocks, studs and light at every step

Putting the grammar and the top games' levers together gives a ladder for the game's seven tiers. Each rung changes three things a player can see from across the map: how many blocks the creature has, how tall it stands against a 5-stud avatar, and how far its light reaches. The numbers are proposals to try on a review sheet, not measurements.

| Tier | Blocks and the detail added | Height | Effects (outward from the body) | Presence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common | 5 to 10 blocks; one hero block; pixel face; 2 colours plus ink | about 2 studs (0.4 avatar) | none, or one blink | none |
| Uncommon | 8 to 15; a two-tone body; one secondary block (crest, backpack, collar) | about 2.25 studs | one touch in a second colour | coloured tier word |
| Rare | 15 to 30; a stamped element glyph on the back, Bee Swarm's star idea; one glossy trim slot | about 2.5 to 3 studs | a soft light | name in the tier colour |
| Epic | 30 to 60; trim bands, stripes, inset grooves, a stepped emblem; a glow region on 2 or 3 extremities | about 3.5 to 4 studs | light, glow, one emitter, kept **on the body** | outlined plate |
| Legendary | 60 to 100; accessories and emissive lines or cells drawn into the pattern | about 5 to 6 studs (one avatar) | adds orbiting particles and a ground ring: **touches the ground** | in-server banner |
| Cosmic | 100 to 180; articulated parts (beating wing cards, a segmented tail), a halo or floating accessory, colour-cycled glow | 10 to 20 studs (2 to 4 avatars), ridable | adds a trail and a beam: **reaches the sky** | an in-server sky moment |
| Secret | as Cosmic, in a dark palette with one accent; glow cycles only within its own palette | 10 to 20 studs, ridable | the Cosmic set in shadow colours | a cross-server line with no username |

The block counts follow a Blockbench MCP's own complexity budget ("prop 30-60, mob 100-180, hero 180-300+") ([sosadly/blockbench-mcp](https://github.com/sosadly/blockbench-mcp)), capped so that plain 12-triangle detail cubes plus two bevelled masses stay inside the project's 2,500-triangle cap. Merging touching cells into one mesh also deletes their hidden faces, which leaves headroom. Detail climbs in the order the references and hiring posts suggest. A secondary block comes first, then a stamped mark, then trim and pattern, then emissive cells, and finally articulated parts. That makes "more detail" something a script can count rather than a matter of taste. One option deepens this: shrink the voxel cell as the tier rises, chunky cells about 1/8 to 1/12 of the height on Commons and finer stepped ornament on Cosmic and Secret. Ethan's Void Hydra reference uses 1/30 to 1/40 ([NOTES.md](../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)), but at that size the cells must live in the texture, or be merged with a planar decimate, to stay within budget.

The height column swaps the current 1.0 to 1.5 scale for the jumps the top games use. Common to Legendary is about 2.5x and Common to Secret about 5x to 10x. That is gentler than PS99's 24x Gargantuan but steep enough that a top-tier alien towers over the player, as the Mythic and Secret creatures in ZPLSHwXqp do at "about four avatar heights" ([NOTES.md](../media/tiktok/NOTES.md)). Above Legendary, the alien should stand at full size in the wild, at the reveal and as a mount, which the game already has. As a follower, it should use a capped companion scale, because PS99's answer to a pet too big to follow is to ride it. The existing size roll (Tiny to Huge) still adds variety within a tier. One rarer roll at about 2x would copy the top games' discontinuous size classes, but it needs an original name, not Titanic, Gargantuan or Colossal.

The effects column keeps the project's rule that an Epic's effects stay on its body, a Legendary's touch the ground and a Cosmic's reach the sky ([Roster-Redesign.md](../docs/vault/01-game-design/Roster-Redesign.md)). Riot's VFX guide gives the principle behind it: visual weight must track importance, a big moment and a basic one "should not be swapped", and every effect has bold primary elements and quieter secondary ones ([Riot VFX style guide](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2017/10/dev-leagues-vfx-style-guide/)). Phones set the hard limits. An emitter makes at most **100 particles a second on mobile** and the on-screen count is Rate × Lifetime ([ParticleEmitter](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/ParticleEmitter#Rate)), so a Cosmic at 60 a second with 2-second lifetimes holds 120 live particles and at 5 seconds holds 300. `:Emit()` bursts skip Roblox's automatic quality reduction ([DevForum 968599](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/custom-particles-emitting-system/968599)). A burst of about 300 particles near the camera caused **18 ms spikes** ([DevForum 2543010](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/particleemitteremit-causes-huge-lag-spikes-when-camera-is-close-to-particleemitter/2543010)), which matters because the reveal and the codex put the camera close. Flipbooks are switched off automatically "when [clients] are low on memory, which is likely for older mobile phones" ([particle-emitters.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/effects/particle-emitters.md)). The first `Highlight` costs "up to 1 millisecond of GPU time on mobile devices", and adding or removing one forces a rebuild, so it should be toggled, not created ([highlighting.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/effects/highlighting.md)). All of this favours Adopt Me's approach: glow regions and colour cycles first, particles second, and reveal bursts scaled by the client's quality level.

The face holds the line Ethan drew. Every tier keeps a cute pixel face with big eyes. The top tiers earn awe from height, a larger crest or halo, and light, not from a narrowed brow, and B43 retires the temperament column as the design driver ([Decisions.md](../docs/vault/07-alien-game/Decisions.md)). The presence column copies the three rungs the top games use above an in-server banner: a sky moment everyone in the server sees (PS99's Gargantuan), and a cross-server line without the finder's name (BGSI), which on Roblox means a server-side cross-server message. Whether to show names is for the project's safety review to decide. A cheap collection cue rounds it off: Bee Swarm's codex shows no star for an undiscovered gifted bee, a faded star once it has been seen, and a black star once owned ([BSS wiki](https://bee-swarm-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Gifted_Bee)).

## Spec first: Obsidian holds the numbers, Claude Design draws them, Blender builds them

### Claude's builds fail from pictures and work from tables

The order of the pipeline follows from where Claude-plus-Blender breaks. In a 2026 test, simply asking an AI model driving Blender to "create this based on this image" gave "a blocky model made of cubes, cylinders, spheres" that missed the curves of the reference ([Zenn](https://zenn.dev/test_myname/articles/gpt-5-6-blender-mcp-modeling?locale=en)). A single image also leaves the back and sides to guesswork ([lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/blender-mcp-practice)). That failure *is* the target style: a blocky creature is primitives by design. So blocky is where scripted building is strongest, provided the numbers (sizes, offsets, pivots, which side is left) come from a table and the image is used only to check the result. Image-to-3D generators are the wrong starting point here, because they return dense smooth meshes that would have to be rebuilt as blocks. Every step below therefore reads and writes one spec, and nothing under `assets/models` changes until Ethan approves a sheet, as B43 requires ([Decisions.md](../docs/vault/07-alien-game/Decisions.md)).

| Step | Tool | What it makes | Gate to pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Style bible | Obsidian | `06-art-pipelines/Blocky-Style.md`: numbered rules, each with a number and a do/don't pair, plus the ladder table | Ethan approves the bible |
| 2. Spec note | Obsidian | One note per creature: frontmatter and a parts table in studs | `status: concept` |
| 3. Concept board | Claude Design | One canvas per world: orthographic views drawn from the parts table, palette, pixel face, silhouette strip, tier ladder; 2 or 3 options per species | Ethan picks; `status: board`, artboard link saved |
| 4. Build | Blender (headless bpy) | GLB, four orthographic renders and a three-quarter view, a silhouette match per view, block and triangle counts written back | `status: build` |
| 5. Review | All three | Renders placed beside the sheet on the canvas; one dated verdict line per review in the note | `status: review`, then `approved` |
| 6. Ship | Studio | GLB under `assets/models/<Id>/`, with a data row that cites the note | Only an approved note ships |

### The style bible and one spec note per creature live in Obsidian

Published style guides agree on what a roster bible must fix with rules rather than adjectives. Valve's 2012 Dota 2 character guide puts silhouette first and says value (lightness) is often more important than hue ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2012/06/valve-releases-useful-character-design-guide)). Roblox's own curriculum calls an art style "visual ground rules", warns "don't always rely on color alone", and gives props "only enough geometry that users can tell what a prop is from its silhouette" ([choose-an-art-style.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/tutorials/curriculums/user-interface-design/choose-an-art-style.md); [develop-polished-assets.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/develop-polished-assets.md)). A Claude Code "art-bible" skill asks for "5–7 colors with roles — not just hex codes, but what each color means" and for each reference source, what to take and "what to explicitly avoid" ([art-bible SKILL.md](https://github.com/Donchitos/Claude-Code-Game-Studios/blob/main/.claude/skills/art-bible/SKILL.md)). For this roster, the bible's rules are seven: shape language (boxes only, the bevel rule, one hero block per species unique within its world); proportions in studs on a 0.25-stud grid; a palette with roles, checked in greyscale before hue; the rarity ladder table above; VFX rules (primary and secondary elements, the per-tier particle caps, effects never hiding the face); a do/don't board per rule; and a silhouette test, with every species of a world shown as a 64 px black shape in one row, failing any two that read alike. No Roblox pet studio has published its own creature bible, so this one is assembled from general practice.

Each creature's note has frontmatter that serves both as its row in the roster view and as the build's input. The fields are id, world, tier, status, height in studs, block and triangle counts, palette hexes, hero block, artboard link, render link and GLB path, with the parts table and a dated Reviews list in the body. Obsidian's core Bases plugin turns those notes into a roster with no extra plugins. Cards view takes its image from a property that may be "a link to a local attachment", "an external link" or "a hex color code", so a species shows its main colour as its card until a render exists ([Cards view.md](https://github.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/blob/master/en/Bases/Layouts/Cards%20view.md)). Kanban view (Obsidian 1.14) shows notes as "cards organized into columns based on a grouped property", which makes it a concept-to-approved board ([Views.md](https://github.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/blob/master/en/Bases/Views.md)), and the Mac already runs **Obsidian 1.14.4** ([Tools-Status.md](../docs/vault/02-how-we-work/Tools-Status.md)). A `.base` file is plain YAML of filters, formulas and views ([Bases syntax.md](https://github.com/obsidianmd/obsidian-help/blob/master/en/Bases/Bases%20syntax.md)), so cloud workers can write it without the app. A lint can refuse `approved` on any note that lacks an artboard link, a render and a review line, which turns "every design passes through all three" from a habit into a check.

### Claude Design draws measured sheets, not paintings

Claude Design is now a canvas of HTML artboards inside every Claude conversation, out of beta since 8 October 2026. The standalone site, the only place with a 3D mode, closes on **14 December 2026**, and its projects do not migrate ([release notes](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12138966-release-notes); [migration](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/17440474-migrate-from-standalone-claude-design-to-claude)). Claude "doesn't generate photos or illustrations the way image-generation tools do"; its visuals are HTML and SVG ([Claude help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9002504-can-claude-produce-images)). That is a weakness for painted concept art and exactly right for a blocky creature, whose front, side and top views are axis-aligned rectangles that SVG draws without error. The Design type's own rules allow "one `<svg>` ... where nobody will move or retype a part: an icon, a logo, an illustration", with labels as separate positioned elements, and `data-props` controls such as a colour picker let a palette be adjusted from the properties panel ([Design type](https://claude.ai/artifact/QKN21svewxgyPb6SYRqWnd)). A board drawn this way is a measured drawing. Every block's width, height and offset can be read off the stud grid, which is what a box modeller needs and a painted concept never gives. A 16 × 16 pixel face is just 256 SVG rectangles.

Each species' sheet should carry the four orthographic views (front, right, top, back) at one scale on a shared baseline, facing -Z as Roblox and Blockbench do. It needs a stud grid (major lines every 1 stud, minor every 0.25, total height printed), the parts table it was drawn from, the palette with each hex's role, the face as a pixel grid, a greyscale silhouette strip at thumbnail size, and the tier ladder: the same base figure with blocks added, height stepped and the emissive overlay shown. Generate the drawing from the note's parts table, not freehand, so that the board and the build read the same numbers. One canvas per world, redrawn only when the numbers change, keeps usage within plan limits, since Design "counts toward the same shared usage limits as the rest of Claude, including Claude Code" ([Get started](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14604416-get-started-with-claude-design)). No public example yet shows Claude Design producing creature turnarounds, and whether it keeps 30 or more parts aligned is unknown until the first sheet is drawn.

### Blender builds from the table and checks against the sheet

The build runs as headless `bpy` in cloud workers (bpy 5.0.1 there; the Mac has Blender 5.2.2 with the Blender MCP for live looks, per [Setup-and-Tips.md](../docs/vault/00-start-here/Setup-and-Tips.md)). Roblox's own Blender guide sets Scene Units to None so that one Blender unit is one stud, and the Studio importer's scale unit to Stud ([blender.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/blender.md)); a stud is "about 28 cm" ([greybox tutorial](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/tutorials/curriculums/core/building/greybox-a-playable-area.md)). A public Roblox asset library in this exact style works the same way, with "1 Blender unit = 1 stud", "pets 2–5 studs long", "square pixel eyes (black square with a white highlight)" and curves of "8 or fewer" sides ([7llamaking-collab/Roblox](https://github.com/7llamaking-collab/Roblox)). Its author and quality are unverified, but it confirms the recipe. The builder makes each part as a cube primitive with the bevel rule above, mirrors paired parts, and applies scale before export. Round bits use cheap primitives: an icosphere at subdivision 1 is 20 triangles and a 6-sided cone 10 (measured). Each moving part (wing, tail, ear) stays a separate object with its origin on its hinge, so the game's procedural idle can rotate it without a rig, as Decision B39 already specifies ([Decisions.md](../docs/vault/07-alien-game/Decisions.md)). That also avoids a measured engine cost: skinned meshes that move in a Model without a Humanoid "must be continually added to and removed from" spatial clusters ([improve.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/performance-optimization/improve.md)). For a voxel look on a curved form, Blender's Remesh in Blocks mode at depth 4 followed by a planar decimate turned a sphere into **886 triangles** (measured), in budget, though its UVs must be redone.

Surfaces follow the texture rules. Roblox suggests "256×256 for smaller 5×5 stud objects" ([texture-specifications.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/art/modeling/texture-specifications.md)). A face cell at 16 px per stud, Blockbench's convention, is 32 or 64 px square, shipped through a SurfaceAppearance set to `Pixelated`, with a nearest-neighbour upscale as the fallback if the Mac test fails. Bodies draw from one shared palette atlas, with the inset grooves and edge highlights painted in, and overlays come from `TintMask`. Every follower part gets `CollisionFidelity = Box` (scripts cannot change it at runtime), collision, touch and query off, and shadows off on small moving parts ([CollisionFidelity.yaml](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/enums/CollisionFidelity.yaml); [improve.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/performance-optimization/improve.md)). Static parts merge into one mesh per species, with moving parts limited to two or three extra MeshParts.

Effects come from the same Blender pipeline. Particle flipbooks are rendered frames packed into a 1024 sheet (an 8 × 8 sheet holds 64 frames, played at up to 30 fps), with transparent spacing between frames ([particle-emitters.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/effects/particle-emitters.md)); one user kept the art about 8 px inside each 128 px cell ([DevForum 1718023](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/animated-particles-with-the-particle-flipbooks-beta/1718023?page=4)). Mesh effects are low-poly rings, light columns, zig-zag ribbons and shards with a gradient alpha, made double-sided and animated by tweens in Roblox. That is what Ethan's VFX reference ZPLSQ1o7Q shows ([NOTES.md](../media/tiktok/NOTES.md); [DevForum 3941014](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-do-i-make-a-transparency-gradient-in-blender/3941014)).

The check closes the loop without anyone judging pixels. The builder renders the four orthographic views at the sheet's scale and scores each view's silhouette overlap with the board's exported PNG. Two open-source tools now do exactly this, one Blender server's `blender_compare_ref` and a Blockbench server's `compare_reference` with "add/trim mass here" advice ([thanhdoancong127/blender-mcp](https://github.com/thanhdoancong127/blender-mcp); [sosadly/blockbench-mcp](https://github.com/sosadly/blockbench-mcp)), and both are easy to copy in bpy with a mask render. No source gives an accepted pass mark, so 0.9 per view is a first guess to tune. People are still needed at two points: at the board, where taste decides whether the face reads cute and the hero block is exaggerated enough, and at the render, where VFX feel is judged.

Blockbench has a supporting role at most. It has no Bevel action ([en.json](https://github.com/JannisX11/blockbench/blob/master/lang/en.json)), its Beveled Cuboid is a single chamfer, and its route into Roblox draws reports of wrong scale, rejected textures and animations that do not carry over ([DevForum 2462295](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-do-i-import-models-from-blockbench-into-roblox/2462295); [DevForum 3639381](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-import-model-with-animations-that-are-made-in-blockbench-into-studio/3639381)). A headless `.bbmodel` server now exists, but it lists no glTF export and needs a GPU to render ([headless README](https://github.com/jasonjgardner/blockbench-mcp-plugin/blob/main/headless/README.md)). Blender stays the builder. Blockbench is an optional desk on the Mac for hand-painting a face.

## Conclusion

The switch to blocky changes more than the look. It makes art quality something the pipeline can check. With smooth skins, "too simplistic" was a judgement nobody could measure. With boxes, the qualities Ethan asked for are numbers a script can verify: block count per tier, one bevel width, height in studs, glow regions per species and silhouette overlap with an approved sheet. The rejected roster did not fail for lack of polygons. It failed for lack of a rule, and blocky is the first style in which the rule and the build share the same table. The real originality risk sits elsewhere: in a genre whose leader has sent takedown notices over resemblance in "the cube style", the face, the alien silhouette and the surface motif are the parts that must be visibly the game's own.

The bigger lesson is that the gap between this roster and the top games is less about detail than about steepness. The current ladder rises 50% in size across seven tiers. The reference games rise 2x to 3x per step, make the giants ridable, and end with a moment the whole server sees. Retuning height and presence costs a data table, not a modelling pass, and is likely to do more for "worth finding" than any amount of trim. Four cheap probes should come before the roster is rebuilt, because each settles a question no source answered: whether `ResampleMode = Pixelated` holds on a real phone, whether one Claude Design canvas can draw an aligned orthographic sheet from a parts table, whether one Common and one Cosmic make it through the full six-step loop, and what frame time and draw-call count a phone shows with 50 and 100 blocky aliens on screen.
