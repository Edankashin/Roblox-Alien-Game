# Give Aliens Jobs and One Currency

Neither of the team's two models should ship as described. The evidence favors a hybrid that keeps Model 1's spine (aliens have jobs, and rarer aliens do those jobs faster, several per station) and Model 2's legibility (one soft currency and a plain shop), then adds the ingredient both models lack: a per-planet "key" component that can only be found by exploring. Concretely, aliens assigned to camp stations generate a single currency (placeholder: Scrap) at a rate set by their job level; the ship console sells generic module blueprints for Scrap; but each module also needs one planet-unique material found in the wild, and it only assembles while aliens with the matching job stand in its slots, at a speed equal to their summed work speed. This matters because the two most-played games in Roblox history, Grow a Garden (**22.3M peak CCU**) and Steal a Brainrot (**25.87M**, the Guinness record), are single-currency loops ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden); [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/503322-most-concurrent-players-for-a-videogame-made-in-roblox)), while Minecraft-style crafting on Roblox has a documented decline history; yet a pure income model reproduces Steal a Brainrot's **nine-orders-of-magnitude** ladder ($1/s to $1B/s) in which commons are obsolete within an hour ([games.gg](https://games.gg/steal-a-brainrot/guides/steal-a-brainrot-complete-brainrot-tier-list/)). The report proposes a six-tier ladder spanning roughly **12x from Common to Legendary** plus a relative-scaled top tier, with commons kept useful by station slots, duplicate fusion, codex payouts and visual overlays that can land on any body. Exploration pays off when rarity is gated by biome, time, weather and tool rather than by an egg, and when Legendary spawns are server-announced appointments. The right server shape is the one the 2025 hits used: **8-player public planets** with one persistent camp per player, reserved servers for Party co-op, no trading at launch, and social features that work without chat now that Roblox segments chat by age. Monetize certainty, capacity and cosmetics and keep randomness in the free loop, because Roblox now requires per-item numeric odds for anything paid-random, already blocks it for UK under-18s, all Australians and Brazilian minors, and the EU's September 2026 KIDS Act proposal would ban it for minors outright ([Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/09/eu-kids-act-online-child-safety-proposal)). The two-person MVP is one handcrafted planet, about 15 aliens across 4 jobs and 5 tiers, 5 ship modules, a trainee-style tutorial, offline accrual, and analytics aimed at the 28-day retention signals Roblox's 2026 algorithm rewards. Evidence is thin in three places: no head-to-head retention data exists for exploration versus hatching, no Roblox precedent for a build-the-ship-to-leave loop was found, and every figure in the underlying notes came from search-result summaries rather than full-page reads, so load-bearing numbers should be spot-checked before they are quoted externally.

## Single-currency loops won 2025, but pure income makes commons worthless

The 2025 breakouts share one template. Grow a Garden is buy seeds, plant, wait, harvest, sell, reinvest, with crops growing offline so every login opens on a fresh harvest and a seed shop that restocks on a fixed **5-minute** global clock ([Free Systems](https://freesystems.substack.com/p/the-algorithm-behind-steal-a-brainrot); [SSEGold](https://www.ssegold.com/grow-a-garden-seed-shop-restock-guide)). Steal a Brainrot is buy a unit from a conveyor, place it in your base, earn cash per second scaled by rarity and mutation, and steal from unlocked neighbors ([U7Buy](https://www.u7buy.com/blog/steal-a-brainrot-game-mechanics/)). Naavik describes Grow a Garden as feeling "lifted from a 2010s Facebook game," with idle timers and a gentle first-time experience ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/predicting-the-next-big-hits-on-roblox/)). Even the survival hits that look like crafting games are hybrids: 99 Nights in the Forest ties progression to a numbered Crafting Bench inside a bounded run ([Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/99-nights-in-the-forest-crafting)), Dead Rails spends in-run cash on coal and banks a persistent lobby currency, Bonds, between runs ([Dead Rails Wiki](https://deadrails.fandom.com/wiki/Bonds)), and in Build a Boat for Treasure "Gold is the only currency that matters" with a ladder of about nine block tiers and a Gold Block obtainable only by reaching the treasure ([BABFT guide](https://build-a-boat-for-treasure-wiki.wiki/en/guides/build-a-boat-for-treasure-complete-guide/); [BABFT Wiki](https://build-a-boat-for-treasure.fandom.com/wiki/Gold_Block)). The closest Roblox analogue to Minecraft crafting, Islands, peaked at a community-reported 50–60K concurrent in 2020, lost many players after a 2021 wipe, and declined toward about 8K before discontinuation ([Roblox Islands Fandom thread](https://robloxislands.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000142497)); this is a community post and only directional, but no counter-example of a pure-crafting Roblox collection game at scale surfaced in any note. The audience explains the pattern: of age-checked Roblox users, **35% are under 13 and 38% are 13–17** ([Investing.com transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-roblox-q2-2026-beats-eps-but-shares-sink-on-bookings-93CH-4826338)), roughly 72% of activity is on mobile, and the median session across 500+ games with 1M+ MAU is **7.28 minutes** ([GameAnalytics 2025](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-roblox-report); [GameAnalytics 2026](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-roblox-report)). A recipe web does not fit in a seven-minute, one-thumb session; a number going up does.

That is the case for Model 2, and it is real. But Model 2 as described ("aliens generate currency at rates based on rarity, and a shop sells ship parts, simple and generic") fails three of the team's four criteria. It makes rarity a strict stat ladder, so a Common is dominated the moment anything better exists; Steal a Brainrot only tolerates this because its economy is disposable and spans **$1/s to $1B/s** across tiers with mutations stacking multiplicatively on top ([Steal a Brainrot Wiki](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Rarities); [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/roblox-steal-a-brainrot-all-traits-multipliers/)). It removes any reason to explore, since the optimal play is to idle. And it severs the fantasy the original idea rests on: aliens become batteries, and "generic" ship parts erase the per-planet novelty that distinguishes this concept from the hundred-plus brainrot clones launching weekly ([OpenPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4260068/how-roblox-brainrot-games-are-taking-over)). Model 1 ("no currency, everything crafted, aliens have roles, rarer ones work faster, several aliens may share a job at different speeds") gets the alien fantasy right and is, almost verbatim, Palworld's work-suitability system, which is the most-praised creatures-as-workers design in recent memory: each Pal has job suitabilities at levels 1–10, "a higher level generally means the Pal completes suitable tasks faster," and the satisfying moment is "returning from a long exploration to find your base humming along" ([Nodecraft](https://nodecraft.com/support/games/palworld/general/palworld-work-suitability-level-10-explained); [gametruth](https://www.gametruth.com/guides/palworld-complete-base-building-and-automation-guide/)). But Model 1 strips out the single legible number, the thing every Roblox hit uses to end a session on a visible target, and it leaves the team with nothing clean to sell: developers warn that passes which inflate income "can easily drive away players," so a crafting-only game has to invent a currency anyway to monetize ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/monetization-ideas-for-my-tycoon-game/3048249)).

| Criterion | Model 1 (crafting only, roles) | Model 2 (currency, generic shop) | Recommended hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Finding rare aliens feels rewarding | Yes if roles are gated by biome; weak without a number to show gain | No: rare = bigger number, no reason to leave camp | Yes: rare aliens unlock jobs, speed, and the planet key is only found by exploring |
| (b) Rare > common without commons being pointless | Yes via role specialization and slots | No: strict dominance, needs inflation | Yes: slots, fusion, codex payouts, overlays on any body |
| (c) Cute-to-epic aesthetic | Neutral | Neutral | Neutral (handled by overlay rules, section 4) |
| (d) Player count and retention in 2026 | Risky: Roblox crafting games have weak history, recipe UI on mobile | Proven loop but now a crowded, decaying genre | Proven loop plus a progression spine the 2025 sims lack |
| Monetization surface | Awkward (nothing to sell but speed) | Easy but pushes toward 2x-income passes | Capacity, certainty, cosmetics |

The hybrid works like this. A camp on each planet has four stations at launch (placeholders: Mine, Haul, Weld, Wire). Each alien has one or two jobs at a level, and aliens placed in a station's slots produce Scrap or progress a module at a rate equal to their summed work speed; this directly implements the team's "several aliens doing the same job at different speeds" while borrowing Palworld's finding that "a Pal that does one job at level 8 outproduces one that spreads itself across five jobs, because production throughput is gated by the level of the Pal actually on the task" ([guildorder](https://guildorder.com/games/palworld/guides/work-suitability-base-labor)). Scrap is the one number on the HUD. The ship console sells module blueprints for Scrap (Model 2's shop), but each of the planet's five modules also consumes one planet-unique key material, exactly the two-part gate Astroneer uses, where every planet needs a rising amount of generic power to activate its chambers plus one planet-specific resource (Quartz, Zinc, Explosive Powder, Methane, Hydrogen) to activate its core ([Nerdburglars](https://nerdburglars.net/gameguides/activating-gateway-engines-on-each-planet/)). The bulk cost is ground passively by aliens; the key forces one deliberate expedition per module. When the fifth module completes the ship launches, carrying the roster and the ship to the next planet while the camp is left behind; that preserves sunk cost the way Starbound's persistent, upgradable ship does across tiered planets ([Starbound Fandom](https://starbound.fandom.com/wiki/Spaceship)), and it is the opposite of the Islands wipe that cost that game its casual players. The trade-off is honest: the hybrid has more systems than either pitch, and its central risk is mobile micromanagement. Mitigate by auto-assigning aliens to their best open slot by default (Palworld Pals self-select suitable tasks) and reserving manual placement as an optional optimization, and keep the station count at four until retention data says players want more.

## A 12x ladder beats a billion-x ladder

Top Roblox collection games converge on **6–8 tiers**. Steal a Brainrot runs Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic, Brainrot God, Secret, OG, with adjacent-tier income jumps of roughly 4–15x and Secret spawns reported below 0.001% ([Steal a Brainrot Wiki](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Rarities); [u4gm](https://www.u4gm.com/steal-a-brainrot/blog-what-are-the-chances-of-a-secret-spawning-in-steal-a-brainrot)). Grow a Garden's pet tiers fall from **30–50% at Common to 0.1–0.5% at Divine** ([mygagcalculator](https://mygagcalculator.com/rarest-pets-in-grow-a-garden/)), and Adopt Me's Cracked Egg is 45/33/14.5/6/1.5 across five tiers ([games.gg](https://games.gg/adopt-me/guides/adopt-me-rare-egg-hatching-guide/)). Bubble Gum Simulator Infinity quotes secrets as "1 in 50,000,000" and "1 in 1,000,000,000" because a fraction reads as more prestigious than a percentage ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/roblox-bubble-gum-simulator-infinity-pet-guide/)). Variant overlays are consistently smaller than tier jumps for always-available variants (Steal a Brainrot Gold 1.25x, Diamond 1.5x) and reach tier size only for rare or event-limited ones (Rainbow 10x in Steal a Brainrot; Rainbow x50 and Shocked x100 in Grow a Garden) ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/roblox-steal-a-brainrot-all-traits-multipliers/); [Grow a Garden Wiki](https://growagarden.miraheze.org/wiki/Mutations)). The important lesson is that nobody balances commons into relevance; they use sinks and relative scaling. Pet Simulator 99's Fuse Machine turns 3–100 pets into one stronger pet and its Huge pets are defined as a multiple of the player's strongest regular pet, so they never go stale across 2,720 pets and 790 eggs ([Pet Simulator Wiki: Fuse Machine](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Fuse_Machine_(Pet_Simulator_99)); [Pet Simulator Wiki: Huge Pets](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Huge_Pets_(Pet_Simulator_99)); [BloxGuides](https://bloxguidesgg.com/blog/pet-simulator-99-wiki-launch-2720-pets-790-eggs-may-2026)). Adopt Me needs **four full-grown copies to make a Neon and four Neons (16 pets) to make a Mega Neon** ([Adopt Me Wiki](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Mega_Neons)). Palworld's condenser consumes 48 duplicates for four stars, each star adding +7 work speed ([Palworld Wiki](https://palworld.wiki.gg/wiki/Pal_Essence_Condenser)). Grow a Garden pets each carry one timed ability (Cardinal grows crops 1.55x faster; a Divine pet fires every 16 minutes with a 15% chance to apply a mutation), and duplicates stack, so a specific common can be the only thing that does a specific job ([DroidGamers](https://www.droidgamers.com/guides/grow-a-garden-pet-abilities-guide/); [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/all-divine-pets-grow-garden); [Grow a Garden Fandom](https://growagarden.fandom.com/wiki/Pets)). Grow a Garden also caps runaway with an additive-inside-the-bracket formula so ten x2 mutations give x11 rather than x1024 ([gagdata](https://www.gagdata.com/mutations/stacking)).

The proposed ladder applies those lessons to aliens whose value is work, not cash. Multipliers are cumulative versus Common; encounter shares describe the free world-spawn loop and are a starting point to tune, not tested thresholds.

| Tier | Share of wild spawns | Work speed vs Common | Jobs and level | Camp-wide passive | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common | ~50% | 1x | 1 job, L1 | none | every biome, any time |
| Uncommon | ~27% | 1.6x | 1 job, L2 | none | every biome, any time |
| Rare | ~15% | 3x | 1 job L3, or 2 jobs L2 | none | one specific biome |
| Epic | ~6% | 6x | 2 jobs, L3–4 | none | biome plus condition (night or a weather state) |
| Legendary | ~1.5% | 12x | 2 jobs, L4–5 | +10% to all stations | biome plus condition plus Tier-2 lure; server-announced spawn |
| Cosmic (top tier) | event or quest only; ~0.2% per Meteor Shower spawn, plus one guaranteed per planet via codex quest | max(25x, 2x the player's best alien) | all jobs, L5 | +25% to all stations | 2-hourly server event, or deterministic quest |

This spans about **12x from Common to Legendary** and roughly 25x to Cosmic, which is about 1.4 orders of magnitude on the alien stat, versus nine in Steal a Brainrot. The shallower slope is deliberate: it keeps Scrap readable within three to four orders of magnitude per planet (a Common L1 at a Mine station producing about 1 Scrap/s against a first-planet ship costing on the order of 20,000 Scrap), and it lets the planet change (new biome, new key material, new job) rather than the numbers inflate. Overlays sit on top, multiplicative with tier but limited to one per alien as Grow a Garden limits growth variants: Shiny 1.25x (~5% of spawns), Gold 1.5x (~1.5%), Crystal 2x (~0.3%), Rainbow 5x (event-only). Because overlays roll on any body, a Rainbow Common out-works a plain Rare and is a visible flex, which is the single cheapest way to keep commons desirable late. Commons stay useful through four further mechanisms. Each station has one slot at first and up to three later, so three Common welders (3x) beat an empty slot and beat one Rare (3x) only on tie, while an Epic (6x) clearly beats them; a full slot is always better than an empty one, which is the "several aliens doing the same job" clause made concrete. Four duplicates of any species fuse into +1 job level on one copy (max +2), the Adopt Me pattern scaled down, so nine commons become a L3 Common at roughly 1.7x and the surplus always has a destination. A codex pays Scrap and a camp decoration on the first catch of each species, following Pet Simulator 99's Index Rewards, so a Common caught late is worthless as a unit but worth something as an entry ([BIG Games](https://www.biggames.io/post/pet-simulator-99-update-5)). And one launch job (Haul) is held only by Common and Uncommon species, so the camp cannot run without them.

Value must be expressed in four channels at once, because the research shows rarity is made legible to other players first and the owner second. On the station, the assembly timer visibly shrinks when a better alien is dropped in (Palworld players notice a Work Speed 70 Pal finishing roughly twice as fast as one at 35 ([Game8](https://game8.co/games/Palworld/archives/447263))). On the HUD, a single "Scrap/min" readout rises. On the alien's card, job badges and level pips tell the player what it can do. In the world, the overlay and tier signature (section 4) are visible from across the camp. The top tier also borrows Pet Simulator 99's trick of showing Titanic stats as "????" and making them ridable ([Pet Simulator Wiki: Titanic Pets](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Titanic_Pets_(Pet_Simulator_99))). One design decision the notes could not settle from Roblox evidence is pity: Cookie Run: Kingdom guarantees an Epic every 100 draws ([Cookie Run: Kingdom Wiki](https://cookierunkingdom.fandom.com/wiki/Gacha)), but no Roblox title in the research set was confirmed to have a hard pity; they substitute luck meters, mastery gates and purchasable server luck. The recommendation is a soft, visible pity (a "scanner charge" that raises rare odds the longer a player goes without one, shown numerically) plus the deterministic Cosmic quest per planet, because any numeric luck modifier has to be disclosed under current Roblox policy anyway (section 6).

## Put rarity in the world, then announce it

Exploration-driven games gate rarity by where, when and how you look; hatching games gate it by volume. Fisch ties mutations to specific rods and baits (Ascended from the Masterline Rod at 25%, Royal from the Royal Spear at 35%) and boosts 1% Shiny odds with enchants ([Bloxodes](https://bloxodes.com/wiki/fisch/mutations)). Loomian Legacy's gleamings are wild encounters at **1/4096** that players push to 1/128 by stacking a charm, a boost and rally chains ([Loomian Legacy Wiki: Gleaming](https://loomian-legacy.fandom.com/wiki/Gleaming_Loomian); [Rally Ranch](https://loomian-legacy.fandom.com/wiki/Rally_Ranch)). Pokémon GO keeps the wild shiny rate at 1/512 and concentrates boosted odds into time-boxed events, Community Day at about 1/25, so rare finds cluster into social sessions ([Pokémon DB](https://pokemondb.net/go/shiny); [Pokémon GO Hub](https://pokemongohub.net/post/wiki/community-day-event-shiny-odds/)). The 2025–26 Roblox leaders chose hybrids in which the rare thing is physically present in the shared server: Steal a Brainrot's conveyor spawns are visible to everyone, a Secret spawn triggers a server-wide announcement, and "The Witching Hour" fires every two hours and spawns a Secret somewhere on the map ([Steal a Brainrot Wiki: Secret](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Secret)); Pet Catchers spawns pets in the world and resolves the catch with a thrown cube whose tier sets success odds ([Pet Catchers Wiki](https://petcatchers.fandom.com/wiki/Items)); Grow a Garden's weather events last 2–5 minutes and apply mutations in front of the whole server ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/grow-garden-mutations-guide)). Evidence on which model retains better is thin: no head-to-head retention comparison between exploration and hatching surfaced in any source, so the recommendation rests on fit and on the observation that visible spawns plus contest dominated attention in 2025, not on measured retention.

For a game whose premise is exploring planets, the Fisch and Palworld pattern maps directly and the egg pattern does not, so aliens should spawn physically in biomes, gated by four layers that each add content cheaply. Biome: each planet is handcrafted with three biomes at launch, each with its own Rare. Condition: Epics and Legendaries require night or a weather state, on a planetary cycle of **3 minutes of day and 90 seconds of night** borrowed from 99 Nights in the Forest, whose 4:30 cycle gives a 15-minute session three natural checkpoints ([99 Nights Miraheze](https://99nightsintheforestroblox.miraheze.org/wiki/Days)), with weather rolling randomly for 2–5 minutes as in Grow a Garden. Tool: catching is a short thrown-lure interaction, and lures are crafted from Scrap in three tiers that raise success odds, so a player who spots a Legendary with a Tier-1 lure has a reason to come back rather than a dead roll. Appointment: a Meteor Shower fires on a fixed two-hour server clock, is announced to the whole server with a world-space beacon, and is the only place Cosmics spawn, converting the rarest find from a private slot machine into a shared calendar event, which the notes identify as the highest-retention form of rarity reveal because it recurs. The key material for each ship module lives in a specific biome under a specific condition, so the Astroneer-style expedition doubles as the moment players meet that biome's aliens.

Two deterministic layers protect players who never roll well. The codex pays on first catch, as above, and completing a planet's set of Rares reveals a Legendary nest, a guaranteed encounter earned through play, which is the structure of Pokémon GO's Masterwork Research, a 10-step non-expiring quest ending in a guaranteed shiny ([Serebii](https://www.serebii.net/pokemongo/masterworkresearch/sparkleandshine.shtml)). Neko Atsume's mementos, awarded only after several visits and themed to each cat, show a second collection layer can be built on the same roster for free ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/neko-atsume-how-to-get-all-mementos/)); here, each species has one camp decoration unlocked by catching it and one by fusing it, so the codex has two columns without a single extra creature. A visible luck meter matters too: Bubble Gum Simulator Infinity exposes the player's Lucky boost, shiny chance and mythic chance in a Debug toggle, and Steal a Brainrot's server luck is a shared state players actively seek out ([Slyther Games](https://www.slythergames.com/2025/05/06/best-bubble-gum-simulator-infinity-tips-and-tricks/); [FandomWire](https://fandomwire.com/all-types-of-luck-in-roblox-steal-a-brainrot/)). Showing the number turns a dry streak into something the player feels they are working. The trade-off against egg-based acquisition is revenue: eggs monetize volume (Grow a Garden sells eggs at 19–129 Robux as an alternative to grind currency ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/grow-a-garden-all-eggs-list-hatch-times-probabilities/))), while exploration monetizes less directly. Given the regulatory direction in section 6, that is a trade worth making.

## Same body, five escalating overlays

Cuteness is a small, repeatable recipe. Kirby's spherical body, short plump limbs, large eyes and small mouth fit the Kindchenschema, the infantile proportions that trigger caregiving responses, and he was given a circle so anyone could draw him ([WiKirby](https://wikirby.com/wiki/Kirby's_characteristics); [Retroware](https://articles.retroware.com/2021/09/16/kirbys-simple-character-design-defines-his-games/)). Among Us's crewmate was kept minimal so "nearly anyone" can draw it and project onto it ([Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/among-us-concept-art-crewmate-design/)). Big eyes read as innocent and sweet ([Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/the-elements-of-cute-character-design--vector-3533)), round silhouettes read as friendly, and the stylized low-poly house style of Adopt Me and Brookhaven exists because bold colors and clean forms are both appealing and cheap to render ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/why-the-low-poly-graphics-style-is-so-common/511551)). "Epic" is produced not by abandoning that recipe but by layering overlays on the same base body. Adopt Me's Neons glow on species-specific parts (ears, paws and tail tip on a dog; wings and spine on a dragon) and Mega Neons glow in the same places but cycle through the rainbow ([Adopt Me Wiki: Neon Pets](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Neon_Pets)). Steal a Brainrot's mutations recolor the whole model (Gold "completely golden," Diamond "completely blue," Galaxy purple) so income tier is readable at a glance across a base ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/all-mutations-effects-steal-brainrot)). Pet Simulator 99 stacks three escalators: size class (regular, Huge, Titanic), rarity, and variant, with Titanics oversized, ridable and showing "????" stats ([Pet Simulator Wiki: Titanic Pets](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/wiki/Titanic_Pets_(Pet_Simulator_99))). Grow a Garden's Rainbow mutation "cycles continuously through every color of the spectrum and emits yellow particles and displays a rainbow arc halo" ([Grow a Garden Wiki](https://growagarden.miraheze.org/wiki/Mutations)). Pokémon's three-stage rule states the principle: a childlike first stage and an adult final stage "that expands on design ideas seen in the child stage," and Ken Sugimori's finishing move is to add something uncool to a design that is too cool so it stays memorable ([Bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Evolution); [ResetEra compilation](https://www.resetera.com/threads/how-game-freak-designs-pok%C3%A9mon.105057/)).

| Tier | Silhouette and size | Material and color | Glow | Particles and extras | Sound | Discovery presentation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common | round base body, 100% scale | one flat saturated color | none | none | short squeak | silent catch, card slides in |
| Uncommon | same body, one small prop or accent | two-tone | none | none | squeak with a second note | card with tier color |
| Rare | same body, 110% | base color plus metallic trim | localized emissive on species-specific parts (Neon pattern) | none | three-note jingle | card plus local burst and jingle |
| Epic | same body, 120%, one silhouette add (horns, fins, antenna) | full-body material swap (gold, crystal) | stronger localized glow | one particle emitter | signature voice line | rarity-colored flash, jingle, name text |
| Legendary | same body, 125%, two silhouette adds | full-body material plus animated texture | rainbow-cycling glow | halo and ground ring | signature voice line, longer | server-wide banner, beacon at spawn, unique sound |
| Cosmic | oversized (150%+), ridable | shifting cosmic material | full-body rainbow cycle | trail, aura, "????" stats | unique theme stinger | server-wide banner with location, beacon, camera pull |

Four rules govern the ladder. First, one mesh family per species: tier and overlay are shader, scale and attachment changes on the same body, which is how Grow a Garden fields 158+ crop mutations and 47 pet mutations on a fixed roster, and which is the only way a two-person team ships a 15-species roster with six tiers and four overlays ([Grow a Garden Wiki](https://growagarden.miraheze.org/wiki/Mutations); [gagdata](https://www.gagdata.com/pets/mutations)). Second, every tier must be legible in greyscale and at thumbnail scale, because on small screens "below roughly 5mm on-screen height, texture detail is noise, and only silhouette and value survive" ([AppVertices](https://appvertices.io/building-iconic-game-characters-for-mobile-apps/)); that is why the table advances silhouette and size with tier rather than relying on color, and why each color-coded tier needs a secondary signal given that about 1 in 12 men are colorblind ([Game-Ace](https://game-ace.com/blog/color-psychology-of-game-characters-the-good-the-bad-the-ugly/)). Third, keep one goofy element at every tier (the Sugimori rule): a Legendary should still have the big eyes and a silly idle, both because that preserves the family and because it is what makes the design meme-able. Fourth, design for the clip. Italian brainrot's formula was hybrid creatures (animal plus object), rhyming pseudo-Italian names and a signature synthetic voice line, and Steal a Brainrot has a catalog of per-creature spawn sounds that became standalone soundboard memes ([Wikipedia: Italian brainrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_brainrot); [Steal a Brainrot Wiki: Sound Effects](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Sound_Effects/Spawns)). Gen Alpha content is "built on repetition, absurdity and rhythm" with a gag every few seconds ([Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/why-brain-rot-makes-sense-gen-alpha/1945896); [ScreenWise](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/understanding-gen-alpha-humor-and-why-everything-is-brainrot)). An original roster should take the properties rather than the characters: each alien is an alien-plus-object or alien-plus-tool hybrid describable in three words (which also telegraphs its job), has a name with reduplication or rhythm, one voice line, and one looping idle animation tied to its job. The discovery ritual follows the standard egg kit beats, shake, crack, reveal with rarity-colored text ([BuiltByBit](https://builtbybit.com/resources/uxr-pet-system.39104/)), but at three escalating presentation levels, with Legendary and Cosmic breaking out of the personal UI into the server, as Steal an Egg does by broadcasting rare egg name and location ([Eldorado](https://www.eldorado.gg/blog/steal-an-egg/steal-an-egg-eggs-list/)). Palworld's reception offers the design target in one phrase: Pals praised as "as cute as they are surprisingly efficient," with Caprity's floppy ears and goat horns attached to a creature that is "really useful to have on your base" ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/palworld-best-pal-designs/)).

## Eight-player planets with personal camps, co-op by Party

The two most-played games in Roblox history share a server shape: tiny public servers, one persistent plot or base per player, every collection visible and touchable. Grow a Garden servers hold 6 plots and are capped at 5 players ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/grow-a-garden-player-count-server-limit-and-record-highs/)); Steal a Brainrot spawns each player at one of eight bases whose first floor has 10 slots, locked for 30 seconds on join, with friends admittable through the laser gate ([Roblox Wiki](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/BRAZILIAN_SPYDER/Steal_a_Brainrot); [Steal a Brainrot Wiki: Base](https://stealabrainrot.fandom.com/wiki/Base)). Roblox's own design guidance says "single-player games often find it harder to build and retain an audience on Roblox," that players "can act as evergreen sources of content for each other," and that private servers should not be priced so high that friend groups cannot play together ([Roblox Creator Docs: Design for Roblox](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/design-for-roblox)). Its retention doc lists trading, parties, guilds and leaderboards as Day-30 levers ([Roblox Creator Docs: Retention](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/retention)). The round-based co-op hits use a different shape, lobby plus a reserved server per squad: Dead Rails parties of 1–10 with a Party Code, 99 Nights squads of up to about five with difficulty scaling to team size ([Dead Rails Wiki](https://deadrails.fandom.com/wiki/Lobby); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/NinetyNineNightsInTheForest)). The team's instinct, each server is one planet, each player has a separate ship and camp, with a party or invite co-op layer, is therefore the proven shape, and the specifics follow from Roblox's platform constraints.

The expedition planet should be the start place, with **MaxPlayers 8 and PreferredPlayers 6**, eight fixed camp slots, and one or two social slots reserved so a friend can always join (Roblox allows up to 20% of max players, rounded down, as social slots, and only one slot below five players) ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/experience-join-improvements-server-size-join-queues-and-social-slots-reservations/2294621); [Roblox Creator Docs: Players](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Players)). Eight keeps every neighbor legible, matches Steal a Brainrot, stays under the 100-player cap for voice chat, which Roblox says lifts engagement and spending ([Roblox Creator Docs: Voice chat](https://create.roblox.com/docs/chat/voice-chat)), and keeps the creature count manageable. A player's camp state (slot contents, module progress, inventory) is restored from their DataStore key on join, so leaving mid-planet costs nothing; this is how Grow a Garden and Steal a Brainrot persist bases. Co-op uses the platform Party (up to six friends, auto-placed in the same server) and the Party API: `Player.PartyId` identifies the party, `SocialService:GetPlayersByPartyId` gathers members on the current server, and `TeleportAsync` with `ShouldReserveServer = true` moves them to a private planet place; `GetPartyAsync` later exposes each member's `ReservedServerAccessCode` so a late joiner can follow, which matters because the friends-list Join button cannot enter reserved servers ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2024/12/join-the-party-on-roblox); [Roblox Creator Docs: SocialService](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/SocialService); [Roblox Creator Docs: Teleport](https://create.roblox.com/docs/projects/teleport)). The public planet should be "Fully open" so friends-list joins land next to the friend; the private planet should be a non-start place set to "Secure within universe only." Progress stays per player in DataStores, which are consistent across every place in the universe; shared planet state exists only for the session, which is the model every hit uses, since shared ownership of a base has no Roblox precedent in the research.

The technical budget is tight but known. Each player's save should be one key under a static pattern like `User_{UserId}`, written with `UpdateAsync` and session locking, because without locking "one server saves out-of-date information," which produces data loss and item-duplication loopholes ([Roblox Creator Docs: Player data sample](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/player-data-purchasing); [Best practices](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/best-practices)). The per-key limit is **4 MB** and total storage is 500 MB plus 1 MB per lifetime user ([Roblox Creator Docs: limits](https://create.roblox.com/docs/cloud-services/data-stores/error-codes-and-limits)); a slot-based camp (slot index, alien id, level, overlay) fits in well under 100 KB, whereas free-form building is where keys balloon, which is why Lumber Tycoon 2 shows a "Data Size" on every save slot ([Lumber Tycoon 2 Wiki](https://lumber-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Load)). Creatures are the first thing that will break: `Humanoid` "comes with a significant computation cost," so aliens should be lightweight server records (position, target, state as attributes on a tagged part) rendered and animated on each client with `AnimationController`, spawned only near players, with `StreamingEnabled` on, following Roblox's performance guidance and community pet-system practice ([Roblox Creator Docs: Improve performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve); [DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/syncing-equipped-pets-for-new-players-performance-considerations/2857024)). Eight camps with 10–30 aliens each is 80–240 creatures per server; server-side Humanoids at that count are not viable. Cross-server notifications ("a friend landed on your planet") fit MessagingService's 1 kB messages and 600 + 240 per-player sends per minute, but state sync does not ([Roblox Creator Docs: MessagingService](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/MessagingService)).

The home planet the team is considering should be the persistent, visible record of progress that Bloxburg's house is ("a visual record of progress visible to every visitor on your plot"), monetized by capacity rather than power, as Bloxburg's 30x30 to 50x50 plot pass, Adopt Me's 4,000 to 8,000 furniture cap and Restaurant Tycoon 2's 7 to 12 worker pass all do ([welcometobloxburg.wiki](https://welcometobloxburg.wiki/build/); [Bloxburg Fandom](https://welcome-to-bloxburg.fandom.com/wiki/House); [Adopt Me Fandom](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Houses); [Restaurant Tycoon 2 Fandom](https://restaurant-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Workers)). It also solves the concept's structural tension: an idle game wants a base you never leave, and this game's whole arc is leaving. The ship and roster carry forward, the expedition camp is disposable, and the home planet is where offline accrual, display slots and decorations live, visitable by friends through Adopt Me-style lock levels (owner, friends, anyone) and loadable from the owner's DataStore key even when they are offline, which is what Bloxburg's neighbor visits already do ([Adopt Me Wiki](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Houses); [Playbite](https://www.playbite.com/how-to-go-to-someones-house-in-bloxburg/)). For a two-person team the home planet must be modular (pedestals and prefab rooms unlocked by Scrap and codex progress), not free-build, for both data-size and scope reasons, and it belongs in the first post-launch month rather than the MVP, with the camp slot on the shared planet carrying the display role until then.

Two social decisions need to be made against thin evidence. Stealing made Steal a Brainrot: analysts call it "the entire foundation" of the economy, and the game's viral clips were "reactions of children crying after having their Brainrots stolen" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@emtertoszef/steal-a-brainrot-phenomenon-6af7b49081e0)); Grow a Garden runs a softer dial, 37 Robux to steal a crop with the whole server notified ([Droid Gamers](https://www.droidgamers.com/guides/grow-a-garden-stealing/)). For a kids-skewed game in 2026, with state attorneys general suing Roblox over child safety and Grow a Garden's pay-to-steal drawing named criticism ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/louisiana-attorney-general-sues-roblox); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)), the recommendation is visit-and-borrow (a friend lends an alien to your station for a timed window, keeps ownership, and both get a bonus), with an opt-in raid mode on public planets added later behind Steal a Brainrot's guardrails (lock timers, thief debuffs, recoverable aliens). No Roblox game with a borrow mechanic was found, so this is an inference from visiting and stealing precedents, not a proven pattern. Trading should not ship at launch: Adopt Me needed a Trade License quiz, 30-day history and in-game reporting to contain scams, Pet Simulator 99's open booths still produce last-second swap and "extra zero" scams, and Roblox itself surfaces trading as a mid-game feature ([Adopt Me](https://www.playadopt.me/news/trade-changes-and-scam-prevention-update); [Pet Simulator Wiki forum](https://pet-simulator.fandom.com/f/p/4400000000000333139); [Roblox Creator Docs: Onboarding](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding-techniques)). Finally, Roblox has required facial age checks for chat since January 2026 and restricts chat to similar age groups, so an under-9 and a 16-year-old on the same planet cannot talk ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/11/roblox-requires-age-checks-limits-minor-and-adult-chat)); visiting, borrowing, emotes, gifts, visible camps and notifications have to carry the social load, and squad formation should call `TextChatService:GetChatGroupsAsync` so co-op groups can actually talk ([Roblox Creator Docs: TextChatService](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/TextChatService)).

## Sell certainty and capacity; keep the dice free

Roblox's paid random items policy now covers anything random bought with Robux or Robux-derived currency, including capsule items, enhancement items, combination items and probability modifiers, and requires odds to be "accurately and numerically disclosed to the eligible user before the moment of purchase," per item, summing to exactly 100%, with luck boosts and pity systems numerically disclosed and displayed odds updating dynamically while they are active, and with no outcome in which the user simply loses their payment ([Roblox Creator Docs: Paid random items](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items)). The tightening is reported in a "Clarifying Requirements for Paid Random Items" announcement dated May 26, 2026, a date resting on a single search-result statement, and was driven by South Korea's odds-disclosure law and applied globally ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/clarifying-requirements-for-paid-random-items/4654622); [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319148/20260626/koreas-loot-box-rules-push-roblox-disclose-item-odds-worldwide.htm)). `PolicyService.ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted` must gate every such purchase, and the restriction already applies to **UK users under 18 (since August 13, 2024), all Australian users (since September 20, 2024) and Brazilian minors** ([Roblox Creator Docs: PolicyService](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/PolicyService); [DevForum: UK](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/update-on-paid-random-items-restriction-for-uk-users-under-18/3072183); [DevForum: Australia](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/update-on-paid-random-items-restriction-for-australian-users/3153602); [Bloxy News](https://x.com/Bloxy_News/status/2034153291899408514)). The direction of travel is one way. The European Commission's KIDS Act proposal of September 17, 2026 would prohibit loot boxes for minors, ban rewarding regular log-ins, and require virtual currency to show its value in official currency, pending Parliament and Council approval ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/09/eu-kids-act-online-child-safety-proposal)); the FTC's January 2025 Genshin Impact settlement ($20M) bars loot box sales to under-16s without parental consent ([Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/lootbox-regulation-2026-game-studios/)); and since June 2026 every Roblox account under 16 sits in a Kids or Select tier with parent-set monthly spend limits ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/06/age-based-roblox-kids-and-select-accounts-now-globally-available); [EndGameTools](https://endgametools.com/en/blog/roblox-kids-select-parental-controls)). The research on children is blunt: kids find virtual-currency conversions "scary," experience harm as feeling deceived, and researchers concluded "monetized random reward mechanics and virtual currencies are not appropriate in children's games" ([University of Sydney](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2025/03/24/literally-just-child-gambling-study-urges-swift-regulation-of-roblox.html)); a CHI 2025 study of Roblox creators found the platform's ranking "weight[s] revenue," pushing gambling-like games to child audiences ([ACM CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713170)).

The commercially tested stack is still visible in the price points. A 2x multiplier pass is reported as "the single highest-converting game pass for most games" at 50–300 Robux, sub-50 Robux items convert 5–15% of viewers and 75–200 Robux items 2–8%, and games with five or more passes earn 2.3x more; these figures come from a developer-education site without disclosed methodology ([Generalist Programmer](https://generalistprogrammer.com/tutorials/roblox-game-pass-pricing-guide)). Grow a Garden dual-prices eggs in Sheckles or 19–129 Robux and sells pet equip slots at 249, 399, 799 and 1,699 Robux ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/grow-a-garden-all-eggs-list-hatch-times-probabilities/); [U4GM](https://www.u4gm.com/grow-a-garden/blog-grow-a-garden-pet-egg-equip-slot-upgrades-guide)); it reportedly earned **$12 million in May 2025** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)). Steal a Brainrot sells server-wide luck at 249, 999 and 2,999 Robux for 15 minutes each, benefiting every player on the server ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/all-luck-variants-steal-a-brainrot)). Adopt Me prices all eggs in earned Bucks with published odds and makes its Neon and Mega Neon tiers a deterministic fusion grind ([Adopt Me Wiki: Eggs](https://adoptme.fandom.com/wiki/Eggs)). The platform pays creators $0.0038 per Robux since September 5, 2025, with a US 18+ rate of about $0.0054 from June 8, 2026 limited to R15 experiences ([MathGyro](https://mathgyro.com/robux-devex-rates/); [DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introducing-the-us-18-devex-rate-earn-42-more-on-spend-from-18-us-players/4607091)), and Creator Rewards pays 5 Robux per Active Spender who plays 10+ minutes a day, but only after 100+ DAU sustained for 60 days ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introducing-creator-rewards-earn-more-by-growing-the-community/3777628); [Rolearn](https://rolearn.dev/insights/roblox-creator-rewards-payout-guide/)). Expectations should be set by Roblox's own Q2 2026 letter, which blamed a bookings miss on engagement shifting from "high monetizing, 2025-vintage viral games" to games "with lower hourly monetization," with the decline sharpest among younger US and Canadian cohorts ([Roblox Q2 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001315098/000162828026051059/ex991-robloxq22026earnin.htm)).

|---|---|---|---|---|

The design rule that follows is to keep randomness in the free loop and sell certainty, capacity and cosmetics. World spawns and Scrap-priced incubators with published odds are not paid random items under the policy's definition as excerpted (scope is Robux or Robux-derived currency; whether earned-only currency is formally out of scope is an inference, not confirmed text), so the whole rarity economy runs in restricted regions without modification. The one randomness-adjacent SKU, server-wide luck, is the most socially efficient consumable in the research, since one buyer benefits eight players, but it is a probability modifier and must show its numeric effect, update displayed odds live and disappear for restricted players, with a deterministic alternative (a direct-buy Rare) offered instead. Show the real-currency equivalent next to every Robux price and never introduce a second premium currency, because currency opacity is the single most-cited child harm and a named KIDS Act target. Industry guidance for kids-focused Roblox experiences is consistent: "no pressure loops, no aggressive scarcity, no dark patterns, and price points a parent can approve without a second thought," with cosmetic passes working and repeated small consumables not ([Metamate KR](https://metamatekr.com/en/blog/roblox-all-ages-policy-checklist)). Regional pricing should be on from day one; Roblox reports 4–10% higher overall spend and 43–52% more pass purchases in discounted regions ([DevForum: Managed Pricing](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/managed-pricing-one-system-for-better-pricing-and-earnings-growth/4684738)). The explicit trade-off is revenue: this stack forgoes paid eggs, the engine of Grow a Garden's 2025 numbers, in exchange for a game that does not need re-architecting when the EU acts and that stays monetizable in the UK, Australia and Brazil today. Because Roblox's August 2026 discovery update rewards "retention and monetization together" ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/boost-your-discovery-by-building-games-people-want-to-play/4779042)), a capacity-and-cosmetics stack that converts steadily is also better for ranking than a spiky one.

## Ship one planet and fifteen aliens, then earn week two

The window is short and the bar has moved. Grow a Garden was built "in only three days" by a 16-year-old and reached 5M CCU in two months; 99 Nights came from a three-person team in New Zealand; Steal an Egg went from a July 25, 2026 launch to 14.27M CCU in eight weeks ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/30/roblox-video-game-grow-a-garden-viral-summer-hit/); [Roblox DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/creator-spotlight-the-story-behind-99-nights-in-the-forest/4036940); [RoVitals](https://rovitals.com/game/107778070777162)). Those peaks were event-driven and decayed hard: both 2025 giants fell from 1–2M average CCU to roughly **76K–99K** by September 2026 ([BloxQuiz](https://www.bloxquiz.gg/stats/grow-a-garden/history); [RoMonitor Stats](https://romonitorstats.com/experience/109983668079237/)), and Naavik calls 2025 "a spike, not a slope" ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/robloxs-expensive-year-of-good-intentions/)). Roblox rewrote Recommended For You in June 2026 to score 28 days of return behavior, play-through rate, first-play bounce, play days and playtime, explicitly because the old 7-day proxy let "exciting thumbnails but shallow gameplay" displace better games ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/06/optimizing-discovery-great-games-reach-millions-players-roblox)). About 2,000 experiences launch daily and 1 in 50,000 reaches the top 10 by spend ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-grow-a-gardens-89-million-ccu)). The benchmarks to beat are median D1 10.3%, D7 1.6% and D30 0.5%, with the top 1% at D1 22.2%, D7 9.1% and D30 4.7%, and GameAnalytics notes that high D30 "almost always belongs to games with big IPs or deep long-term progression" ([GameAnalytics 2026](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-roblox-report)). That last line is the original idea's advantage: a per-planet chapter structure is deep progression that a plot of crops is not, and the MAYA logic behind Dead Rails' rise, a familiar loop in a fresh setting ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/dead-rails-and-the-hit-makers-formula)), applies to "a Grow-and-Steal-style collection loop where the point is to leave."

The first ten minutes decide D1. Tycoon developers front-load, with "about 200 buttons, with 50 of which unlocked within the first 10–20 minutes" ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-this-a-good-tycoon-progress-rate/2225354)). The team's tutorial idea (easy aliens help build the first ship section) should use Restaurant Tycoon 2's trainee pattern, where chefs "need to watch the player cook 5 dishes until they can start cooking by themselves" ([Restaurant Tycoon 2 Fandom](https://restaurant-tycoon-2.fandom.com/wiki/Workers)): the player hauls three pieces of wreckage by hand, a Common Hauler appears and takes over; the player welds one panel by hand, a Common Welder appears and takes over; the first module completes inside eight minutes with five to ten small unlocks along the way, and the session ends on a visible timer (next Meteor Shower, a module at 80%) so the return is pre-motivated. Offline accrual should fill a storage cap in about eight hours, inside the 2–24 hour range idle designers use so a return is always rewarded but never complete ([Game-Ace](https://game-ace.com/blog/idle-game-development/)). Every spawn-and-reveal should be filmable in ten seconds, because Home now autoplays gameplay video and Roblox urges creators to upload it ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-we-are-improving-home-this-year/4502571)), and sponsored ads should wait until retention is strong, since a developer bug report shows sponsored traffic drags down the very engagement signals discovery uses ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/sponsored-ads-traffic-degrades-the-engagement-metrics-used-for-discovery-causing-paid-acquisition-to-harm-organic-ranking/4838351)).

| Priority | Scope | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| P0, launch | One handcrafted planet with 3 biomes; 12–15 species across 4 jobs and tiers Common–Legendary (Cosmic via the 2-hour event only); 5 ship modules each with a key material; one currency; station slots; trainee tutorial; 3:00/1:30 day-night and 2–5 minute weather; Meteor Shower on a 2-hour server clock; codex with first-catch payouts; offline accrual with 8-hour cap; 8-player public servers with camp restore; client-rendered aliens; 3–4 passes plus server luck with PolicyService gating; funnel analytics on tutorial play-through and first-play bounce | Everything D1 and the first week depend on; nothing here requires a second place or cross-server systems |
| P1, weeks 2–6 | Overlays (Shiny, Gold, Crystal); 4-duplicate fusion; home planet as a modular, friend-visitable place loaded from DataStore; Party co-op reserved-server planet; second planet with one new job and key material; weekly scheduled in-game event creators can stream; share links and friend-referral rewards | D7–D28 depth that the 28-day algorithm scores; gives the first content update cadence 99 Nights used |
| P2, after traction | Planets 3–5 with one new constraint each; Cosmic quest line; visit-and-borrow, then opt-in raid; gated trading (license quiz, 30-day log, two-step confirm, item caps); guilds with weekly rewards; seasonal overlays with announced end dates | Each adds moderation or economy burden a two-person team should not carry before 100+ DAU is sustained |

Keep the planet count small and handcrafted: Astroneer's seven bodies and Outer Wilds' one dense system are consistently praised over No Man's Sky's procedural sameness, and each planet should change a constraint, not a skin ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/753640/discussions/0/2518023667589043781/); [Nerdburglars](https://nerdburglars.net/gameguides/activating-gateway-engines-on-each-planet/)). Use Theme Park Tycoon 2's 2024 choose-one-reward-per-level pattern for which module to unlock next, which adds agency without branching ([TPT2 Fandom](https://tpt2.fandom.com/wiki/Leveling)). Balance Scrap with Roblox's economy tooling, Configs for live price changes without restarts and Experiments for A/B tests ([Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/balance-virtual-economies)). Weekly updates mattered for 99 Nights but loop depth held its chart position when the cadence paused ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/survival-crafting/with-a-peak-player-count-of-14-2-million-99-nights-in-the-forest-has-an-audience-other-multiplayer-games-would-kill-for-to-find-these-behemoth-playerbases-you-need-to-be-on-a-platform-like-roblox/)), so a two-person team should commit to one scheduled event a week and one content drop every two, rather than a weekly feature. The launch targets are D1 above 20% and D7 above 8%, a median session above 10 minutes, and tutorial play-through above 60%; if D1 is under 15% after the first week, the problem is the first eight minutes, not the roster.

## Conclusion

The debate between "crafting like Minecraft" and "currency and a shop" is a false choice, and the research says why: the thing that made 2025's hits enormous (one number, five-minute timers, visible neighbors) is also what made them disposable, and the thing that makes Palworld's workers memorable (specialized jobs you can watch) is what Roblox's idle sims never had. This concept can hold both because its premise supplies a progression spine, planets as chapters, that the genre lacks and that Roblox's 2026 algorithm now explicitly pays for. The alien has to be three things at once, a worker whose speed you can see, a find whose location you had to learn, and a body whose overlays everyone on the server can read, and the hybrid is the only structure in which all three are true simultaneously.

The least-certain parts of this report are the ones the team will most want to copy: the exact tier shares, the 12x span, the eight-hour cap and the price points are starting values, not tested thresholds, and every statistic came through search summaries rather than full pages. What is firmly supported is directional: small shared servers with personal bases, randomness kept out of Robux, rarity expressed socially before numerically, and an MVP that is one planet done well. Two people cannot out-ship the clone wave, but they can out-last it by building the game the algorithm, the regulators and the parents are all now pointing at.
